Rationale 
The upgrade in the long-term rating of NLC Tamilnadu Power Limited (NTPL) reflects a significant reduction in its debt (including 
bill discounting) to Rs. 822.7 crore as on September 30, 2025, from Rs. 2502.1 crore as on March 31, 2025 and Rs. 4211.3 crore 
as on March 31, 2024. This improvement was driven by the receipt of cumulative arrears of ~Rs. 1,861 crore between October 
2024 and June 2025, following favourable tariff orders from the Central Electricity Regulatory Commission (CERC) for the 2014–
19 and 2019–24 control periods. The arrears were primarily used to repay project term loans and reduce the working capital 
borrowings. The outstanding debt now primarily comprises the term loan taken for the flue gas desulphurisation (FGD) capex
and working capital borrowings, which are significantly lower than the historical debt levels, supporting comfortable debt 
coverage indicators. The total debt/OPBDIT improved to 0.8 times in H1 FY2026 from 8.6 times in FY2024.
The rating reaffirmation also reflects the benefits derived by NLC Tamilnadu Power Limited (NTPL) from its parent, NLC India 
Limited {NLCIL; rated [ICRA]AAA (Stable)}, a Navratna entity, with an established track record of more than 50 years in lignite 
mining and power generation. NLCIL holds an 89% stake in NTPL. ICRA takes note of the shared management, operational and 
financial linkages and the track record of financial support provided by the parent. The ratings also positively factor in the longterm power purchase agreements (PPAs) for the company’s entire thermal power capacity of 1,000 MW under the cost-plus 
tariff structure, based on the CERC’s tariff regulations, which limit the demand and tariff risks for NTPL. 
ICRA also considers the long-term fuel supply agreements (FSAs) that ensure the availability of domestic coal for the project. 
The company also continues to import coal with high gross calorific value (GCV) for blending with domestic coal to meet the 
technical requirements of the plant. On the operational side, the plant availability has improved to ~83% in FY2025 and ~89% 
in H1 FY2026 from ~69% in FY2024 with the completion of the planned overhaul and improved imported coal availability.
The ratings are, however, constrained by NTPL’s exposure to financially weak discoms such as Tamil Nadu Generation and 
Distribution Corporation Ltd (TANGEDCO) and the discoms in Telangana, Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka and Kerala, evident from 
the payment delays in the past. Further, the ratings consider the operating inefficiencies because of the higher-than-normative 
station heat rate (SHR) and auxiliary consumption, leading to under-recovery of variable charges. The relatively high landed 
cost of fuel has also impacted NTPL’s position in the merit order list of the offtaking discoms. In addition, the company has 
witnessed periods when the actual plant availability has been less than the normative level of 85%, resulting in under-recovery 
of fixed charges as well. The company’s ability to maintain the operating parameters in line with normative levels would be a 
key monitorable from a credit perspective. Further, ICRA notes that NTPL’s business profile is characterised by its reliance on 
its single asset – the 1,000-MW thermal power plant at Tuticorin – which remains its sole revenue driver.

The ratings also factor in the company’s moderate return indicators because of disallowed project cost. The approved project
capital cost of Rs. 6,004 crore is lower than the actual incurred project capital cost of ~Rs. 7,212 crore (excluding Rs. 81 crore 
LD charges recovered from contractor). NTPL has filed an appeal with APTEL for the disallowed portion (~Rs. 897 crore, 
including towards IDC and incidental expense during construction) to be considered in the tariff. The disallowance of capital
cost has moderated the return indicators. The outcome of the quantum of the incremental project cost approved by APTEL 
has the potential to increase the regulated asset base, which can uplift the regulated returns from the prevailing levels.
The Stable outlook reflects ICRA’s opinion that NTPL shall benefit from the cost-plus based PPA, supply arrangement for coal 
sourcing and the stable operating efficiencies, which are expected to generate a stable and predictable earnings stream,
thereby supporting its credit profile, going forward.
Key rating drivers and their description 
Credit strengths 
Comfortable debt coverage metrics – NTPL has received cumulative arrears of ~Rs. 1,861 crore between October 2024 and 
June 2025, following favourable tariff orders from the CERC for the 2014–19 and 2019–24 control periods. The arrears were 
primarily utilised to repay project term loans and reduce the working capital borrowings. This has significantly reduced the 
debt to Rs. 822.7 crore as on September 30, 2025, from Rs. 2502.1 crore as on March 31, 2025 and Rs. 4211.3 crore as on 
March 31, 2024. Consequently, the outstanding debt now primarily comprises the FGD capex-related term loan and working 
capital borrowings, which are significantly lower than the historical debt levels, supporting comfortable debt coverage 
indicators.
Strong parentage of NLCIL – NTPL benefits from its strong parentage with NLCIL holding an 89% stake in the company. NLCIL 
is a Navratna PSU with sovereign ownership and is strategically important to the Government of India (GoI) in the lignite mining 
and power sectors. NLCIL had extended financial support to NTPL in the past. The parent also extends other operational 
support along with providing coal supply from its captive Talabira mine in Odisha. NLCIL and NTPL have common directors, and 
other senior officials of NLCIL are part of the board of directors/management team of NTPL. 
Long-term PPAs with cost-plus tariff limit demand and tariff risks – NTPL has signed long-term PPAs for its entire capacity 
with the distribution utilities of the five southern states and the Union Territory of Puducherry under the cost-plus tariff 
regulations of the CERC. This mitigates the offtake risks for the company. The two-part tariff structure enables the company 
to recover fixed charges, including the fixed return on equity based on plant availability and pass-through of fuel costs, subject 
to meeting the normative operating efficiency parameters, supporting the profitability and debt coverage metrics.
Fuel supply arrangements in place – The company’s total annual coal requirement is estimated at around 4 MMTPA. It had 
earlier signed long-term fuel supply agreements (FSA) with Mahanadi Coalfields Ltd (MCL) and Eastern Coalfield Limited (ECL), 
the wholly-owned subsidiaries of CIL, to procure 3 million metric tonnes per annum (MMTPA) and 1.3 MMTPA of coal, 
respectively. At present, NLCIL has signed an MoU with NLC’s Talabira coal mine for 3.0 MMTPA. It also imports 1.0 MMTPA 
of high-GCV coal for blending and has diversified procurement by splitting tenders between L1 and L2 bidders (70:30) to 
mitigate the supply risks. While imported coal is costlier than Talabira coal, the pass-through mechanism for fuel cost, subject 
to the normative operating parameters, largely offsets the price risk.
Credit challenges 
Counterparty credit risks from exposure to state distribution utilities with modest financial profiles – The company’s 
counterparty credits risks remain high owing to the exposure to discoms with modest financial profiles, including TANGEDCO 
and the Telangana and Andhra Pradesh discoms. ICRA notes that there had been a build-up of receivables over the last few 
years (till FY2021) due to delayed payments by the state distribution utilities and the pandemic’s impact on their finances. 
Nonetheless, the company’s receivable position has significantly improved with the realisation of dues under the earlier 
Atmanirbhar package, LPS scheme and the use of the bill discounting mechanism.

Post the receipt of arrears, the receivables (excluding unbilled revenues) reduced to ~Rs. 387 as on September 30, 2025, from 
~Rs. 1,079 crore as on March 31, 2025. Further, NTPL has discontinued the bill discounting arrangement, in line with the policy 
of its parent, NLCIL, reducing the exposure to bill discounting to Rs. 280.7 crore as on September 30, 2025 from Rs. 988.1 crore 
as on March 31, 2025 . The bill discounting is likely to be nil as on November 30, 2025. However, going forward, a timely 
payment by the offtakers shall be a key rating monitorable. 
Sizeable disallowances in capital costs by the regulator, leading to moderate return indicators – Although the tariff is of a 
cost-plus nature, the regulatory framework allows for the recovery of only the approved capital costs. For NTPL, the CERC had 
allowed a project cost Rs. 6,004 crore against a total project cost of ~Rs. 7,212 crore. NTPL has filed an appeal with APTEL for 
the disallowed portion (~Rs. 897 crore, including towards IDC and incidental expense during construction) to be considered in 
the tariff. The disallowance of the capital cost has moderated the return indicators. The outcome of the quantum of the 
incremental project cost approved by APTEL has the potential to increase the regulated asset base, which can uplift the 
regulated returns from the prevailing levels.
Operating inefficiencies leading to under-recovery in fixed and variable charge - In four of the last five years ended FY2025, 
the company’s plant availability factor (PAF) has been lower than the normative levels, limiting full recovery of capacity 
charges. In FY2025, the PAF was marginally lower compared to the normative level due to an extended planned overhaul at 
one of the unit, resulting in under-recovery in fixed charges. However, the PAF improved to 89.1% in H1 FY2026, eliminating 
any fixed charge under-recovery so far in the current fiscal.
Further, there is continued under-recovery in energy charges due to higher-than-normative SHR and auxiliary consumption.
Also, the relatively high landed cost of fuel has increased the cost of generation, impacting the plant’s merit order position. 
However, the company expects operational efficiency to improve in the coming years, and ash handling augmentation plans 
in FY2027 are likely to reduce the reliance on imported coal, thereby lowering the landed fuel cost. These parameters would 
continue to be the key rating monitorable for the company.
Liquidity position: Adequate
The liquidity profile of NTPL is expected to remain adequate, supported by a healthy cash flow. This, along with its undrawn 
working capital lines and financial flexibility to raise capital at competitive rates, will continue to support the liquidity. Its
liquidity strengthened with the receipt of ~Rs. 1,861 crore as arrears between October 2024 and June 2025 which was used to 
repay the debt and reduce the working capital borrowings. Going forward, the company’s cash flows are expected to be 
sufficient to meet its annual debt repayments of Rs. 57 crore each in FY2027 and FY2028. Further, the company has unutilised 
fund-based working capital limits of more than ~Rs. 450 crore as of September 2025 against the drawing power of ~Rs. 650
crore.
Rating sensitivities 
Positive factors – An upgrade could be considered if NTPL demonstrates a sustained operational performance above the 
normative levels, coupled with the timely recovery of receivables, thereby strengthening the cash flow, liquidity and credit 
metrics. Additionally, a favourable resolution of the disallowed capital cost enhancing the earnings would further support an 
upgrade.
Negative factors – NTPL’s ratings could be under pressure if the credit profile of the parent – NLCIL – deteriorates, or if the 
linkages between NLCIL and NTPL weaken. Further, the ratings can be downgraded if the receivable position increases 
significantly, adversely impacting its liquidity profile. Continued underperformance on normative parameters adversely 
impacting the debt coverage metrics would be another trigger for a downward rating revision.

About the company 
NLC Tamil Nadu Power Limited (NTPL) is a joint venture (JV) between NLCIL and Tamil Nadu Generation and Distribution 
Corporation Limited (TANGEDCO), operating a 1,000-MW coal-based power generation project at Tuticorin, Tamil Nadu. NLC 
has an 89% equity stake, along with management control of the company, while TANGEDCO holds the balance 11%. The JV 
was incorporated on November 18, 2005, and the project was conceived by NLC as part of its expansion strategy. The total 
cost of setting up the project is ~Rs. 7,212 crore (excluding Rs. 81 crore LD charges recovered from contractor), funded through 
a mix of debt and equity in a ratio of 70:30. Unit I (500 MW) of the power plant commenced its operation on June 18, 2015, 
and Unit II (500 MW) on August 29, 2015.
