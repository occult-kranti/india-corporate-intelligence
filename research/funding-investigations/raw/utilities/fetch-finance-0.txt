Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has assigned its ‘Crisil AA-/Stable’ rating to Rs 850 crore non-convertible debentures (NCDs) and long-term bank facility of Adani Transmission Step-S ix L td (ATSSL).

ATSSL is implementing advanced metering infrastructure (AMI) for Maharashtra State Electricity Discom (Pkg V & Pkg VI) (c ollectively referred to as Discom) under design, build, finance, own, operate and transfer (DBFOOT) model.

The rating s factor in the company’s long-term revenue visibility due to the concession agreement (C A) with the discom for 10 years, wherein ATSSL is expected to implement the meters in 27 months as per the contracts and receive monthly charges from the discom for 93 months. Price risk is mitigated by fixed revenue per month as part of the contract. ATSSL has signed fixed-price contracts with suppliers for smart meters and associated infrastructure, thereby mitigating risk of any cost overrun. Major operations and maintenance (O&M) cost pertains to networking costs, database management and cloud services, for which ATSSL has entered into fixed-price contracts for nine years, mitigating any risk of O&M cost escalation.

The rating s are further supported by the obligor-co-obligor structure with four other smart meter special purpose vehicles (SPVs) of Adani Energy Solutions Ltd (AESL), thereby forming a restricted group (RG), wherein cash flow of each SPV can be used across the group for debt servicing and debt service reserve account (DSRA) maintenance, along with presence of cross-default clause across the obligor group.

The rating s also factor in the presence of a strong and experienced sponsor, AESL (‘Crisil AA+/Stable’), the parent. The smart meter business fits into the transmission and distribution (T&D) business of the Adani group and hence remains strategically important. AESL is likely to provide need-based assistance to all five smart meter SPVs in case of cashflow mismatch for debt servicing.

These strengths are partially offset by exposure to implementation risk and limited track record of the d irect d ebit facility (DDF). All the SPVs in the RG group have entered into a quadripartite agreement with the respective discom s, agent bank and payment gateway service provider, wherein all online payments to the discom for sale of power are first routed through a DDF account. This is expected to reduce the risk of delayed payment/non-payment from the discom. While the payment security mechanism enabled through the DDF reduces the collection risk, the track record of collection through the DDF is limited and varies across SPVs. Although the track record of collections for the installed meters has improved, the collections remain erratic and not timely as intended. Further the RG, excluding one SPV (BEST Smart Metering Ltd), receives lumpsum payments for the installed meters as part of the contract. These payments are also coming with a lag with large delays in Andhra Pradesh, Assam and Uttarakhand. Additionally, the company conducts s ystem a cceptance t est (SAT) for the installed meters, which often takes t ime and results in delayed invoice raising and temporary lag in revenue recognition. This issue is expected to persist until the implementation phase concludes. Timely billing and collections as per DDF will remain monitorable.

Further t he consolidated progress for the meters installed by the SPVs in the RG was 40% by December 2025 compared with expected progress of 50% by March-October 2025. An overall delay of ~10 months is observed in the installation. The delay is largely on account of issues related to unavailability of site where meter needs to be installed. As confirmed by management, no penalties have been incurred so far given the delay was on account of right of way (ROW) issues, which are to be provided by the discom as per CA. Any penalties levied on account of delays will remain monitorable. Furthermore, the modular nature of capital expenditure (capex) wherein for each monthly tranche of meters installed, the company is eligible to bill monthly service charges to discom (post achieving Go-Liv e) as well as drawn debt mitigates risks of project stasis.

The overall project, including all SPVs under RG, will be funded through debt, equity, lumpsum payments for installed meters as part of the contract and cash accrual received from ongoing installations. For debt, RG initially tied up a Rs 1000 crore construction debt revolver facility, which is now refinanced through a long-term takeout debt funding for first 24% of installed meters. AESL is expected to continue to do more rounds of takeout fundin g in tranches based on the number of smart meters installed in future. All five SPVs under the RG raised the first takeout facility of Rs 2105 crore in December 2025 towards the meters installed up to June 2025.

Analytical Approach

Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of five smart meter SPVs, in line with its criteria for rating entities in homogeneous groups. The rating of each SPV has been equated to that of the group. The SPVs are BEST Smart Metering Ltd, NE Smart Metering Ltd, Adani Transmission Step-Six Ltd, Adani Transmission Step-Seven Ltd and Adani Transmission Step-Eight Ltd. All SPVs are implementing agencies for DBFOOT the AMI. Each SPV acts as a co-obligor to the other. The cash flow of eac h SPV can be used across the group for debt servicing and DSRA maintenance, along with presence of cross-default clause across the obligor group.

Furthermore, Crisil Ratings has applied its parent notch-up framework to factor in the support of the parent and sponsor, AESL.

Key Rating Drivers - Strengths

Long-term CA providing revenue visibility: The ratings are supported by the long-term contract signed with ATSSL based on the total expenditure including capital and operational expenditure (TOTEX) mode for installation of smart meters and associated infrastructure under the revamped distribution sector scheme (RDSS). ATSSL is responsible for the installation of 133.3 lakh smart meters along with associated infrastructure through the pre-paid mode in Maharashtra. The total contract is for 10 years wherein A TSSL will have two revenue streams mainly— first from installation of smart meter for which discom shall pay lumpsum charges installed per meter and second from O&M of the smart meters for which discom shall pay on a monthly basis for both these services as per the terms of CA. The project SPV will continue to earn monthly service charge for every meter installed post achieving Go-Live status for an average span of 93 months from installation.

Modular nature of capex, debt disbursement and upfronting of ca sh flow: Under the CA, for each monthly tranche of meters installed, revenue will start accruing in the subsequent month. In addition, debt draw down has started post achievement of operational Go-Live (installation of 5% of meters under the project).

Further, post operation Go-Live is achieved, ATSSL has started receiving cash flows from Discom in the form of lumpsum and monthly service charge for the meters that have been installed ensuring sufficiency of cash flow for implementation of the project. The project also benefits from the modular nature of capex and hence in case the implementation is delayed, the capital deployed for meters installed till date will be revenue generating.

Strong sponsors and experienced management team: ATSSL is a wholly owned subsidiary of AESL, the T&D arm of the Adani group with transmission assets spanning 14 states and distribution network catering to Mumbai and Mundra. The AESL team has experience of executing large transmission projects and ATSSL is expected to receiv e operational and managerial support for deployment of smart meters. Furthermore, AESL is expected to support ATSSL in case of any cashflow mismatch for debt servicing.

Key Rating Drivers - We a knesses

Exposure to implementation risks: The project is running with delays in installation due to unavailability of site or other on-ground issues. However, in case of delays in implementation beyond the installation period (27 months from contract signing date), the company is unlikely to face any loss in revenue due to the design of the contract which enables part of revenue collection for the installed meters during the installation stage. However, any further delay beyond expectations may lead to some loss in revenue due to nature of contract and provisions of l iquidated d amages.

The project is being undertaken under the RDSS, which aims to improve efficiency, reduce losses and receive grants from the c entral government for the discom. Hence, given the importance of the project, implementation of the project is unlikely to face material risks.

Limited track record of DDF contracts in India with discoms: ATSSL has signed a quadripartite agreement with discoms, agent bank and payment gateway service provider wherein all online consumer payments from sale of power by the discom shall be first deposited to the DDF account. This is expected to reduce risk of delayed payment/ non-payment from the discom. Only post payment of any undisputed monthly service charge from the DDF to the company, money will flow to the discom. Currently, the DDF agreement has been signed for all five SPVs under the restricted group, but the collections are not timely and as intended. As per the company’s management, this is on account of teething issues and expected to streamline in the coming months. A long track record of timely collections through auto-debit mechanism will remain monitorable.

Liquidity Strong

The project is in the implementation stage, and the RG has refinanced the construction debt and raised the first takeout facility towards the meters that were installed till June 2025, with repayment commencing from January 2026 onwards. The funding risk is low as these projects are backed by AESL as the ultimate parent. Liquidity is expected to remain healthy, given the presence of strong financial covenants, which are tested on a half-yearly basis. A DSRA covering three months of debt obligation has been created. Sponsors will also provide for any cost and time overruns beyond base case during the implementation phase and arrange from their own sources for shortfall in creation of the DSRA.

Outlook Stable

Crisil Ratings believe ATSSL will benefit from a long-term CA signed with u tility and support from AESL.

About the Company

ATSSL is the project SPV and implementing agency for implementing AMI on DBFOOT basis in Maharashtra.

About AESL

AESL houses the T&D business of the Adani group. Assets are housed under individual SPVs, in which the company holds 100% stake (except for AEML, in which it holds 74.9% stake). As on March 31, 2025, AESL had a portfolio of 30 operational transmission SPVs, nine under-construction transmission assets, distribution business in Mumbai housed under AEML (catering to three million customers in Mumbai) and distribution business in Mundra Special Economic Zone. AESL is the largest privately operating transmission line company in India. The company has ventured into the smart meters business and has bagged nine contracts, involving installation of 22.8 million smart meters.
