## Airport file: what changes the theory

The original 37-page **85th PPPAC record** includes both final meeting minutes and an earlier DEA appraisal. The DEA proposal on PDF page 16 recommends a maximum of two airports per winner. The minutes on PDF pages 3–4 retain the airport EGoS's earlier no-cap choice. This is a documented difference in risk policy, **not the removal of an existing binding statutory cap**. The EGoS decision of 17 November 2018 precedes the DEA note of 10 December 2018 and the financial-bid outcome.

The final minutes also retain or revise safeguards. AAI explained a ₹1,000 crore net-worth threshold in light of uncapped awards. PPPAC increased the single-project technical-capacity threshold, revised termination terms, and addressed asset return and performance security. Omitting those measures would exaggerate the adverse interpretation.

MoCA's 21 January 2021 reply reports 32 bids from 10 entities and AEL highest at all six airports. It says the design broadened entry and that the committees considered ministry views. The source does not erase the distinct DEA recommendation. Conversely, that recommendation does not prove bribery, a predetermined winner or secret instructions. The government's 9.5% versus over 45% passenger-share comparison concerns different airport portfolios and years and is not a matched concentration estimate.

The 17 October 2022 Supreme Court order, inspected through a legal-text mirror, dismissed the connected Thiruvananthapuram challenge. Its ₹168/₹135 per-passenger comparison, agreed preference range, employee options and operational history materially constrain a simplistic rigged-award narrative. It does not determine every later service-quality or tariff question.

The remaining hypothesis asks whether uncapped simultaneous awards weakened resilience and later operator comparisons. A separate, disabled-by-default question link asks whether a *documented* bidder-specific representation affected the design. No such representation was found. It is not an observed edge or an allegation of an established agreement.

## Coal file: exact stages and denominators

The 20 October 2023 Ministry release publicly acknowledges ECoS discretion over repeated lone bids and reports eleven approvals by that date. The Ministry's stated aim is production and reduced import dependence, not revenue maximisation. A low bid count alone therefore cannot establish a breach of the applicable methodology.

The June 2025 opening lists 41 bids for 11 blocks. Three blocks are second-attempt round 11 lone bids: Jawardaha South, Margo East and Margo West. The separate bidder-frequency table cannot assign a company to any of these blocks. Their mine-specific ECoS decisions or vesting orders were not located in this bounded acquisition.

The 26 August 2025 notice identifies Patal East (Western Part)/Amalgam Steel and Power Limited and Tambia South/Sunflag Iron and Steel Co. Ltd. for bid-guarantee return. Those are **first-attempt round 12** blocks. This is evidence against universal automatic allocation of lone bids. It is an instruction to collect guarantees, not proof of actual bank release or a paid cash refund.

The 1 August 2025 overall result has seven closing-bid rows. **Five** exactly match the June opening table; two Rajgamar entries do not. The June release expressly excluded six underground blocks. Mandakini B appears at opening but not in this closing table; its absence does not establish cancellation. `raw/resources-contracts/coal-cohort-reconciliation.json` preserves the manual exact-name joins and count checks. No seven-of-eleven completion rate is valid across these mixed populations.

The 7 October 2026 official briefing lists 145 auctioned blocks across 15 tranches; the mutable index still displays 141 across 14. This is a source-vintage warning, not evidence of four missing assets. The briefing distinguishes a 2% underground floor from 4% generally and retains affiliate restrictions.
