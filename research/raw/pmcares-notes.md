# PM CARES — what the record establishes

Companion prose for `research/raw/pmcares.json`. Everything asserted here is sourced in
that file. Nothing here alleges misappropriation, wrongdoing or improper intent by any
person or institution. Figures are as at **12 August 2026**; the last audited statement
either PMO relief fund has published covers the year ended **31 March 2023**.

---

## Lead with the control, because the control changes the story

The two facts that carry almost all of the public argument about PM CARES are that it
refuses RTI requests on the ground that it is not a "public authority", and that its
published accounts stop at FY2022-23.

Run the identical analysis on the **Prime Minister's National Relief Fund** — a 1948
fund nobody had a theory about until 2020 — and both facts survive intact:

| Property | PM CARES | PMNRF |
|---|---|---|
| Asserts it is not a "public authority" under s.2(h) RTI Act | Yes | **Yes, first — from 2011** |
| Litigated the point to a Delhi High Court Division Bench | Not yet decided | **Split 1–1, 23 May 2018** |
| Audited by CAG | No | No |
| Audited by SARC & Associates, Sunil Kumar Gupta signing | Yes | Yes |
| Has since appointed KKC & Associates LLP | Yes | Yes |
| Publishes only a Receipts and Payments Account | Yes | Yes |
| Publishes the accompanying notes | **No** (1–6, 1–10, 1–11) | **No** (1–12) |
| Publishes an auditor's report / balance sheet | No | No |
| Last published financial year | **FY2022-23** | **FY2022-23** |
| Financial years published, of 7 elapsed | **4** | **4** |
| Administered by PMO officers on an honorary basis | Yes | Yes |
| Named in Schedule VII item (viii), Companies Act | Yes (2020) | Yes (since 2014) |

Two of two PMO-administered relief funds have this property set. A base rate of 100%
among comparables makes a property **non-discriminating** — it proves nothing about the
entity you started from. That is the tiering rule, and it applies here.

So the widely repeated claim that **PM CARES was created in order to escape the RTI
Act** dies on the date test. The identical position was asserted for PMNRF in response
to an RTI application in **2011**, litigated by PMNRF from **2012**, and left unresolved
by a split verdict in **2018**. PM CARES did not exist until 27 March 2020. A legal
position maintained in court for nine years before an entity existed cannot be that
entity's purpose.

### The 2018 split verdict, which almost nobody cites

`Prime Minister's National Relief Fund v. Aseem Takyar`, LPA 231/2016, Delhi High
Court, reserved 21 September 2017, pronounced 23 May 2018.

**S. Ravindra Bhat J** (later of the Supreme Court) held PMNRF *is* a public authority:

> "All those seconded to the office of the fund are public servants; they have no
> personal stake or interest. Such a fund cannot but be called as a public fund."

He also disposed of two arguments that are still being made in 2026 — that the Lok
Sabha and Rajya Sabha rules of procedure bar the questions, and that the fund is not
subject to CAG audit — on one ground:

> "Section 22 of the RTI Act overrides all other laws; the effect is that any impediment
> or bar — in any law in force — to the supply of information is overborne."

**Sunil Gaur J** held the opposite:

> "PMNRF is a Trust which is neither owned, controlled or financed by the Government …
> I humbly disagree with my esteemed brother and hold that appellant-PMNRF is not a
> 'Public Authority' within the definition of Section 2(h) of RTI Act."

The question went to a third judge. **Whether it has been answered, eight years later,
is not established by any document read here.** That is gap G05, and it is the single
most consequential unresolved legal fact in this file: it is the closest thing to
binding authority on the question everyone is arguing about PM CARES.

### One chronology worth noticing

In February 2026 it was reported that the PMO told the Lok Sabha Secretariat that
questions on PM CARES, PMNRF and the National Defence Fund are inadmissible under
**Rules 41(2)(viii) and 41(2)(xvii)** of the Lok Sabha rules of procedure.

Rule 41 is not a new argument. It is the rule **Gaur J relied on in 2018** to hold that
PMNRF is not a public authority — and it is the argument **Bhat J rejected in terms**.
The reasoning now reported to be operating inside the Lok Sabha Secretariat is the
reasoning that won exactly half of a split verdict and has never been confirmed by a
third judge.

That connection is documented on both ends and, on the searches run, has not been made
anywhere. It is also the weakest-sourced item in the file: the PMO communication itself
was not retrieved, and its date is unknown.

## What survives the control

Four things are genuinely specific to PM CARES.

### 1. PMNRF refuses PSU money. PM CARES accepts PSU CSR.

Both funds state their contribution rules on their own current websites, and the
statements cannot be reconciled:

> **PMNRF:** "Contributions flowing out of budgetary sources of Government **or from the
> balance sheets of the public sector undertakings** are not accepted."

> **PM CARES:** "PM CARES Fund accepts voluntary contributions by individuals/
> organizations **as well as contributions as part of CSR from Companies/Public Sector
> Undertakings (PSUs)**. However, contributions flowing out of budgetary sources of the
> PSUs are not accepted."

PMNRF excludes PSU balance-sheet money entirely. PM CARES excludes only *budgetary*
money, and expressly admits PSU CSR — which comes off the balance sheet. **The exclusion
PMNRF applies is precisely the one PM CARES drops.**

This matters because it supplies the missing mechanism for a base rate this project had
already established but not explained: **38 of 38 responding PSUs contributed** (of 55
asked, by RTI). That is not evidence of pressure and it is not evidence of anything
about any individual company — it is what happens when a statutory 2% levy acquires a
new permitted destination that the older equivalent destination refuses to accept.

The innocent reading ships with it: PMNRF's rule predates s.135 CSR entirely. Once CSR
became a compulsory levy with a closed list of destinations, a fund refusing CSR money
would simply be refusing money companies are compelled to spend somewhere.

### 2. PMNRF has no trust deed. PM CARES has one, and it pre-answers the statutory test.

PMNRF's origin is a press note of 24 January 1948 — which is why both judges in 2018
had to argue from a website "Preamble" rather than an instrument.

PM CARES's instrument, clause 5.3, executed and registered on 27 March 2020:

> "This Trust is not created by or under the Constitution of India or by any law made by
> the Parliament or by any State Legislature. This Trust is neither intended to be or is
> in fact owned, controlled or substantially financed by any Government or any
> instrumentality of the Government. There is no control of either the Central
> Government or any State Government/s, either direct or indirect, in functioning of the
> Trust in any manner whatsoever. The composition of the Board of Trustees consisting of
> holders of public office *ex officio* is merely for administrative convenience …"

Those are the four limbs of s.2(h) RTI Act, recited in the negative, in the instrument.
**Whether that is drafting prudence or anticipation is not something the record settles,
and this file asserts nothing about it.** The innocent reading is strong: any competent
draftsman settling a public charitable trust would recite non-governmental character,
because the alternative is to leave the characterisation to be litigated later — and on
the funding, the recital is on its face simply true.

What the record *does* establish is that the deed is internally in tension with itself.
The same instrument that says there is no governmental control "in any manner
whatsoever" also provides that the Prime Minister chairs the Board (6.6), that the
Ministers of Defence, Home Affairs and Finance are trustees *ex officio* (6.2), that the
Chairperson alone may add, remove or replace any trustee (6.7), that the Chairperson
alone may alter any term of the deed including the Objects (18.1), and that the PMO
provides the trust's administration (6.8). The fund's own FAQ adds that it is
administered by the Additional Secretary/Joint Secretary in charge in the PMO, and that
its head office **is** the Prime Minister's Office.

Both readings come from the same documents. This file records the tension and adjudicates
nothing — which is exactly what two experienced judges did in opposite directions on the
identical facts about the other fund.

### 3. Scale, and the speed of it

- **Rs 3,076.63 cr in five days** (27–31 March 2020). PMNRF's income for the whole of
  FY2019-20 was Rs 814.63 cr. **3.8×.**
- FY2020-21: PM CARES Rs 7,913.55 cr against PMNRF's Rs 657.07 cr. **12.0×.**
- Closing balance at 31 March 2023: PM CARES **Rs 6,283.68 cr** after three years;
  PMNRF Rs 5,956.50 cr after seventy-five.

### 4. Audit turnaround

100 days → 286 → 183 → **364**. The slowest year is the most recent published one.
PMNRF's one comparable year (FY2022-23) was signed in **182** days — half the time.

### The trap in the "Total" line

Every published statement's bold **Total** includes the opening balance brought forward.
The Rs 10,990.17 cr figure that circulates for FY2020-21 is that line. Actual FY2020-21
receipts are **Rs 7,913.55 cr**. Anyone adding the four Total lines to get lifetime
inflow double-counts three opening balances.

### The absence was tested, not assumed

The About page (last updated **10 August 2026**, two days before this file) indexes four
statements. The site's own naming convention is `Audited_Statement_YYYY_YY.pdf`;
`Audited_Statement_2023_24.pdf` and `Audited_Statement_2024_25.pdf` both return **HTTP
404**.

But note what this establishes and what it does not. Deed clause 14.3 requires an annual
audit. **It does not require publication of anything.** The 864-day gap is a publication
gap. It is not evidence that no audit exists.

### What is missing from every year

Each of the four statements says "Accompanying notes to the Financial Statements — 1 to
6 / 1 to 10 / 1 to 11" and "As per our report of even date". **None of those notes has
been published. No auditor's report has been published. No balance sheet. No income and
expenditure account.** Nineteen named disbursement captions cover Rs 8,131.85 cr: no
recipient is named, no transaction is dated, no counterparty is identified. No donor is
named in any year — and that one is a term of the trust instrument, not an
administrative choice.

The News Minute reported the missing notes in January 2025. What is added here is that
**PMNRF does the same thing** — notes 1 to 12, unpublished — so this is the PMO's
standing practice for both funds.

### The Rs 100 crore

The 13 May 2020 PIB release is explicit: "an amount of Rs. 100 Crore will be given from
PM CARES Fund as a helping hand to catalyse vaccine development, which will be utilized
under the supervision of Principal Scientific Advisor."

No head corresponding to vaccine development or research appears under Payments in
FY2019-20, FY2020-21, FY2021-22 or FY2022-23. The two vaccine-adjacent heads that do
exist are different things: **Rs 1,392.83 cr** for "Procurement of 6.6 crore doses" is
purchase, not development; **Rs 20.42 cr** for two DBT laboratories upgraded to Central
Drug Laboratory status is testing and batch release, not development.

That the pledge was never disbursed has been reported before. What is established here
is the **absence from the primary record**, across all four published statements, rather
than from a report.

The innocent readings ship with it and are not weak. A Receipts and Payments Account is
a cash statement showing only the captions the trustees chose; Rs 100 cr could sit inside
one of them. The pledge was to "catalyse" development, and if the intended vehicle was
superseded by a dose-procurement route thirteen times larger, non-disbursement is a
programme decision. And the notes, which would settle it, are not published.

### PM CARES for Children — do not divide

Rs 346.00 cr appears in FY2022-23; 4,345 children were recorded as supported at the
September 2022 trustee meeting. **Do not divide one by the other.** The Rs 10 lakh
entitlement is payable on attaining 23 years of age, so the FY2022-23 outflow is almost
certainly a corpus placement, not beneficiary payments. The relationship is unresolved
and is recorded as a gap.

## The CSR notification, and three corrections to how it is usually described

The instrument is **Notification G.S.R. 313(E), dated 26 May 2020, with effect from 28
March 2020** — established at documented tier not from mca.gov.in (which returns 403 to
everything) but from footnote 5 to Schedule VII in India Code's official consolidated
text of the Companies Act 2013.

**Correction 1 — this was not a new privilege for a Prime Minister's fund.** PMNRF has
been in item (viii) since the whole Schedule was substituted by G.S.R. 130(E) of 27
February 2014. PM CARES was *added to an existing entry*, not given a new one.

**Correction 2 — the retrospectivity is real and precise.** Notified 26 May 2020,
operative from 28 March 2020: **59 days backdated**, to the day of the PIB donation
appeal and the day after the deed was registered. The FY2019-20 voluntary contributions
of Rs 3,075.85 cr were received in the window 27–31 March 2020 — almost entirely inside
the retrospective period. The MCA's own account of the sequence, given in the Lok Sabha,
is that it "conveyed" eligibility administratively on 28 March 2020 and "subsequently,
issued a gazette notification to this effect".

**Correction 3 — the asymmetry that matters is central-versus-state, not PM CARES
versus PMNRF.** Item (viii) is confined to funds "set up by the Central Government". A
Chief Minister's Relief Fund is not, and it is not a State Disaster Management Authority
either, so it falls into neither item (viii) nor item (xii). Item (xii), "disaster
management, including relief, rehabilitation and reconstruction activities", was inserted
by **G.S.R. 390(E) of 30 May 2019** — a year before COVID — and is the head under which
SDMA contributions qualify. The exclusion of CM Relief Funds is a consequence of statutory
text as it stood in 2019, not a carve-out invented in 2020. That does not make the
exclusion uncontroversial. It makes the usual framing of it inaccurate.

## What the Supreme Court did and did not decide

`Centre for Public Interest Litigation v. Union of India`, W.P.(C) 546/2020, 18 August
2020 (Ashok Bhushan, R. Subhash Reddy, M.R. Shah JJ). Paragraph 67:

> "The PM CARES Fund is a public charitable trust and is not a Government fund."

Paragraph 70, answering the CAG point directly:

> "The nature of NDRF and PM CARES Fund are entirely different. The guidelines issued
> under Act, 2005 with regard to NDRF specifically provides for audit of the NDRF by the
> Comptroller & Auditor General of India whereas for public charitable trust there is no
> occasion for audit by the Comptroller & Auditor General of India."

**Section 2(h) of the RTI Act is not mentioned anywhere in the judgment.** The three
questions the Court answered — numbered 3, 4 and 5 — are all about the NDRF and the
Disaster Management Act 2005. Both sides of the public argument cite this judgment for a
proposition it does not contain, and that is recorded as a killed claim (R07).

## Gaps, at the same prominence as the findings

1. **FY2023-24, FY2024-25, FY2025-26 unpublished** — 864, 499 and 134 days out. Same for
   PMNRF.
2. **Notes, auditor's report, balance sheet: never published, any year, either fund.**
3. **No donor, no recipient, no date, no counterparty** in any published statement.
4. **Delhi High Court records unreachable.** The court serves judgments at
   `/app/case_number_pdf/<neutral citation>/<file>.pdf` — which is how the PMNRF judgment
   was obtained — but exposes no free-text search, and case status is captcha-gated.
   Without a neutral citation the path cannot be built, and this desk does not guess
   identifiers. So the PMO's affidavit of 14 September 2021, the Samyak Gangwal
   petitions, and the 22 January 2024 judgment in *CPIO/Dy CIT (HQ Exemption) v Girish
   Mittal* are all at **reported** tier, sourced to outlets and not to the record.
5. **The third-judge reference in LPA 231/2016 — outcome unknown.** The most important
   hole in the file.
6. **Parliament: the contradiction hunt is incomplete, and here is exactly why.** The
   individual answer PDFs *are* retrievable from sansad.in at static paths, both verified
   in this session:
   - `https://sansad.in/getFile/loksabhaquestions/annex/<lsNo><sessionNo>/AU<qno>.pdf?source=pqals`
   - `https://sansad.in/getFile/annex/<rsSessionNo>/AU<qno>.pdf?source=pqars`

   What is missing is the **question number**. The search UI is a client-rendered Next.js
   page with no data in `__NEXT_DATA__`; `/api_ls/question` exposes only Spring Boot
   actuator links and eight guessed search paths all 404'd; `loksabhaph.nic.in` did not
   resolve by DNS; `rsdoc.nic.in` returned 405 to GET. The one answer recorded — the
   "No sir" to Shri Sunil Dattatray Tatkare in the 2020 Monsoon Session — is therefore at
   **reported** tier. Recorded in this detail so the next pass starts where this one
   stopped.
7. **mca.gov.in 403s to everything**, including its static PDF paths; csr.gov.in and
   egazette.gov.in fail TLS verification. Only the circulars' own wording is missing —
   the statutory change itself was recovered from India Code.
8. **The PMO's communication on question admissibility** was not retrieved and its date
   is unknown.
9. **An unexplained second e-stamp.** The PDF the fund publishes as its trust deed
   contains a second e-Stamp certificate — IN-DL42057009731875S, **11 May 2020**,
   **Rs 6,750** — with no instrument attached to it. The registered deed itself is stamped
   Rs 100 on 27 March 2020. This is **not** evidence of an amendment, and it is recorded
   as an artefact rather than pressed into a theory it does not support.
10. **cag.gov.in was not checked at all this session.** The Supreme Court's point is that
    a charitable trust gives no occasion for CAG audit — but that says nothing about the
    *spending ministries* that executed PM CARES-funded procurement: MoHFW, CMSS, DRDO,
    IOCL through MoPNG. Per this project's own research plan (P1.2), that is where the
    audit trail actually is. It is untouched here.
11. The relationship between Rs 346.00 cr and 4,345 children.
12. The **FCRA exemption** is asserted by the fund; the notification granting it was not
    retrieved and its number and date are unknown.

## A note on method, because the numbers depend on it

Both funds publish their accounts as **scanned images with no text layer**. pypdf
extracted 0 characters from three of the four PM CARES PDFs and 69 from the fourth.
Every page was rasterised with `pdftoppm` at 200 dpi and read as an image.

Every column was then re-added programmatically against its printed total. **FY2022-23
casts exactly in both directions.** FY2020-21 receipts and FY2021-22 payments each come
out **Rs 1** away from their printed totals — far more likely a single misread digit on a
scan than an error in the statement. The printed totals are carried as authoritative and
the discrepancy is recorded rather than quietly patched, because a component figure read
off a scan is a weaker fact than a bold total and the file should say so.

## What would change the conclusion

- **An FY2023-24 statement appears for one fund and not the other.** That breaks the
  control and would be the first evidence the two funds' practice has diverged.
- **The third-judge reference in LPA 231/2016 is answered.** Either way, it changes the
  tier of the central contested item.
- **Question numbers are obtained for the PM CARES parliamentary answers.** The PDFs are
  then one fetch away and the contradiction hunt can actually be finished.
- **A CAG report covering MoHFW/CMSS procurement executed with PM CARES money surfaces.**
  That is the only route to the ventilator and oxygen-plant detail the fund's own
  nineteen captions do not carry.
