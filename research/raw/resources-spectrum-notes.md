# Indian telecom spectrum auctions — what the record establishes

Companion prose for `research/raw/resources-spectrum.json`. As of **2026-08-12**.
Everything asserted here is sourced in that file. Nothing here alleges wrongdoing by any
named company or individual.

Scope: the ten central-government auctions of telecom access spectrum from 2010 to 2024,
and the documentary record of the 2G licences issued in 2008 — from the CAG's presumptive
estimates through the Supreme Court's 2012 cancellation to the criminal court's 2017
acquittal of every accused.

---

## 2. What the unsold-share series does **not** show

This is the part that ships with the number, not after it.

**A high unsold share is exactly what an aggressive reserve price produces.** It is also
exactly what a market with no unmet demand produces. Both readings fit every row of the
table above, and nothing in this register distinguishes them. Four facts should stop anyone
reading the series as a scandal series.

**The regulator treated the 2016 result as a pricing problem and cut the price.** The single
band that accounts for 55% of everything unsold in 2016 is 700 MHz — 770 MHz offered, not one
bid. In its recommendations of 1 August 2018 TRAI recommended "that reserve price of 700 MHz
band should be equal to 2 times of reserve price of 1800 MHz spectrum band" (¶3.106), and
that "in LSAs where spectrum was offered in October 2016 auction but remained entirely
unsold, the reserve price should be lower of the figures — 80% of average valuation or the
reserve price as fixed in October 2016 auction" (¶3.59). A regulator lowering a price after
nobody bought is the mechanism working.

**The regulator also recorded that the buyer side had shrunk, for reasons outside any
auction.** TRAI, ¶3.104: *"At the time of previous auctions on an average there were 8 to 10
TSPs… in last two years the Telecom Industry has been undergoing consolidation phase… With
the result, now the no of TSPs is reduced to practically four. Further, consequent upon issue
of spectrum trading and sharing guidelines, in past two years secondary spectrum trading and
sharing market is evolving fast. These two developments have increased the spectrum
availability with existing leading TSPs manifold."* Fewer buyers, already holding more
spectrum, produce a lower sold share at any price.

**In 2024 the seller gave an operational reason in the release itself:** *"As auction for 5G
spectrum was held recently & 5G Monetization is still in progress, no bidding took place in
800MHz, 2300MHz, 3300MHz and 26GHz bands."* An operator sitting on unused spectrum has no
reason to buy more at any price.

**And the two 100% rows were engineered on the supply side.** The 2010 auctions offered three
or four blocks per circle into a field of seven to eleven interested parties. Scarcity was
designed in.

A sold share is a joint product of supply, reserve price and demand. This register measures
one of the three.

### The statistic the series cannot see

The realisation-to-reserve ratio is a sharper test, and it can be computed for only four of
ten auctions because the reserve valuation is not published for the other six:

| Auction | Reserve | Realised | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2010 3G | ₹14,330 cr | ₹67,718.95 cr | **472%** |
| 2010 BWA | ₹5,250 cr | ₹38,542.11 cr | **734%** |
| 2021 | ₹3,92,332.70 cr | ₹77,814.80 cr | **19.8%** |
| 2024 | ₹96,238.45 cr | ₹11,340.78 cr | **11.8%** |

In 2010 the market paid five to seven times the reserve. In 2021 and 2024 it paid a fifth and
an eighth. That is the innocent reading of the whole series in four numbers — and it is also
confounded, because supply, reserve price and the number of buyers all moved together.

## 3. Two traps in the numbers themselves

### The units trap: 2022 is not comparable with anything

The 2022 auction sold 71% of what was offered, which reads as the best result in the series.
**87% of the 72,098 MHz offered was 26 GHz millimetre wave** — a band never auctioned in India
before, which DoT itself describes as having "very short range" and being "likely to be
utilized for captive or non-public network". 45,350 MHz of the 51,236 MHz sold was 26 GHz.

Strip out 26 GHz and 3300 MHz and count only the traditional sub-3 GHz bands with published
columns: **396.2 MHz of 1,417 MHz, or 27.96%** — worse than 2016 and worse than 2021. The
mmWave band carries 30% of the quantum at 2.3% of the value (₹14,709 cr of ₹1,50,173 cr).

Adding a 26 GHz megahertz to a 700 MHz megahertz is not addition. It is the clearest case in
the file of a headline being an artefact of units.

### The denominator trap: 2024, two figures one day apart

- **25 June 2024**, opening release: *"Total quantum of spectrum being auctioned is 10,522.35
  MHz in various bands valuing Rs 96,238.45 Crores at reserve prices."*
- **26 June 2024**, closing release: *"A total quantum of 141.4 MHz (26.5%) was sold from the
  balance 533.6 MHz Spectrum."*

141.4 ÷ 10,522.35 = **1.34%**. 141.4 ÷ 533.6 = **26.5%**, which is what the closing release
prints. Neither release defines "balance". What is checkable: the four bands in which bidding
actually took place — 900, 1800, 2100, 2500 — were offered at 117.2 + 221.4 + 125 + 70 =
**533.6 MHz** exactly. The closing denominator is the spectrum in the bands that turned out to
draw bids, identified after the auction closed.

That is a legitimate statistic and it is not the same statistic. A denominator chosen after
the result is known cannot be compared across years, so the series uses the ex-ante figure and
carries both.

## 4. What the bidder series shows, and what it can never show

The count falls from nine and eleven applicants in 2010 to three participants in 2021 and
2024. The register records the fall and does not explain it, for three reasons.

**The units change.** Applicants in 2010, winners in 2012, participants from 2016 — an
applicant list is longer than a bidder list, which is at least as long as a winner list.

**The eligible universe changed outside the auctions.** 122 licences were quashed in 2012;
the sector merged and exited between 2016 and 2020; and TRAI's own account (¶3.104, above)
names merger and secondary trading, not auction design.

**And the count cannot detect losing.** Across the seven auctions with a documented bidder
count, **32 of 34 bidders won spectrum**. Exactly two bidders in the entire file bid and won
nothing: Tata Teleservices in February 2014 and Telewings Communications Services in March
2015. From 2016 onward the rate is **17 of 17**.

A count that can barely fall below the winner count carries almost no information about
competitive tension. The statistic that would — *how many bidders contested the same block in
the same service area* — is **not published for any Indian spectrum auction** in any document
opened for this file. DoT's daily reports publish "Aggregate Demand" and "Excess Demand" per
service area per band per round, and both count *blocks* demanded, not bidders demanding them.

That absence is a finding, and it is rendered as loudly as the series it undermines.

**One row deserves its own sentence.** In March 2013, applications closed with **one**
applicant for the 800 MHz band and **zero** applicants for 1800 MHz and 900 MHz. The auctions
in those two bands did not take place, for want of anyone to bid in them. The 800 MHz auction
ran on 11 March 2013 and ended after **three clock rounds**; DoT's own analysis records the
share of spectrum sold above reserve price that year as **0%**. Every block that sold, sold at
the floor. That is what an auction with one bidder is.

## 5. The 2G record, end to end — the worked example

This is the case the platform is built around, and it is built around it because it is the
clearest demonstration that **the date test and the outcome test are different tests, and a
record that runs only one of them gets the wrong answer.**

### 5.1 The date test: the 2008 allocation, as a sequence

Every element below is a date in a tabled audit report or a Supreme Court judgment. None of
it is an allegation.

- **24 September 2007** — DoT press release: no new UAS licence application will be accepted
  after **1 October 2007**. TRAI had recommended "no cap" three weeks earlier, so a deadline
  functioned as one. DDG(AS) had suggested 10 October; the Minister fixed 1 October.
- **24 September – 1 October 2007** — the Supreme Court records that "over 300 applications
  were received". The CAG puts it at 408 more applications in eight days, taking the pile to
  575.
- **2 November 2007** — the Minister decides the operative cut-off is **25 September 2007**,
  moving it *backwards* to the day the newspapers carried the deadline. 232 applications
  survive.
- **7 January 2008** — the first-come-first-served rule, in force since 2003, is changed.
- **7 January 2008** — the full Telecom Commission meeting scheduled for **9 January** to
  discuss licences and spectrum pricing is postponed to **15 January**.
- **10 January 2008, afternoon** — a press release announces both the cut-off date (unpublished
  for ten weeks) and the new rule: *"who so ever, complies with the condition of LoIs first
  will be granted UAS licence"*. A second release the same day: *"All above are requested to
  assemble at 3:30 pm on 10.1.2008 at Committee Room, 2nd Floor, Sanchar Bhavan… The companies
  which fail to report before 4:30 P.M. on 10.1.2008, the responses of DOT will be dispatched
  by post."* Letters of Intent issue; the CAG notes the notice gave "less than an hour".
- **The price** — ₹1,658 crore for a pan-India licence, the figure discovered by bidding in
  2001, unrevised.

The sequence is checkable and it is what the date test is for. What it does *not* establish
is that any named person acted corruptly, or that any named company knew the sequence in
advance. The CAG's own language on that point is conditional — *"some licensees, who **could
proactively anticipate** such procedural changes"* — and it names nobody.

**A caution the register carries: how many licences?** Four numbers circulate for what
happened on and around 10 January 2008, and they come from the same two publishers:

- **121** — LoIs issued on 10 January 2008 (CAG Report No. 19 of 2010, Chapter 4 body)
- **120** — licences issued that day (CAG's *own web page* for that report)
- **120** — applications *accepted* that day (Supreme Court, ¶43)
- **122** — new UAS licences awarded across 2008 (CAG, Chapter 6; and the CBI's FIR)

These are three or four different events. They are routinely quoted as one.

### 5.2 The four estimates, not one

The sentence "the CAG found a 2G loss of ₹1.76 lakh crore" is wrong in three separate ways,
and the report contradicts it in terms.

**It is a range of four, not a figure.** Executive Summary item (xi), reproduced as the closing
table of Chapter 5, prints four columns side by side (₹ crore):

| Basis | New licences | Dual technology | Beyond contracted 6.2 MHz | **Total** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S Tel rate | 38,950 | 14,573 | 13,841 | **67,364** |
| Rates on the basis of 3G auction | 1,02,498 | 37,154 | 36,993 | **1,76,645** |
| Sale of equity — Unitech | 40,442 | 15,132 | 14,052 | **69,626** |
| Sale of equity — Swan | 33,230 | 12,433 | 12,003 | **57,666** |

₹1.76 lakh crore is the **top** of a range whose bottom is ₹57,666 crore — a factor of three.

**Each column is a different assumption, and each is disputable:**

- *S Tel*: that a letter of 27 December 2007 offering ₹13,752 crore over ten years, discounted
  to ₹8,825 crore, is a market valuation. DoT's recorded objection: the offer carried
  conditions and was later withdrawn in the Supreme Court.
- *3G auction*: that spectrum given in January 2008 was worth the pan-India ₹16,750 crore
  discovered in May 2010 — two years and a different band later. The CAG expressly notes that
  the TRAI recommendation underpinning the comparison "ha[s] not so far been accepted by the
  Government". DoT's recorded objection: "it was incorrect to calculate the notional loss…
  at 3G price".
- *Unitech equity*: that Telenor's ₹6,120 crore for 67.25% values the whole at ₹9,100 crore and
  that it was paid for spectrum. Unitech's contrary claim is in the report: ₹2,100 crore of
  effort had already gone in and "the entity's value was not only that of spectrum."
- *Swan equity*: Etisalat's ₹3,217 crore for 44.73% scaled to ₹7,758 crore pan-India — the
  lowest of the four, and the bottom of the range.

**A fifth of it is not about the 122 licences at all.** ₹36,993 crore of the ₹1,76,645 crore is
the third row — spectrum given to *thirteen incumbent operators* beyond their contracted
6.2 MHz. The report's own figure for the 157 licences of 2007-08 is different, and it is a
different kind of number too: *"the presumptive value of 2G spectrum on account of grant of 157
licenses in different circles during 2007-08 would be in the range of approximately ₹58,000
crores to ₹1,52,038 crores."* Note *value*, not loss.

**And the report says, twice, that it is not a measurement.** The Preface:

> "Each set of assumptions underlying the economic models could be open to questions and be
> disputed. For this reason we have only attempted to arrive at a presumptive value in this
> Report."

Paragraph 5.1.2:

> "Any loss ascertained while attempting to value the spectrum in hindsight can only be
> 'presumptive'…"

Chapter 6:

> "The fact that there has been loss to the national exchequer in the allocation of 2G spectrum
> cannot be denied. However, the amount of loss could be debated."

The register therefore tiers the ₹1,76,645 crore as **`documented` — as an estimate the CAG
published, expressly not as a loss that occurred** — the same construction the coal register
uses for the ₹1.86 lakh crore, which is likewise a *gain to private parties* of which the
auditor said only that *a part* could have been captured.

### 5.3 The Supreme Court, 2 February 2012

*Centre for Public Interest Litigation & Ors v. Union of India & Ors*, W.P.(C) No. 423 of 2010,
with Dr Subramanian Swamy's W.P.(C) No. 10 of 2011. Bench of **two**: **G.S. Singhvi, J**
(author) and **Asok Kumar Ganguly, J**. Read from the Supreme Court's own server.

The operative order, ¶81:

> "(i) The licences granted to the private respondents on or after 10.1.2008 pursuant to two
> press releases issued on 10.1.2008 and subsequent allocation of spectrum to the licensees are
> declared illegal and are quashed. (ii) The above direction shall become operative after four
> months. (iii) … TRAI shall make fresh recommendations for grant of licence and allocation of
> spectrum in 2G band in 22 Service Areas by auction, as was done for allocation of spectrum in
> 3G band. (iv) The Central Government shall consider the recommendations of TRAI and take
> appropriate decision within next one month and fresh licences be granted by auction."

On first-come-first-served, ¶76:

> "There is a fundamental flaw in the first-come-first-served policy inasmuch as it involves an
> element of pure chance or accident… while transferring or alienating the natural resources,
> the State is duty bound to adopt the method of auction by giving wide publicity so that all
> eligible persons can participate in the process."

On the 2007-08 exercise, ¶77:

> "The exercise undertaken by the officers of the DoT between September, 2007 and March 2008,
> under the leadership of the then Minister of C&IT was wholly arbitrary, capricious and
> contrary to public interest apart from being violative of the doctrine of equality."

**Two precisions the register insists on.**

*The order never says 122.* The string "122" appears exactly once in 94 pages, in the recital
of counsel's submissions at page 64. The order quashes a *description*, not a count. 122 is
the CAG's and the CBI's licence count and is accurate as such; "the Supreme Court cancelled
122 licences" is a correct shorthand for the effect and a misquotation of the order. This is
the same failure mode the coal register documents at length around "204 of 218".

*The Court severed the criminal case from its own findings* — ¶81(vii), and this is the hinge
of the whole record:

> "However, it is made clear that the observations made in this judgment shall not, in any
> manner, affect the pending investigation by the CBI, Directorate of Enforcement and others
> agencies or cause prejudice to those who are facing prosecution… **and the Special Judge, CBI
> shall decide the matter uninfluenced by this judgment.**"

Everything in ¶81(iii) and (iv) is also why the rest of this register exists: the 2012, 2013
and 2014 auctions are the direct execution of that direction.

### 5.4 The outcome test: 21 December 2017

**Every accused was acquitted.** Court of **O. P. Saini, Special Judge, CBI (04) (2G Spectrum
Cases)**, Patiala House Courts, New Delhi. **CC No. 01/11**, RC No. 45(A) 2009. Instituted
2 April 2011; final arguments concluded 26 April 2017; order reserved 5 December 2017;
pronounced **21 December 2017**; **1,553 pages**. **17 accused — 14 individuals and 3
companies — all acquitted.**

¶1817:

> "There is no evidence on the record produced before the Court indicating any criminality in
> the acts allegedly committed by the accused persons relating to fixation of cut-off date,
> manipulation of first-come first-served policy, allocation of spectrum to dual technology
> applicants, ignoring ineligibility of STPL and Unitech group companies, non-revision of entry
> fee and transfer of Rs. 200 crore to Kalaignar TV (P) Limited as illegal gratification. The
> charge sheet of the instant case is based mainly on misreading, selective reading,
> non-reading and out of context reading of the official record."

¶1818–1819:

> "The end result of the above discussion is that, **I have absolutely no hesitation in holding
> that the prosecution has miserably failed to prove any charge against any of the accused,
> made in its well choreographed charge sheet.** … Accordingly, all accused are entitled to be
> acquitted and are acquitted."

¶1811:

> "I may also add that for the last about seven years, on all working days, summer vacation
> included, I religiously sat in the open Court from 10 AM to 5 PM, awaiting for someone with
> some legally admissible evidence in his possession, but all in vain. Not a single soul turned
> up. This indicates that everybody was going by public perception created by rumour, gossip
> and speculation. However, public perception has no place in judicial proceedings."

¶1814, under the heading "About the DoT Files":

> "…on account of the various actions and inactions of the officials, as noted above, nobody
> believed the version of DoT and a huge scam was seen by everyone where there was none. These
> factors compelled people to conjecture about a big scam. Thus, **some people created a scam by
> artfully arranging a few selected facts and exaggerating things beyond recognition to
> astronomical levels.**"

**The same judge acquitted all nineteen accused in the money-laundering case the same day.**
Complaint Case No. 01/14, ECIR/31/DZ/2010, 105 pages, ¶166:

> "In view of acquittal of accused in the case relating to scheduled offence, there are no
> 'proceeds of crime'. 'Proceeds of crime' is the foundational fact for the offence of
> money-laundering. Since there are no 'proceeds of crime', there can be no offence of
> money-laundering, as nothing remains to be laundered."

Property worth **₹223.55 crore** attached by the ED was ordered released.

**Read the PMLA acquittal correctly in both directions.** It is *derivative*: the court did not
find that the ₹200 crore transfer did not happen, it found that once the predicate offence
collapsed there was nothing for the Act to operate on, and expressly declined to go further.
Anyone citing it as an independent finding on the money trail is over-reading it — in the
opposite direction from those who ignore it.

**A provenance weakness the register states rather than hides.** Both 2017 judgments were read
from Internet Archive captures of a third-party judgment database. `indiankanoon.org` is now
behind a Cloudflare challenge this environment cannot pass; Delhi district-court hosts under
`dcourts.gov.in` did not resolve; `services.ecourts.gov.in` is behind a session-bound captcha;
`judgments.ecourts.gov.in` covers only the Supreme Court and High Courts. Every
case-management fact in those captures — case number, dates, page count, outcome, number of
accused, witness counts — *is* independently corroborated by the Delhi High Court's own
judgment, read from `delhihighcourt.nic.in`. The quoted passages rest on the mirror, and the
record says so.

Leave was granted on **22 March 2024** — six years and three months after the acquittal — by
Justice Dinesh Kumar Sharma, in a 120-page judgment. Its operative paragraphs:

> "269. I must hasten to add that this court is expressing these concerns only to take a
> **prima facie** view of the matter and nothing in this order to be construed as final opinion.
> 270. The court… has reached on an objective satisfaction that there is a prima facie case
> which requires deeper examination and re-appreciation/re-appraisal of entire evidence.
> 271. … arguable points have been made out by the CBI thereby converting grant of Leave to
> Appeal."

**Granting leave is not a finding on the merits, and the judgment says so.** It is a threshold
ruling that the CBI has raised arguable points. The acquittal remains the operative outcome
unless and until the appeal succeeds.

The appeal's own listing history, from the court's cause-history record: listed 12 February,
4–5 May and 19 May 2026, then set down for four consecutive days from 28 to 31 July 2026 for
arguments — and moved again, to 27–28 October 2026. **Nine years and eight months after the
acquittal, and two years and five months after leave was granted, the appeal has not been
argued to a conclusion on the record opened for this file.** The Enforcement Directorate, eight
years and five months after filing, does not yet have leave.

*"Pending" is a fact with a date and it decays.* Anyone reading this file after 28 October 2026
must re-check the status before repeating it. That is why every source object carries a
retrieval date and why this record carries an explicit `asOfDate`.

**A small precision on the case reference.** Three courts render it three ways: the trial
judgment says *"RC No: 45 (A) 2009, CBI, ACB, New Delhi"*; the High Court's leave judgment says
*"RC.DAL2009.A.0045"*; the High Court's own case-history record says *"DAI-2009-A-0045"*. All
three are carried and none is corrected against the others.

No Supreme Court proceeding arising out of the acquittal appears in any document opened here —
which is **not** a finding that none exists. No exhaustive Supreme Court sweep was completed.

### 5.6 Why both tests are needed

Three institutions asked three different questions about the same events and got three
different answers, and none of the three answers is wrong.

| Question | Who answered | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| Was the administrative process lawful? | Supreme Court, 2012 | **No** — arbitrary, capricious, violative of the doctrine of equality |
| What might the spectrum have fetched under a different process? | CAG, 2010 | **Somewhere between ₹57,666 cr and ₹1,76,645 cr, depending on which of four assumptions you take** |
| Was any criminal charge proved against anyone? | Special CBI Court, 2017 | **No** |

**The date test** — run the sequence, check the chronology — establishes the first and is what
makes the 2008 record a finding rather than a rumour. **The outcome test** — follow the matter
to its end and publish where it landed — establishes the third. A file that runs the date test
and stops has not produced a shorter record. It has produced one that reads as an unrebutted
accusation against seventeen people a court acquitted, and it has left the reader unable to
tell that the appeal against that acquittal is undecided too.

That is why the acquittal is a first-class record in this file with its own tier, its own
exact wording, its own falsifier and its own provenance caveat — not a footnote to the
allegation.

## 6. Where official sources disagree

Nine conflicts are carried in full, with both values, rather than resolved:

1. **2010 MHz sold.** 355 and 880 MHz counted from DoT's own result notes; **465** and **1,320**
   MHz in DoT's WPC analysis. The gap is exactly one block per service area in each case —
   the shape of the BSNL/MTNL allotments — but no document says so, so it stays an observation.
2. **2012 quantity and money.** 126.25 MHz / ₹9,407.64 cr counted from DoT result documents;
   **127.5 MHz / ₹9,642.11 cr** in the WPC analysis. One block and ₹234.47 crore apart.
3. **2012 offered quantum, inside one document.** The NIA's prose gives 61 blocks of 800 MHz
   (76.25 MHz); its Table 2-B lists 76 frequency spots (95 MHz).
4. **2013 money.** ₹3,639.48 cr computed from DoT's own corrected result annexure; **₹4,113.65
   cr** in the WPC analysis — for an auction with one bidder and 24 blocks.
5. **2015 money.** ₹1,09,874 cr announced by PIB as provisional on the closing day;
   **₹1,13,932.2 cr** in the WPC analysis. 3.7% apart.
6. **2016 money.** ₹65,789.12 cr computed from DoT's frequency-assignment report (and matching
   the figure in wide circulation); **₹64,809.12 cr** in the WPC analysis. Exactly ₹980 crore
   apart, which happens to be the Karnataka 2300 MHz per-block price. An observation, not an
   explanation.
7. **2021 offered quantum — three official figures in eleven weeks.** 2,250 MHz (sum of the
   NIA release's band figures), **2,251.25 MHz** (Cabinet, and the figure the ₹3,92,332.70 cr
   reserve attaches to), **2,308.80 MHz** (closing release, "including spectrum that is expiring
   up to December, 2021").
8. **2021 winning total.** ₹77,814.80 cr (provisional, 2 March) versus **₹77,820.81 cr**
   (payable, 16 April). ₹6.01 crore apart, six weeks apart, both DoT.
9. **2022 Bharti Airtel's bid, inside one press release.** The prose says ₹43,048 cr; the table
   two paragraphs below says **₹43,084 cr**. The prose figures sum to ₹1,50,137 cr against the
   ₹1,50,173 cr total stated in the same sentence; the table's sum to exactly ₹1,50,173 cr.

Plus the 2024 double denominator (§3) and the four different licence counts for January 2008
(§5.1).

The register's rule is the coal file's rule: where two official sources disagree, carry both
and say so. Picking one and moving on is the thing that makes a register untrustworthy in the
places nobody checks.

## 7. What is not established

Full list in the file's `gaps` array (20 entries). The six that matter most:

1. **No single official table covers 2010–2024.** The nearest thing — DoT's WPC Wing paper
   *"A short Analysis of Spectrum auction in India"* by V. J. Christopher, Senior Deputy
   Wireless Adviser — tabulates six auctions and stops at 2016, has no offered-MHz and no
   reserve-price column, and is the source of four of the nine conflicts above. It is an
   officer-authored study, not a statutory return. **DoT Annual Reports 2013-14 to 2016-17
   could not be retrieved at all** and are the most promising missing document.
2. **Reserve price totals are missing for six of ten auctions** — 2012, 2013, 2014, 2015, 2016
   and 2022 — which is why the realisation-to-reserve ratio, the sharpest available test of an
   aggressive reserve, exists for only four rows.
3. **The offered quantum is missing for 2013, 2014 and 2015** even though their sold shares are
   published, so nothing about those three years can be recomputed or recombined.
4. **Bidders per lot is published nowhere, for any auction, ever.** See §4.
5. **The BSNL/MTNL component of the 2010 auctions is not broken out.** The Rajya Sabha answer
   of 19 August 2010 gives combined totals and says in terms "This amount includes the share of
   MTNL/BSNL", without splitting it.
6. **No official-court-server copy of the 21 December 2017 judgments could be opened**, and
   **the Essar/Loop acquittal of the same day is not in this file at all** — the Internet
   Archive has no capture of it, so nothing about its case number, accused or reasoning is
   asserted.

## 8. Falsifiers

Stated in advance so the claims above can be killed cleanly.

- **On the unsold-share series.** If a DoT annual report or a consolidated parliamentary answer
  publishes offered-versus-sold for 2013, 2014 and 2015 with denominators, three rows of the
  series move from "DoT's percentage" to a checkable ratio — and if those denominators
  contradict the percentages, the WPC paper stops being usable and six figures in this file go
  with it.
- **On 2016.** If DoT ever printed a winning-bid total for the October 2016 auction, and it is
  ₹64,809.12 crore, then this file's ₹65,789.12 crore is a computation error and should be
  retired — even though its MHz reconciles three ways.
- **On the 2012 zero in 800 MHz.** One November 2012 800 MHz public report showing any sale
  kills the inferred zero and moves the 2012 sold share.
- **On the bidder series.** If a per-lot bidder count is published anywhere for any Indian
  auction, §4's central claim — that the participation data cannot detect competition — fails,
  and the series should be rebuilt on that field instead.
- **On the CAG range.** If the printed table in Report No. 19 of 2010 does not carry these four
  columns with these totals, or does not carry the qualifying language, record `2g-02` is wrong
  and should be deleted. Both were read from the report's own PDF on `cag.gov.in`.
- **On the 2017 judgments.** An official copy of CC No. 01/11 from a Delhi district-court server
  that does not contain ¶¶1811–1823 in these terms, or records a different outcome, kills
  records `2g-04` and `2g-05`. Obtaining an official copy would also *upgrade* them, from
  "mirror, corroborated" to primary record.
- **On the appeal.** Any Delhi High Court judgment disposing of CRL.A. 277/2024 supersedes §5.5
  and must be recorded the day it is found, in either direction.
