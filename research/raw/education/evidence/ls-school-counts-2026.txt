CLOSURE OF GOVERNMENT SCHOOLS 
1374. Shri Anand Bhadauria: 
Will the Minister of EDUCATION be pleased to state:  
(a) whether widespread closure and merger of thousands of primary and secondary 
Government schools in the country in recent years has adversely affected the 
implementation of RTE Act, 2009 which guarante es free and compulsory education for 
children aged 6-14 in neighbourhood schools; 
(b) if so, the details thereof; 
(c) the data on such closures and mergers of schools in the country during the last five years 
State/UT and year-wise including district wise for Uttar Pradesh; 
(d) the extent to which the increased distance to merged schools affect enrolment, retention 
and learning outcomes, especially for girls, SC/ST, and economically weaker children, 
State/UT-wise; and 
(e) the details of corrective measures initiated by the Government to ensure full compliance 
with RTE norms regarding pupil -teacher ratio, infrastructure and access to schools as per 
RTE Act, 2009? 
 
ANSWER 
MINISTER OF STATE IN THE MINISTRY OF EDUCATION 
(SHRI JAYANT CHAUDHARY) 
 
(a) to ( c): Education is in the concurrent list of the Constitution and the opening, 
merger/closing of schools is within  the purview of respective State Government and UT 
Administration. The National Education Policy 2020 underscores that although consolidation 
of schools is an option that is often discussed, it must be carried out very judiciously, and 
only when it is ensured that there is no impact on access (Para 7.4). The aim of the school 
complex/cluster is to ensure greater resource efficiency and more effective f unctioning, 
coordination, leadership, governance, and management of schools in the cluster.

  As per the information provided/uploaded by the States and UTs on the Unified 
District Information System for Education Plus  (UDISE+), the number of Government 
schools in the country, State/ UT-wise for the last five years is at Annexure I. The district -
wise number of Government schools in Uttar Pradesh for the last five years is at Annexure

(d): The Gross Enrolment Ratio (GER) of girls, SC students and ST students during the 
last 2 years is at Annexure III, Annexure IV and Annexure V, respectively. 
 
 The overall Retention Rate for girls during the last 2 years is at Annexure VI.  
 
The Ministry of Education undertakes large-scale assessments to measure learning 
outcomes in schools across the country from time to time. In this regard, the PARAKH 
Rashtriya Sarvekshan 2024 (formerly National Achievement Survey) was conducted by the 
National Assessment Centre -PARAKH, NCERT under the aegis of the Department of 
School Education & Literacy, Ministry of Education to measure the baseline performance 
under the National Education Policy (NEP) 2020 in development of competencies among 
students at the end of the Foundational, Preparatory and Middle Stages (Grades 3, 6 and 9 
respectively) of schooling. Nationwide over 21.15 lakh students, including SC, ST and OBC 
students, and 2.70 lakh teachers from more than 74,000 schools across 781 districts from 
all States/UTs participated in the assessment. National, State and District  level reports for 
PARAKH Rashtriya Sarvekshan 2024 providing insights on variation in learning outcomes 
across social groups for all subjects assessed are available at 
https://dashboard.parakh.ncert.gov.in/en, a dedicated dashboard designed to disseminate  
the findings of the assessment.  
  
(e): This Department is implementing an integrated centrally sponsored scheme of 
Samagra Shiksha since 2018-19.  The Samagra Shiksha scheme has been aligned with the 
aims and objective of the National Education Policy 2020 and the Right of Children to Free 
and Compulsory Education (RTE) Act, 2009. Substantial improvements have been made in 
access across levels of school education. 
 Under Samagra Shiksha, financial assistance is provided to States and UTs for 
undertaking various activities for Universalization of School Education including 
opening/strengthening of new schools upto senior secondary level, construction of school 
buildings &  additional classrooms, development/strengthening of school infrastructure in

northern border areas under Vibrant Village Programme, setting up, up -gradation and 
running of Kasturba Gandhi Balika Vidyalayas, setting up of Netaji Subhash Chandra Bose 
Avasiya Vidyalayas, construction of hostels for PVTGs under Pradhan Mantri Janjati Adivasi 
Nyaya Maha Abhiyan (PM JANMAN), construction of hostels under Dharti Aaba Janjatiya 
Gram Utkarsh Abhiyan (DA-JGUA) for unsaturated ST population, free uniforms to eligible  
children and free text books at elementary level, transport allowance and undertaking 
enrolment & retention drives. Special training for age-appropriate admission of out of school 
children and residential as well as non -residential training for older chil dren, seasonal 
hostels / residential camps, special training centres at worksites, transport/ escort facility are 
also supported to bring out of school children to the formal schooling system including 
support to complete their education through NIOS/SIOS.  Under the student -oriented 
component for the children with special needs, financial assistance is provided for 
identification and assessment of children with special needs, aids and appliances, braille 
kits and books, appropriate teaching learning materia l and stipend to girl students with 
disability etc. 
  In order to maintain appropriate Pupil -Teacher Ratio (PTR) at various levels 
of schooling, the Central Government provides financial assistance to States/UTs as per 
norms of the scheme . However, education is in the concurrent list of the Constitution and 
the opening, merger/closing of schools is within the purview of respective State Government 
and UT Administration - which are the appropriate Government under the RTE Act. 
Accordingly, matters relating to recruitment, service conditions and deployment of teachers 
fall within the administrative domain of the concerned States/UTs.
