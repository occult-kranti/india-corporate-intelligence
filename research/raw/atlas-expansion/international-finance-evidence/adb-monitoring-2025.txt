EXECUTIVE SUMMARY 
i.       The Dhaka and Western Zone Transmission Grid Expansion Projec t (DWZGEP) of a total 
investment of $730.0 million will ensure that the capacity of electricity suppl y in Dhaka and western 
zone of Bangladesh is upgraded, with improvements in the technical efficiency of the  transmission 
system. The proposed project is expected to provide support for the construction of new s ubstations 
and transmission lines and support the improvement of the existing transmission network of Power Grid. 
The DWZTGEP will also strengthen the power transmission system in south west and northwest 
Bangladesh to enhance its reliability and efficiency. The project area comprises agricultural land, where, 
due to inadequate surface water supply, cultivation mostly depends on groundwater. Henc e electricity 
demand is at its peak during the irrigation season. Furthermore, the electricity demand in the southwest 
is expected to rise in the near future with large scale industrialization expected as construction of the 
Padma Bridge has been completed. The subprojects of DWZTGEP are situated i n five administrative 
divisions and twenty-one districts of Bangladesh, comprising construction of 15  new substations, ten 
overhead transmission lines and ten Line-In Line-Out (LILO) connections of a tot al length of 394 km 
overhead lines, 11.60 km underground lines in Dhaka, and bay extensions in 9 existing grid substations. 
All the subprojects are located in agricultural lands and in settlement are as. No ecologically sensitive 
habitats or national protected areas are found in the project area. According to the Environment  
Conservation Act 1995 and Environment Conservation Rules 2023 of Bangladesh, transmiss ion lines 
more than 25 km are categorized as ‘ Red category’ projects that require an Initial Environmental 
Examination (IEE) for site clearance and environment clearance. Envi ronmental Clearance Certificate 
from Department of Environment has been issued on 28/07/2020 and valid till 28 /07/2025. According 
to the Environment Conservation Rules 2023 transmission lines (more than 25 km) are categ orized as 
‘RED Category’ project 1. The project also needs to comply with Asian Development Bank (ADB)’s 
safeguard requirements.  IEE document has been prepared to fulfill environmental ass essment 
requirements under ADB’s Safeguard Policy Statement (SPS) 2009. IEE is updated and disclosed 
whenever any unexpected impacts or design changes occur during project implementation . The IEE 
study has been done based on both primary and secondary data. A composite and interdi sciplinary 
approach has been developed for this study, based on the guidelines of DoE an d ADB’s SPS 2009. A 
scoping process was followed to identify Important Environmental (IECs) and Social Components 
(ISCs), which are likely to be impacted by interventions of the project. Impacts on ecological resources 
will be minimal and localized in all phases of the project because there is  no ecological or 
environmentally sensitive area. The Right of Way (RoW) of the transmission lines and the lands for the 
grid substations (GSs) are on low lying ground. Lands along the transmission line s are predominantly 
cultivated, at least in the dry season, and have homestead activities. Rice-based crops are grown mainly 
in the dry season along with potatoes, onions, mustard, garlic, jute and other vegetables. An area of 
79.60 acres land in 15 new substations plus one existing substation and 1 ac re of land tower footings, 
will be changed from the land type of agricultural use and fisheries. The proposed bay extensions would 
not require land acquisition as the required land extents are available within the existing GSs premises, 
except for Satkhira, which needs 3 acres for the bay extension. There will be a minor impact of noise 
for short periods during construction of foundations for the transmission line towers and also result in 
temporary increase in traffic, but work will take place during daytime and residents  close by will be 
warned of any foundation piling activity. The transmission and LILO lines wil l traverse some populated 
areas. Therefore, inspection of existing electromagnetic fields (EMF) along the selec ted routes of 
existing and new transmission lines in populated areas would be important. F or housing structures, 
clearance between the transmission line and roof tops should be 6.5m-8m and for river crossings it will 
be 14.6m-15.9m which will be ensured through using towers of appropriate extensions. The es timated 
EMF values are within the ICNIRP public exposure limits. No houses or people will be directly affected  
by the EMF of transmission and LILO lines.

1 INTRODUCTION 
1. The development policy framework of the Government of Bangladesh envisages sustain able 
development and moving towards a middle-income threshold by year 2021. The per capita income 
is rising steadily, from $1,110 in 2014 to $1,675 in 2018. The GDP growth rate in 2016 was 7.2% 
while in 2018, it further increased to 7.9%, meeting the national economic growth target of 7.0%. In 
contrast, the per capita grid electricity consumption in Bangladesh is only 336 kWh, which  is lower 
than its neighboring countries. Electricity consumption is growing rapidly at an an nual rate of over 
6%, largely due to the growth in per capita income, increasing industrialization  and the expansion 
of the agriculture and commercial sectors. Energy demand is expected to increase threefold by 
2035 as the government rolls out connections to rural households and Bangladesh continues o n a 
trajectory of strong economic growth. Electricity demand would need to increase to 33,708 MW by  
2035 to meet the desired service quality and quantity projected under future growth. In the endeavor 
to become a high-income country by 2041, development of energy and power infrastructure is vital 
for Bangladesh. 
2. The government’s strategy is to promote power sector, and it has embarked on implementation of 
a master plan to add low cost, base load power generation capacity to compensate for de clining 
domestic gas production and replacement of expensive diesel and fuel oil-based power plants. It 
provides subsidies, including lifeline consumption, to support the objective of increasing electricity 
distribution to all parts of the country. For the energy sector, national targets inc lude increased 
access to electricity, from 93% in 2018, and reaching 100% by 2021. Increase i n per capita 
consumption, from 170 kWh in 2010 to a target of 514 kWh by 2020 is anticipated.  
3. The Power Division, under the Ministry of Power, Energy and Mineral Resources (MPEMR)  leads 
the power sector while the Bangladesh Energy Regulatory Commission (BERC) is the regulato ry 
body which regulates electricity, gas, and petroleum sectors. The electricity  sector is unbundled 
into generation, transmission, and distribution segments. Backbone transmission li nes and 
substation network in the country are operated by the Power Grid Company of Bangladesh Limited 
(Power Grid). As of December 2018, Power Grid network comprised transmission lines operated at 
400 kV, 230 kV and 132 kV voltage levels with a total length of 11,396 circuit k m and 125 132/33 
kV substations with a cumulative capacity of 20,211 MVA. Development of t ransmission and 
distribution network in line with generation has been identified as a key element of the power sector 
strategy in the ‘Bangladesh Seventh Five Year Plan FY 2016 – FY 2020’. Development of an inter-
region transmission network is required due to the imbalance in concentr ation of generation and 
demand, while local network expansions are required to cater to the increasing demand at new load 
centers. Due to the growing electricity demand, a considerable number of grid substations and 
transmission lines have already been overloaded. Therefore, urgent upgrades and expansions are 
required by Power Grid to supply power to the key economic corridors in the southern and western 
regions including the greater Dhaka area.  
4. The Dhaka and Western Zone Transmission Grid Expansion Project (DWZTGE P) focuses on 
expanding the local transmission network to deliver electricity to new and expanding  load centers 
while meeting the network operating criteria.  
a) Component 1: Transmission system in Greater Dhaka expanded. The project will construct 
and commission substations with a total capacity of 4,450 MVA and 40 km of transmission lines 
in Greater Dhaka. 
b) Component 2: Transmission system in Western Zone expanded. The project will construct 
and commission substations with a total capacity of 3,070 MVA and 368 km of transmis sion 
lines in the western zone. 
c) Component 3: Institutional Capacity of Power Grid strengthened. The project will enhance 
capacity by establishing a Drone Inspection Center within the operation and mainten ance 
department of Power Grid with some gender equality elements.

2 COMPLIANCE TO NATIONAL REGULATIONS 
7. The Environment Conservation Act of 1995 establishes the requirement for an environ mental 
assessment in Bangladesh. Any proposed "industrial unit or project" must obtain prior approval from 
the Department of Environment (DoE). The act has classified projects to be assessed by the DoE 
in four categories (Green, Yellow, Orange and Red). Electrical infrastructure proj ects involving 
transmission lines longer than 25 km are categorized as ‘Red’ category, which triggers an automatic 
requirement for an IEE. Environmental Clearance Certificate from Department of Environme nt has 
been issued on 28/07/2020 and was valid till 28/07/2025.  
8. Land acquisition for substations is done in accordance with the Acquisition  and Requisition of 
Immovable Property Act, 2017 according to which the DC of the respective district determines  the 
compensation amount for lands to be acquired as well as trees, crops, vegetation etc. situated in 
that land. The Project office deposits the money to the DC who is authorized by law to distribute 
the compensation amount among the affected people. Under the DWZTGE Project, 79.6 acr es of 
land in 15 places has been acquired and compensation amount for all of the a cquired sites as 
determined by the DC has been deposited by the project office. In all cases, all t he people 
concerned have been notified properly as per the requirements of the law and the project office has 
deposited the compensation amount as determined by the DC.

2.3 Grievance Redress Mechanism 
21. GRM is a locally based, project-specific way to deal with and resolve  complaints and grievances 
faster and thus enhance project performance standards in terms of environmental, social an d 
resettlement management. The fundamental objectives of the GRM, implemented through a 
Grievance Redress Committee (GRC) are to resolve any environmental and resettlement related 
grievances in consultation with the aggrieved party to facilitate smooth impl ementation of 
environmental and resettlement plans and establish accountability of the affected people . 
22. A GRC (Annexure-7) is formed to receive and resolve complaints as well as griev ances from the 
aggrieved people from the local stakeholders including the Project-affected peopl e. Based on 
consensus, the GRC will help to resolve issues/conflicts amicably and quickly, saving the aggrieved 
persons from having to resort to expensive, time-consuming legal actions. The procedure will, 
however, not prevent a person’s right to go to the courts of law.  
23. The established GRC will function throughout the life cycle of the project implementation and Power 
Grid will ensure its accessibility to affected parties and transparency and effic iency in grievance 
resolution. However, it will not deal with or interfere with any matters which are already placed 
before the country’s courts of law. The Project will provide publicity for the GRC established using 
a variety of media such as brochures and leaflets printed in Bengali and through commu nity level 
awareness raising programs.

2.5 Tree Plantation Status 
29. In the original IEE, it was highlighted that the IEE (Table 5-12) had estimate d the removal of 22,378 trees for the construction of new and LILO transmission 
lines based on a 2018 inventory. However, representatives from the PIU, ESU, and EQMS unanimously agreed that the actual number of trees to be removed 
will be significantly lower due to several key factors. These include the reduction in project scope under Packages 1 and 2 (with 18 km of 400 kV and 4.5 km 
of 230 kV lines descoped), the majority of transmission line alignments pass ing through paddy fields, and changes in ground conditions since 2018. 
Additionally, the IEE had considered all trees above 5 meters for removal, whereas the Electricity Rules 2020 require clearance of only 4.6 m for 230 kV and 
3.8 m for 132 kV lines, further reducing the need for tree removal. 
30. In Package 3, during the reporting period about 500 trees have been removed as of June 2025. Although, some voluntary tree plantation initiated in Package 
3 during previous reporting semesters which are not a part of the tree plantat ion plan. Tree plantation for package 7 will be initiated after the construct ion of 
the substations. A comprehensive Tree Plantation Plan for Package 3 has been prepared and submitted to ESU for review and Package 2, 4, 5,6 and 7 will 
be incorporated after receiving the total inventory of tree species cut down from EPC contractor.

analysis methods, such as Particulates 90° Infrared Light Scattering for particulat e matters (SPM, 
PM10, PM2.5), electrochemical sensors for toxic gases (SO2, NO2, CO, O3, SOx and NOx) an d

Atomic Absorption Spectroscopy (AAS) for Lead (Pb) are integrated into the dev ice. Monitoring 
photographs and lab sheets are attached in Annexure 32 and Annexure 33. 
34. For Package 2, air quality monitoring was carried out at three locations (AQ1 –AQ3) along the 
Rampura–Bashundhara underground cable corridor. The recorded PM10 concentrations ranged 
from 91.5 to 102.7 µg/m³, and PM2.5 values ranged from 41.3 to 52.4 µg/m³. These val ues were 
within the national standards of Bangladesh (150 µg/m³ for PM10 and 65 µg/m³ for PM2.5 ), but 
exceeded the IFC/World Bank guideline limits, which are 50 µg/m³ for PM10 and 25 µg/m³ for 
PM2.5. All other parameters SO₂, NO₂, CO, and O₃ were well below both national and international 
standards. These results suggest that while the ambient air complies with local  regulations, there 
are elevated fine particulate levels that could impact workers and public health, l ikely caused by 
construction dust and local vehicular activities. 
35. For Package 4, air quality was extensively monitored at twenty-five locations ( AQ4–AQ28) across 
multiple transmission line alignments, including Gopalganj –Shibchar, Purba Sadipur –Domar, 
Niamatpur–Patnitala, Domar–Hatibandha, and Bagerhat –Pirojpur–Bhandaria. Across these sites, 
PM10 concentrations ranged from approximately 65 to 108 µg/m³, and PM2.5 levels varied between 
30 and 57 µg/m³. While all results complied with the Bangladesh national ambient air quality 
standards, a significant number of locations exceeded IFC recommended limits for both PM10 and 
PM2.5, especially in semi-urban and roadside settings. On the other hand, gaseous pollutants such 
as SO₂ and NO₂ remained low, typically under 20 µg/m³, and CO and O₃ concentrations were also 
minimal. These findings indicate that dust from construction, vehicle movement on unpaved roads, 
and dry soil conditions are the main contributors to elevated particulate levels, although no serious 
industrial pollution is present. 
36. In Package 5, air quality was assessed at four locations (AQ29 –AQ32) near the Kaliganj 400/230 
kV GIS substation. The monitoring results showed that PM10 ranged from 72 to 96 µg/m³, and 
PM2.5 ranged from 38 to 50 µg/m³. These values are within the national standards, but once again, 
PM2.5 exceeded the IFC guideline limit at all locations. All other parameters, i ncluding SO₂, NO₂, 
CO, and O₃, remained well within acceptable thresholds. The elevated particulate levels are l ikely 
due to site preparation and construction activities, particularly the movement of machinery and dry 
material handling. Despite this, the overall air quality does not pose immediate environmental health 
concerns under local standards, although mitigation measures such as water sprinkling and dust 
barriers are advisable. 
37. For Package 6, four monitoring points (AQ33 –AQ36) were established around the Purbachal 
400/230/132 kV substation site. PM10 values at these locations ranged between 84 and 98 µg/m³, 
while PM2.5 values were between 42 and 51 µg/m³, exceeding IFC but staying well below national 
limits. Other pollutant levels such as SO₂ (below 12 µg/m³), NO₂ (under 25 µg/m³), and CO and O₃ 
also remained comfortably within acceptable levels. These results are consiste nt with early-phase 
construction activities in a newly urbanizing area like Purbachal, where dust generation is expected. 
The findings call for continued monitoring and dust control practices, particul arly to reduce the 
health risks associated with prolonged PM2.5 exposure. 
38. Lastly, Package 7 covered air monitoring at five locations (AQ37 –AQ41) in substation areas at 
Rupsha, Phultola, Manirampur, and Meherpur. All recorded values for PM10, PM2.5, and other key 
pollutants were within national standards. PM2.5 and PM10 remained below 40 µg/m³ and 90 µg/m³ 
respectively, and gaseous pollutants such as SO ₂, NO ₂, and CO were found at very low 
concentrations. This indicates that the construction activities in these locations have not significantly 
deteriorated air quality, and the surroundings being more rural or vegetated help with natural 
dispersion of dust and pollutants. The monitoring confirms an overall good air quality condition in 
Package 7 sites with no exceedance of concern under either DoE or IFC guidelines.

39. This section identifies the status of the ambient noise level in Package 2, 4, 5,  6 and 7 project

been recorded in form of A-weighted equivalent continuous sound pressure level (LAeq) values with 
the use of A-weighting filters in the noise measuring instrument.

49. To determine the quality of surface water, a sample was collected from the near est water body of

50. The samples have been analyzed for parameters covering bacteriological and physi o-chemical 
characteristics which include certain heavy metals and trace elements. Sampl es were collected 
from the center of the water body approximately 0.5 m below the water surface, following standard 
procedures for container preparation, and sample collection, storage and preservation. During 
surface water collection USEPA surface water sampling guideline 
(https://www.epa.gov/quality/surface-water-sampling) was followed.

analysis of parameters. After analysis, surface water results have been compared with the national 
standards [ECR-2023, Schedule-2A].

Environment Conservation Rules (ECR) 2023-Schedule 2(A) whereas the groundwater was 
compared with the Drinking Water Standard ECR 2023 Schedule-2(B). The standa rds have been

55. For Package 2, surface water samples were collected from three locations (SW1 –SW3) along the 
Rampura–Bashundhara underground cable route. The analysis revealed exceedances in several  
organic pollution indicators, particularly Biological Oxygen Demand (BOD)  and Chemical Oxygen 
Demand (COD). BOD values ranged from 7.2 to 18.3 mg/L, and COD values reached as high  as 
77 mg/L, both of which exceed the ECR 2023 Schedule 2(A) standard limits of 6 mg/L for BOD and 
50 mg/L for COD. Additionally, Dissolved Oxygen (DO) levels at SW2 dropped to 4.6 mg/L, falling 
below the acceptable minimum of 5.0 mg/L, indicating the presence of organic matter like ly due to 
upstream domestic or industrial discharges. Other parameters such as pH, temperature, turbidity , 
and heavy metals remained within permissible limits, but the organic load sugg ests localized 
pollution that may require mitigation if water use for domestic or agricultural purpos es is planned 
nearby. 
56. In Package 4, surface water quality monitoring was conducted at five transmission line corridor sites 
(SW4–SW8), covering areas such as Gopalganj –Shibchar, Domar –Hatibandha, and Bagerhat –
Bhandaria. The results indicated full compliance with both national and IFC water quality standards. 
BOD values were all below 4.2 mg/L, COD values remained under 35 mg/L, and DO concentrations 
were consistently above 6.1 mg/L, confirming good oxygenation. Additionally, no exc eedances 
were found for other critical parameters such as turbidity, total coliform, TDS, or pH. The consistent 
quality across all sites suggests that water bodies in these zones are relat ively unpolluted and 
unaffected by the construction work taking place, highlighting effective environmental  safeguards 
or naturally resilient water systems. 
57. For Package 5, surface water samples from the Kaliganj substation area (SW9–SW13) also showed 
mostly compliant results, with parameters such as pH, temperature, DO, and turbidity all  falling 
within standard thresholds. BOD values were slightly elevated, with the highest readi ng at SW11 
being 6.8 mg/L, just above the standard limit of 6 mg/L. COD levels remained below 45  mg/L, and 
DO values were consistently over 5.5 mg/L. While this suggests minor organic en richment in 
isolated spots, the overall surface water quality is considered satisfactory and does no t pose 
immediate environmental or health concerns. 
58. Similarly, for Package 6, surface water from five locations near the Purbachal substation  (SW14–
SW18) indicated overall acceptable water quality, with most parameters within ECR 20 23 limits. 
BOD ranged from 3.8 to 6.2 mg/L, and COD varied between 29 and 44 mg/L. Only one s ample 
(SW16) had a BOD value slightly over the threshold, recorded at 6.2 mg/L, which may reflect 
localized contamination from nearby activities but is not indicative of widespre ad water quality 
degradation. DO values ranged from 5.4 to 6.9 mg/L, staying well within safe limits for aquatic  life. 
59. Lastly, in Package 7, surface water quality at five substation locations (S W19–SW23) covering 
Rupsha, Phultola, Manirampur, and Meherpur, was found to be fully compliant with natio nal 
standards. DO levels were all above 6.0 mg/L, and BOD and COD levels remained low (below 4.5  
mg/L and 35 mg/L respectively). Parameters such as pH, turbidity, and microbial indicators like 
coliform counts were also within acceptable limits. This indicates no significa nt impact from 
construction activities on the water bodies adjacent to these sites and reflects eff ective 
implementation of water pollution control measures.

3.3.3.2 Soil Quality Analysis Results 
70. Analysis results of Soil Quality are represented in Table 3-9 where the values are compared with

71. For Package 2, soil quality assessment was conducted at three sampling locati ons (SQ1 –SQ3) 
along the Rampura –Bashundhara 132kV underground cable alignment. Parameters measured 
included pH, electrical conductivity (EC), organic matter content, and concentrations of heavy 
metals such as lead (Pb), cadmium (Cd), chromium (Cr), and copper (Cu). All results were 
compared against the Dutch Soil Standards due to the absence of national benchmarks in  
Bangladesh. The pH values ranged from 6.3 to 6.7, indicating slightly acidic to neutral soils. Organic 
matter content was between 1.1% and 1.4%, which is typical for semi-urban soils. Heavy metal 
concentrations were all well below the Dutch intervention values—for instance, lead content ranged 
from 5.3 to 6.8 mg/kg against a Dutch threshold of 100 mg/kg. This confirms that t he soil in the 
project area is uncontaminated and safe for both human health and ecological rec eptors, showing 
no sign of pollution from construction or historical land use.

4.10 Good Practices During January- June 2025 
81. During the reporting period, several noteworthy, good practices were observed across the proj ect 
sites that contributed to enhanced environmental, health, and safety (EHS) performance and 
community engagement. These practices reflect the project's commitment to sustainable and 
responsible implementation. 
82. Multiple on-site and safety training sessions were conducted for operational s taff to improve 
awareness and compliance with safety procedures. Workers received structured guidance on  
hazard prevention, proper PPE use, and emergency response. Additionally, all workers were 
provided with ID cards, improving site security and worker accountability. The practice of 
appreciating field staff for adhering to safety protocols further encouraged a culture of safety  
consciousness. 
83. A series of medical camps and awareness sessions were organized focusing on HIV, STI, and STD 
prevention, which also provided basic health checkups to workers and surrounding communi ty 
members. These efforts promoted public health, especially among the labor force and local 
residents in remote areas. 
84. During the stringing of transmission lines, particularly under Packages 3 and 4, contractors ensured 
that no trees were cut down and no agricultural land was disrupted, demonstrating an eff ort to 
preserve both ecological integrity and livelihoods. In addition, all construction ma terials were 
properly covered to prevent dust emissions, and debris was promptly removed after work 
completion, allowing farmers to resume cultivation around tower bases without delay. 
85. A noteworthy initiative was observed where local workers commuted to sites using bic ycles, 
promoting a sustainable and cost- effective mode of transport. This reduced the project’s carbon 
footprint while also supporting healthy routines for laborers. 
86. To enhance aesthetics and environmental quality, tree plantation activities  were carried out at 
selected sites. Furthermore, project information boards were installed at construction zones to 
ensure transparency, stakeholder awareness, and community inclusion. 
87. A series of training sessions and toolbox meetings were conducted across various packages during 
this period. Topics included environmental awareness, safe equipment handling, fi re safety, first 
aid response, and proper use of PPE. Records show that over 100 workers and site staff r eceived 
training in at least one of these topics between January and June 2025. Special emp hasis was 
placed on raising awareness about dust control, noise mitigation, and emergency  preparedness, 
with many sites organizing weekly safety briefings. Additionally, contractor safety offi cers ensured 
new laborers received orientation before commencing work. All the above-mentioned  good 
practices were presented in Annexure 41.

5 CONCLUSION 
88. The environmental monitoring and implementation review conducted for the period of January to 
June 2025 under the Dhaka and Western Zone Transmission Grid Expansion Project indicates that 
overall compliance with the Environmental Management Plan (EMP) has been satisfac tory across 
all active packages. Monitoring results for air, noise, surface water, groundwater, and soil quality 
were largely within national standards, with some minor exceedances noted particularly in 
particulate matter (PM2.5 and PM10) levels against IFC guidelines. These findings underscore the 
need for continued dust control and mitigation measures in active construction zones. 
89. Importantly, no critical environmental non-compliances or irreversible im pacts were observed 
during this period. Construction activities were executed with careful considerat ion of surrounding 
communities, ecological features, and agricultural lands. The project demonstrated s everal
