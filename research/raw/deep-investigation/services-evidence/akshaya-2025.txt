            A tapestry is more than a pattern;
            it is a story woven from countless
            individual threads. Each thread,
            distinct in colour and texture,
            intertwining to create something
            larger, beautiful and enduring.

            That’s precisely what Akshaya
            Patra represents: a living tapestry
            of compassion and collaboration
            spread across India’s vast and varied
            landscape. Every region adds its
            own hue… its children, communities,
            kitchen staff, volunteers, donors and
            governments, woven together by a
            shared vision: No child in India shall
            be deprived of education because of
            hunger.

            ‘Tapestry’ celebrates this
            intertwining, the way our work
            threads together 16 states and 3
            union territories, to serve nutritious
            mid-day meals to children in
            government schools every day,
            anchoring health and hope.

             A Story of
Compassion
 One day, while looking out of a window in Mayapur, a
  small hamlet near Calcutta, Srila Prabhupada saw a
 group of children fighting with stray dogs over scraps
 of food. Deeply moved by the incident, he directed his
followers to ensure that no one within a ten-mile radius
    of his centre goes hungry. His resolve inspired the
genesis of The Akshaya Patra Foundation and its vision,
     ‘No child in India shall be deprived of education
                    because of hunger.’




                His Divine Grace
A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada

     Sustainable Practices

     Sustainability is a core pillar of Akshaya Patra’s kitchen operations. Several kitchens are
     equipped with rooftop solar panels, EV vehicles, biomass briquettes made from agricultural
     waste, biogas plants and smokeless stoves. Kitchens also feature energy-efficient boilers,
     RO (Reverse Osmosis) water systems and Effluent Treatment Plants (ETPs) to ensure
     responsible water usage. By integrating these green technologies and practices, Akshaya
     Patra not only reduces its carbon footprint but also reinforces the long-term scalability
     and environmental responsibility of its feeding programmes.

      Collective action has been and will always be the backbone of The Akshaya
      Patra Foundation’s efforts to support children’s health and education. If
      Akshaya Patra serves 2.25 children across 16 states and 3 union territories
      today, it is because of the collective efforts of all its stakeholders. These include
      the Central Government, State Government, UT Administrations, corporate
      partners, individual donors, knowledge partners, volunteers, well-wishers, school
      authorities and the communities it serves.

      From the mountains of Uttarakhand to the coastlines
      of Puducherry, across 16 states and 3 union territories,
      Akshaya Patra’s stakeholders mirror the diversity of the
      geography and demography it serves.

      The selfless dedication of its stakeholders—people from all walks of life—has
      helped the Foundation utilise resources and skills that foster the dreams of over
      22.5 lakhs beneficiaries and provide food assistance to vulnerable communities in
      difficult times.

      As the implementing partner of the PM POSHAN initiative (earlier known as the
      Mid-Day Meal Scheme), Akshaya Patra is grateful to its policy and grantmakers—
      the Central Government, State Governments and UT Administrations—for their
      support. The Foundation is also grateful to its corporate partners, individual
      contributors, bankers, professionals, employees, vendors, regulators and all those
      who are involved in its feeding endeavour and play a crucial role in sustaining its
      operations.

      The generosity of these stakeholders—or the changemakers as Akshaya Patra
      likes to call them for they have continuously championed the growth of its
      cause—empowers it to focus on enhancing the effectiveness of welfare initiatives
      it implements. The Foundation is grateful to all these stakeholders for the vital role
      that they have played in maximising the impact of its mission to serve children
      and the larger society for a better tomorrow.

      Morning Nutrition
      Programme-
      A Bright Start to the Day


         When children come to school without having a breakfast, it leads to
         a critical nutritional gap during the morning hours, precisely the time
         when they need to focus, absorb knowledge and engage in cognitive
         tasks in the classroom. Recognising this vital need, The Akshaya Patra
         Foundation launched the Morning Nutrition Programme to provide
         children a ready-to-eat, nutritious breakfast snack at the beginning of
         the school day.

         School-going children, particularly those from low-income households,
         often arrive at school without having any food in the morning. This
         compromises their ability to concentrate in class, participate in learning
         activities and retain information, which not only affects their academic
         performance but also has a cascading effect on their long-term
         educational trajectory and personal development. The Morning Nutrition
         Programme seeks to bridge this gap by ensuring that children are
         nourished and energised before they begin their lessons, thus creating
         optimal conditions for effective learning.




                                                                                      Studies have consistently highlighted the
                                                                                      importance of breakfast in enhancing cognitive
                                                                                      function. Even the National Education Policy
                                                                                      (NEP) 2020 notes that the morning hours after a
                                                                                      nutritious breakfast are particularly productive for
                                                                                      learning subjects that demand higher cognitive
                                                                                      engagement. It also recommends the provision
                                                                                      of simple yet nutritious morning meals, such as
                                                                                      groundnuts, chana with jaggery or local fruits,
                                                                                      especially in situations where hot meals are not
                                                                                      feasible. The PM POSHAN initiative (the Mid-Day
                                                                                      Meal Scheme) has long served as a cornerstone
                                                                                      in the fight against classroom hunger and child
                                                                                      malnutrition. A strategically designed morning
                                                                                      nutrition component can complement the school
                                                                                      lunch programme by building on the same.
