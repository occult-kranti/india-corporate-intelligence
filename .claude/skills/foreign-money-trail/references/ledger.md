# Foreign-money fleets — the ledger: counting rules, controls, base rates, voids, gaps, disagreements

*Three fleets, written 2026-09-26: `research/raw/finance/` (external finance — lenders, loans, conditions, contracts), `research/raw/ngo/` (FCRA receipts, actions, donors, political trusts, the welfare join) and `research/raw/capital/` (holders, mandates and ventures, rules and regulators, narratives). Sections 1–4 are hand-written from `RECONCILIATION.json`, `controls.json`, `coverage.json`, `holders-aggregates.json` and the three `AUDIT.json` files and cite the record each rule comes from. Sections 5–8 are generated verbatim from the research files' `baseRates`, `voids`, `gaps` and `symmetryCheck` arrays by the generator named at the foot of `narratives.md`; do not edit a figure there — fix the file and regenerate. Section 9 lists where the files disagree. Section 10 is the register of claims the cross-examiners refuted, grouped by failure mode, so the next pass does not repeat them.*

## 1. Counting — which populations are never summed

**Loans: a census and a researched sample, never one total.** `research/raw/finance/worldbank-projects.json` is fetcher output (`scripts/finance/fetch-worldbank.mjs`, World Bank Projects API v3, 1,117 India projects 1949–2027; 742 Closed, 273 Dropped): one `loan` claim per project per lender — the IBRD and IDA legs separately — 849 claims, amounts in ₹ crore at the Bank's `PA.NUS.FCRF` average rate for the approval year. The other finance files (`worldbank.json`, `adb-aiib.json`, `bilateral-china.json`, `literature.json`, `debt-imf-people.json`) hold 98 loans researched by hand at the rate the source printed (the Program Document's dated rate, or an approximate market rate flagged in `d`). The two populations use different exchange-rate bases, different borrower rules (504 of 849 census legs have no borrower in the API and default to `min:ministry-of-finance`, saying so in `d`) and different scope, so `docs/design/FINANCE_PAGE.md` D4/D5 forbids summing them or drawing them in one Sankey. The only ₹ summation on the page is over census legs marked countable.

**Counting marks** (`docs/research/FLEET_CONTRACT.md` → Phase G → "Finance — `projectId` and the counting marks"; owner `research/raw/finance/RECONCILIATION.json` → `countedAs`, `notCountable`; writer `scripts/finance/mark-loans.mjs --write`):

| mark | meaning | instances on 2026-09-26 |
|---|---|---|
| `countable: false` + `countedAs: "<claim id>"` + `notCountableReason` | a researched record that repeats a loan another record counts; `countedAs` names a countable loan edge from the same lender (`s`) on the same `projectId` | 12 researched World Bank records repeat a census project by project id and lender (e.g. `worldbank:c001` → `worldbank:c0754`, P173943 IBRD leg; `worldbank:c002` → `c0755`, the IDA leg); 3 repeat a researched loan and say so (AIIB CARES and AIIB health in `literature.json` → `adb-aiib.json`; MAHSR tranche V in `literature.json` → `bilateral-china.json`) |
| `countable: false` + `notCountableReason`, class `facility-envelope`, with `tranches[]` | a multitranche facility ceiling; its recorded tranches are the loans | `adb-aiib:c005` (MFF 0107-IND, US$1,049m → tranches c006, c007, c008); `adb-aiib:c012` (MFF 0115-IND, US$780m → c013, c014) |
| class `portfolio-aggregate` | a lender's portfolio summed — many loans, not one | `adb-aiib:c049` (ADB's 75 active sovereign loans, US$15,485m at 2022-12-31) |
| class `non-binding-mou` | a framework or pledge MoU, not a loan agreement | `bilateral-china:c033` and the other AidData pledge rows (the US$12bn Reliance Power MoUs, the US$3bn Ming Yang framework, the SBI/ICICI credit lines — 24 of 113 India rows are pledges/MoUs/vague that AidData itself excludes from aggregates) |

A duplicate is never `supersededBy` and never deleted: supersession is a dated correction, not a count. Held for review, unmarked, because neither record says it is the other: NDB Chennai (`adb-aiib:c017` US$347m / `bilateral-china:c022` US$346.72m), NDB Delhi–Meerut RRTS (`adb-aiib:c009` US$500m / `bilateral-china:c021` US$418m), NDB Mumbai metro (`adb-aiib:c011` US$260m / `bilateral-china:c020` US$241m), the 1991 IMF facility (`literature:c007` / `debt:c025`).

**`projectId`** (`P` + six digits) goes on a `loan` or `award` claim only where the claim's own text names it; `mark-loans.mjs` writes it by rule (the one distinct token in `lab`, else in `d`; two tokens → not set, listed for review). `contracts.json` carries it on 26 awards; `worldbank.json` on 11 loans.

**FCRA receipts: three anchors, one state-wise table.** National totals are documented only where a Parliament answer was opened — FY2011-12 ₹11,548 cr (RS Starred *199, 18.12.2013, R.P.N. Singh: 41,844 registrants, 16,756 non-filers), FY2019-20 ₹16,359.48 cr, FY2020-21 ₹17,166.34 cr and FY2021-22 ₹22,121.75 cr (the sums of the 34 state rows of RS Unstarred Q.3253, 29.03.2023, Nityanand Rai — `fcra-receipts.json → fcByState`, 102 rows, Ladakh, Lakshadweep and Daman & Diu absent from the answer, not zero) — and FY2024-25 ₹22,974 cr (MHA to the JPC on the 2026 Bill, via PTI/ThePrint). FY2015-16 ₹17,832 cr and FY2016-17 ₹15,355 cr are press-carried MHA statistics (reported tier). FY2014-15 ₹22,136.77 cr (`fcra-receipts:c003`) is the Annexure-III sum for the 3,068 associations that each received over ₹1 crore, **not** the national total — the AUDIT re-scoped the claim. Never place the West Bengal rows (₹727.16 / 798.18 / 905.50 cr in RS USQ 1662 of 15.03.2023; the file's copy reads 906.12 / 798.18 / 727.20 from Q.3253's annexure — the two answers differ in the FY2019-20 and FY2021-22 rows, and both are on record) against a national figure, and never quote them *as* the national figure: that is the debunked "₹700–900 crore a year" narrative.

**FCRA enforcement: the numerator is mostly compliance.** 21,983 cumulative cancellations (MHA to JPC, 2026), 91.3% for non-filing of annual returns; 20,600 by 2021-02 (LS reply 2021-02-10); 5,933 of 22,762 registrations ceased on 2022-01-01, of which 5,789 of 5,968 were non-applications; 4,138 cancellations in calendar 2012 under the UPA for non-filing (RS USQ 320, 20 Jul 2016 — the AUDIT corrected the file's 4,141 and the "2012-13" window). The 22 named case files in `fcra-actions.json` are ~0.1% of the portal's 20,701 cancellations (2024-05). Zero of the 22 has a final court finding that the association violated the Act in an opened source.

**Holders: the 1% naming line, shares outstanding, and a lower bound.** A SEBI Reg. 31(1)(b) shareholding pattern names a public shareholder only at ≥1% of equity, per fund vehicle, not per manager (`cap:sebi-shp-1pct-threshold`, sourced from the filings' own footnote — the Nov 2015 circular URL guessed at sebi.gov.in returned 404). `holders.json` opened five primary filings (Reliance Industries 30.06.2026, HDFC Bank 30.06.2026, ITC 31.12.2025, Bharti Airtel 31.12.2025, SBI 31.03.2025) and ten aggregator category totals; the nine batch files took the read to 30 primary / 20 aggregator of the 50 constituents (`coverage.json`, keyed to `research/raw/indices.json` nifty50). `holders-aggregates.json` sums eight US-listed iShares ETFs' daily holdings CSVs (INDA, IEMG, EEM, ACWI, INDY, IXUS, ACWX, EMXC, as of 2026-09-24) per company and divides by **shares outstanding** (the primary filing's total for RIL, HDFC Bank, ITC, Bharti Airtel; screener.in equity capital ÷ face value for ICICI Bank, Infosys, TCS, L&T, Axis Bank, SBI). That is a **lower bound** — Irish UCITS ETFs, BlackRock Global Funds sub-funds, institutional mandates and the ADR channel (HDFC Bank 13.3% of shares underlie depositary receipts; RIL 1.70%) are not summed — and the denominator is the whole share capital, not the free float: the plan spoke of "NIFTY 50 free float", the fleet's ledger did not compute one, and no file should call it that. Result: the eight-fund BlackRock aggregate is ≥1% in 7 of the 10 largest constituents while no single iShares line reaches 1% in any (0 of 10) — the invisibility is a registration artefact, not an absence; it is a median ≈5% of each company's total FII/FPI holding; LIC's single line exceeds it in 4 of 4 companies where LIC is named (RIL 6.88 vs 1.01; HDFC Bank 4.77 vs 1.75; ITC 15.69 vs 0.59; SBI 9.38 vs 0.48). Vanguard's fund-level sum was not computable (Vanguard sites are JS shells; SEC N-PORT XML on www.sec.gov/Archives is 403) and is recorded as the named-line lower bound (2.44% of HDFC Bank via two funds). Claims that recorded an *absence* as an `own` edge were killed and moved to `voids` (`holders-b3:c012–c014`, `holders-b9:c008`, `holders-aggregates:c011`).

**External debt: the World Bank series stands in for DEA.** `dea.gov.in` (connection failure) and `indiabudget.gov.in` (403) — the two homes of "India's External Debt: A Status Report" — were unreachable, so `debt-imf-people.json` records the stock from World Bank `DT.DOD.DECT.CD` (US$123.6bn 2004 → 457.5bn 2014 → 716.5bn 2024, current US$, calendar years), IBRD+IDA debt `DT.DOD.MWBG.CD` (US$39.28bn of 647.56bn in 2023, ≈6.1%) and PPG debt `DT.DOD.DPPG.CD` (US$214.9bn, ≈33.2%). The AUDIT (literature narrative:2) later opened the DEA Status Reports: concessional share 28.4% (2006) → 10.5% (2014) → 6.9% (2025), external debt US$440.6bn → 736.3bn, no break at 2014. Any loan figure is read against this stock before "trap" or "capture" is written; the CAGR comparison `debt:c026` (≈14.0%/yr UPA decade vs ≈4.6%/yr NDA decade) survives only with the AUDIT's corrected innocent reading — six of seven peer emerging markets (BRA, IDN, MEX, TUR, ZAF, THA; not PHL) show the same deceleration — not the file's "larger base compounds slower" arithmetic, which the AUDIT found false.

## 3. Coverage — what was read, by name

`research/raw/capital/coverage.json`: 30 of 50 NIFTY 50 constituents read from a primary named-holder table (SEBI Reg. 31 Table II/III on the company's own site, dated), 20 from aggregator category totals only (screener.in, trendlyne), 0 not read. A company with a primary read and no claim is "read, not named"; with an aggregator read it is "not read by name" — no holder can be named *or ruled out* from an aggregator. Stale quarters are recorded, not hidden (SBI and UltraTech 31.03.2025; Axis Bank 30.06.2025; Cipla, Eternal, Kotak, NTPC, Power Grid 30.09.2025); every `supersededBy` is null because no later filing was opened.

`research/raw/finance/contracts.json`: 370 World Bank contract-award notices across 15 projects (the procurement-notices API caps a project feed at 200 rows — PMGSY P124639 returned only its Meghalaya SRRDA packages), of which 55 (2011–2016 ICB format) publish evaluated bidders and 315 (post-2016 STEP format) name the awardee only; the World Bank sanctions list served 1,520 rows, 54 India-country. `worldbank.json`: 12 operations in depth (5 Program Documents read: P173943, P174027, P174292, P181032, Kerala DPO1, Gujarat GOAL). `bilateral-china.json`: 113 India rows of AidData GCDF 3.0 (89 recommended for aggregates). `adb-aiib.json`: ~12 ADB loans/tranches opened, the full AIIB India data file (39 sovereign approvals, 26 nonsovereign entries), 10 AIIB detail pages.

The NDA-era record adds scale (20,600 cancellations by 2021, mostly non-filing; 5,933 cessations on one day in 2022), the 2020 statute, and a longer list of named critics — but the same lens on non-critics produces Missionaries of Charity (refused, restored), World Vision (₹170 crore), CASA, EFI, Compassion (US$45m/yr), CARE India (₹377.5 crore), Bal Raksha Bharat (₹163 crore), VHAI, Hemophilia Federation, Institute of Economic Growth. Government-aligned associations (VIF, India Foundation, Ekal/Vidya Bharati affiliates) were not examined in this file — they belong to the receipts agent; the absence of any recorded enforcement against them is a question for that file, not a finding of this one. Courts: under both governments the High Courts struck MHA on procedure (2013, 2014, 2015); after the 2020 Amendment they deferred (CHRI 2022; Noel Harper 2022).

**fcra-receipts** — Government-critical (Oxfam India, CPR, CARE) and non-critical/aligned (World Vision India, Compassion partners, Believers/Ayana, RDT) recipients were recorded with the same fields: receipts where found, enforcement event, stated ground, response. Result: the largest enforcement events by money fell on service charities that do not criticise the government (Compassion ~₹325 cr/yr; Ayana ₹826 cr; WVI ₹170–319 cr; RDT ₹209 cr), while the critic cases (Oxfam, CPR) carry stated grounds tied to content — the lens produces an alarming picture on BOTH sides, which is a finding about the regime's breadth rather than about targeting. UPA-era control: the Dec 2013 Parliament answer shows the same ministry, same statute, 41,844 registrants with 40% non-filers and ₹11,548 cr receipts — the non-filing problem that drove 91% of later cancellations predates 2014. Aligned-side receipts (Ekal, Sewa, VIF, India Foundation) could not be found at all, so the symmetry on receipts is incomplete and recorded as a void. Domestic control: Azim Premji Foundation alone disburses ₹1,639 cr/yr (7% of all FC), and Bain/Dasra put foreign private giving (~₹23,000 cr) at ~19% of private philanthropy and ~1% of total social-sector spend — foreign money is a minor stream in Indian welfare finance.

**political-trusts** — Applying the same lens (documented FCRA enforcement action, or a documented foreign-money controversy, tied to a national party's central leadership or its aligned think tanks) produced a strikingly asymmetric result in what we could actually find and verify this session — but the asymmetry is partly an artifact of press coverage and partly an artifact of this session's access limits, and one important sub-question is symmetric on the primary evidence we DID verify. On the Congress side: RGF and RGCT were both investigated from 2020 and had their FCRA registrations cancelled in Oct 2022 on stated grounds including a Chinese-embassy donation and a Zakir Naik/IRF donation (detailed in this directory's fcra-actions.json); RGICS and the Indira Gandhi Memorial Trust were named in the same 2020 probe. On the BJP/RSS side, applying the identical search — did VIF, India Foundation, Prajna Pravah or OFBJP-USA face any documented FCRA cancellation, suspension or comparable enforcement action — found NOTHING in any source we could open this session (see voids). That zero could mean these organisations are in fact compliant and never triggered scrutiny, or it could mean our access to the Indian press archive was badly degraded this session (see gaps: most major outlets' search functions returned 403s, JS-only shells, or unrelated fallback content for every guessed URL) — we cannot distinguish these two explanations from what we found, and it would be wrong to present the zero as proof of a double standard. Where we DID verify a genuinely symmetric documented fact — the 2014 Delhi High Court's finding that BOTH BJP and Congress received unlawful foreign-source donations via Vedanta subsidiaries, and the subsequent 2016/2018 retrospective amendments reported to have legalised this for both parties identically — the record is symmetric on its face: same violation, same two respondents, same legislative fix applied to both. That is the one part of this file's brief we can say, with real confidence, treated both parties identically.


### capital

**holders-aggregates** — The same bottom-up lens was applied to the controls to the extent sources allowed. Vanguard: only named lines (HDFC Bank 2.44% via two funds) — larger than BlackRock's eight-fund aggregate in the same company (1.75%), and Vanguard's per-fund concentration makes it more, not less, visible than BlackRock. NBIM: named at 1.34% (HDFC Bank) and 1.06% (Bharti Airtel) — smaller than BlackRock's unnamed aggregate in both (1.75%, 1.09%), yet the one that appears by name; nbim.no data itself not retrievable. GIC: named at 2.17% (HDFC Bank) and 1.22% (Bharti Airtel) — larger than BlackRock's aggregate in both. LIC (domestic control): 4.77–15.69% single lines, 3–27× the BlackRock aggregate. Strategic foreign holders (BAT 22.91% of ITC, Pastel/Singtel 7.49% of Bharti Airtel) dwarf every passive manager. Run on the controls, the lens produces the same shape — passive managers at 1–2.5% per company, sovereign funds at 1–2%, domestic LIC far above — which is a finding about index investing, not about BlackRock. The one asymmetry the lens does expose is disclosure: multi-fund managers (BlackRock) are systematically less visible than single-vehicle holders (NBIM, GIC) of the same or smaller size.

**holders** — The same five-company, primary-filing lens applied to every name in SPEC's mandatory comparison set (BlackRock, Vanguard, NBIM, GIC/Government of Singapore, Capital Group, Fidelity, Kuwait Investment Authority, ADIA) plus LIC as the domestic control produces a graded, not uniform, picture: BlackRock — the name that carries the most alarming popular narrative — crosses the 1% named-holder threshold zero times in five checks; Vanguard once; NBIM and GIC/Singapore twice each; Capital Group, Fidelity, KIA and ADIA zero times each (absence-in-small-sample, not confirmed absence). LIC, the domestic control, crosses 1% in four of five and is the single largest non-promoter shareholder in three of them. The two foreign holdings that actually dwarf every passive-fund line in the sample — British American Tobacco's 22.91% of ITC and (tier reported) Suzuki Motor Corporation's ~58% of Maruti Suzuki — are neither BlackRock nor Vanguard nor any other passive index manager: they are foreign industrial promoters, a structurally different kind of foreign ownership the 'asset managers own India' narrative does not usually mean and does not distinguish. Running the same lens on the controls therefore does not produce an equally alarming picture for BlackRock specifically — if anything the opposite, a systematic UNDER-representation relative to its reputation, for the structural reason recorded on the cap:blackrock entity (fragmentation across many separately registered FPI vehicles, none individually crossing 1%) — while it produces a genuinely large, well-documented, non-alarmist finding (a legacy multinational tobacco company's ~23% stake in India's largest FMCG conglomerate; a Japanese automaker's ~59% stake in India's largest carmaker) that the popular narrative rarely names at all.

**mandates-ventures** — Advisers: the same mandate fields (client, deal, role, date, fee) were filled for Rothschild & Co and for the controls. Result: Rothschild has two dated mid-market mandates (Cropnosys Rs 375 crore, CleanMax US$360m) and a reported #3-by-count 2007–2023 rank; Morgan Stanley (Jio–Facebook US$5.7bn), Citi (#1 M&A and ECM H1 2024), Kotak (#1 fees H1 2024), Perella Weinberg (Holcim CHF 6.4bn) each have larger documented marquee mandates; Lazard and JP Morgan have none in this file and are equally absent from the H1 2024 tables. The lens that makes Rothschild look 'everywhere' makes it look smaller than eight named rivals once fees and values are used; the lens that makes it look 'nowhere' does the same to Lazard. Fees: undisclosed for every adviser on every side except the two Re 1 bids, which are Big Four, not Rothschild. JVs: Jio BlackRock, Bajaj Finserv, IIHL–Invesco and Nippon Life–RNAM were recorded with the same predicates (own, award, law) under the same reg. 7; the domestic control (Bajaj Finserv) waited the same 11 months for in-principle approval; the foreign-majority acquisitions (Nippon Life 75%, Invesco→IIHL 60%) went through change-in-control rather than a new licence, which is faster than either JV route. Persons: Ambani, Fink and Pandey were each recorded only by office with dates; none carries an allegation claim in this file.

**narratives-literature** — Three lenses were run on the controls named in the brief and on the mirror side. (1) 'Foreign holder owns X' — applied to Vanguard, NBIM, GIC, Capital Group, Fidelity and LIC on the same five primary filings (holders.json): the sovereign funds of Norway and Singapore cross 1% twice as often as BlackRock (which never does), and the domestic state insurer LIC crosses 1% in four of five — the lens, applied evenly, produces 'Norway and Singapore own India' or 'the Indian state owns India' before it produces 'BlackRock owns India'; the narrative selects its actor. (2) 'Foreign adviser controls X' — applied to Lazard, Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, Citi, Kotak, Axis, Avendus (mandates-ventures.json): Rothschild & Co is absent from every LSEG H1 2024 top-10 where Citi, Kotak and Goldman appear, so the same lens run on the controls produces a more 'alarming' picture for Citi than for Rothschild; the narrative again selects its actor. (3) 'Coordinated foreign-funded campaign' — the identical test (common funder + same-week statements + shared target + political reading) applied to the response to Hindenburg's Aug 2024 report shows AMFI (the regulated industry), the Adani Group, a BJP office-holder, a senior lawyer, a former Infosys CFO and India's then IMF executive director issuing mutually reinforcing statements within 48 hours, carried by the state broadcaster (newsonair.gov.in) — which under the same lens is 'coordination' by state-funded media of a state-aligned message. Neither side's simultaneity is evidence of direction; both are the base rate of public controversy. The lens produces an equally alarming picture for the controls in all three cases; that is a finding about the lens, and the narratives are calibrated accordingly (unsupported/debunked for the actor-selecting claims; contested only where a documented structure exists without a numerator — Mauritius round-tripping — or where the record is genuinely two-sided — OCCRP's US funding, the 'clean chit').

**rules-regulators** — The brief's control is 'the same disclosure lens on domestic promoters/other FPI-holding groups.' SEBI's own circulars and board minutes state the 50%-concentration and size criteria in group-neutral terms and were, on SEBI's account, triggered by roughly Rs 2.6 lakh crore of FPI AUM as of March 2023 (base rate above) — far more than Adani group companies alone could account for, implying other groups were caught. The March 2024 no-identified-promoter exemption is the clearest documented evidence the rule is built around promoter-linked concentration generically: it is drafted to exclude exactly the companies (those without an identifiable promoter) that structurally cannot present the round-tripping risk the whole regime targets, and by that same logic it cannot apply to Adani group companies, which do have identified promoters. Where the lens fails the symmetry test is transparency, not design: SEBI has never named which non-Adani groups or FPIs were caught, so the claim that the rule bites broadly cannot be verified against named cases from public sources — only inferred from the aggregate AUM figure and the rule's own neutral drafting. That gap cuts both ways: it is equally consistent with a genuinely broad, Adani-including-but-not-Adani-only regime, and with a regime that in practice concentrates its bite on the one group whose FPIs drew press and judicial attention. This file cannot resolve that from what SEBI has published.
