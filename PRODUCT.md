# ICIP product context

ICIP is a static, source-led research application for exploring India's listed companies, public funding and institutional connections. Its core artifact is a public record with a date, scope, source and evidence tier. Researchers should be able to find a register, compare like records, inspect provenance and distinguish a coverage gap from a negative finding.

The education funding desk extends this work to government and private schools and colleges, public programmes, corporate giving, NGOs and foreign finance. Location filters organize the available record; they do not establish complete city coverage. School closures and population change are research questions requiring comparable geography, age groups, dates and institutional identity.

Product priorities:

1. Keep source provenance, denominators and uncertainty beside the relevant claim.
2. Make education and the other registers discoverable in a growing navigation system.
3. Preserve every existing route and analytical workflow.
4. Provide usable mobile exploration, keyboard navigation and readable dense data.
5. Keep the existing React/TypeScript/Vite architecture and reproducible data pipeline.

This brief is inferred from the requested work and repository evidence, not user interviews. Claims about audience preference or task success have not been validated with participants.

The water and food-security register extends the same research workflow across drinking water, groundwater, weather and hazards, public works, farming and food systems. Its publication window is 6 October 2021–6 October 2026. State, city and village searches expose the available evidence at its actual geographic level. Food-chain stages organize independent source records; they do not establish a traced shipment or causal chain. Historic bulletins and events remain dated research, with their original issue and validity periods.

The public-works investigation desk connects procurement evidence to roads and
bridges, electricity, water, health, education, police, military, recruitment and
administration. Audits, contractor relationships, legal changes and government
responses are separate evidence layers. Repeated notices, repeated suppliers and
repeat physical work remain distinct. The user can inspect a sourced subgraph or
compare two records against explicit identity, location, scope and date gates.

The Public Record Atlas is the unified geographic entry point. Researchers move from a national or state view into a sector, institution, case, dated policy change or exact relationship, then inspect sources and responses beside the map. Optional 3D represents the spatial surface; it must not imply that illustrative building height, node prominence, or camera position measures money, guilt, or completeness. Time controls state whether they filter dated evidence or change a view. The original registers remain available as dossiers, and saved casebook records and existing deep links remain valid.

The current redesign is explicitly authorized across all pages. Its evaluation is an independent AI design and technical review, not invented user interviews, participant testing, or editorial adjudication of allegations.

The public-funding research desk adds reviewed case files, institutional decision reconstructions, competing explanations and a visible acquisition backlog across the historical window 2011–2026. It preserves the existing sector atlas, provides exact-stage source-linked case graphs and exports, and exposes retrieval failures and unapproved identity candidates. The window describes research scope, not complete annual or national coverage. No new forecasting accuracy or private motive is inferred.

The coordination-hypothesis desk examines selected decisions of the Modi Union administrations (2014–2026). It separates source-attributed relationships from optional unproved questions, preserves rules in force and rival explanations, and shows the records needed to discriminate among them. Institutional scenarios are retrospective qualitative analysis; no secret intent, guilt probability, exhaustive coverage or newly trained forecasting model is implied.

## Investigation-board redesign, 15 October 2026

The latest request prioritizes desktop research: a pin-and-wool investigation board over a soft map, with meaningful visual depth, intuitive navigation and opt-in contextual popup tutorials across all existing pages. This replaces the earlier atlas-only visual direction while preserving the atlas, original dossiers and every established research workflow. A smaller-window fallback remains available; it does not imply a separately validated mobile design.

The new `#/board` operates on the existing browser-local Casebook. Pins retain sources and original qualifiers. User-created threads are typed analyst annotations and never become sourced graph assertions. Existing source-backed edges remain a separately labeled layer. Source data, legal status and financial stages are not rewritten for onboarding.

The tutorial's Fairfax/IDBI example is a source-bound, already-reported evidence exercise. It distinguishes regulatory approval from completed sale, voting rights from equity, proposed funding from cash paid, and dated valuation changes. It claims no secret plot, unique discovery or newly proven misconduct. The global/finance/media applications retain their specialized features and do not imply a shared Casebook if one is not implemented there.
