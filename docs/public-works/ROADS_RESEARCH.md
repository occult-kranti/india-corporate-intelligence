# Roads and bridges research

Snapshot: **6 October 2026**. Recent-publication window: **6 October 2021–6 October 2026**, inclusive. This is a curated evidence desk, not a nationwide inspection, complete tender archive or current bridge-safety advisory.

The roads specialist used the Exa Search plugin, repository source-retrieval, evidence-tiering and pattern-discipline skills. Thirteen searches requested 77 result slots across audit/engineering, incident/response, procurement-rule and programme/award workstreams. These are requested result slots, not 77 independently verified sources. Fourteen selected sources were opened or read through full indexed extraction; the source JSON identifies the retrieval method. No contractor or official was contacted.

## Two panel rounds

**Round 1 — research and design constraints.** The road-engineering/procurement role recommended joining repeat-work records on asset identifier, chainage, work type, contract stage and time; separating audit claims, official responses and findings; and treating laws by version, effective date and procurement applicability. Corporate/party connections require separately evidenced identity and chronology. A shared corridor or corporate hub is not a causal political connection.

**Round 2 — challenge against retrieved evidence.** The roads role applied these checks to the concrete records and the data-engineering panel challenged schema/identity requirements:

- Replaced the tempting Dwarka cost-ratio claim with an appraisal/design question and retained both the primary ministry reply and the separately reported 2023 response. The latter article’s aggregate and per-km estimates are internally difficult to reconcile, so its numbers are not used to calculate savings.
- Kept Jagatpur restoration **estimate**, affected historical expenditure and contractor repair claims separate; no second-payment total was invented.
- Kept the West Bengal 15/67, 5/30 and 14-bridge samples distinct. Added the department’s acceptance and the absence of an asset-specific load assessment.
- Treated the 2022 Kerala VUP and 2025 slope failure as different assets on a shared corridor. A possible 2024 parliamentary follow-up has a chainage missing from the earlier release, so it is not silently merged.
- Preserved contractor-funded reconstruction in Cherkkala. Kept the show-cause proposal separate from the announced debarment and unknown final/current status.
- Removed invented first-of-month dates and publication-as-commencement dates on historical role edges. Month-only dates and observed-in-release dates remain in prose with null exact role dates. Award/action announcements use the same start/end day and are explicitly announcement events.
- Added explicit geography and scope to every edge: West Bengal bridge/utility links cannot leak into other state filters through an inferred national default. Rule geography is explicitly national central-government guidance.
- Removed corridor endpoints as physical locality tags for the Bihar bridge, and removed Chengala/Neeleshwaram as aliases of Kasaragod district. Corridor names remain searchable without false geographic precision.
- Kept the Bihar project concessionaire distinct from a similarly named contractor group; no ownership edge was inferred.
- Excluded the March 2025 draft Works Manual and read the final edition’s scope and legal hierarchy. Generic central guidance is not presented as an automatic rule for every state/private project.

These are independent AI-role reviews within the working panel, not endorsements by human engineers or auditors.

## Rule history

The 2 November 2021 debarment OM separates ministry/entity action from government-wide action, requires representation and preserves pre-existing contracts. The June 2022 Works Manual consolidates rather than creates a single new effective date. The 1 November 2023 QCBS clarification allows works of any value through the prescribed Quality Oriented Procurement process; a non-lowest award therefore needs its published criteria checked. The final 2025 Works Manual, foreword dated 23 December 2025 and verified on an official republication dated 28 July 2026, recognises specialist engineering frameworks and gives law/GFR priority. Subsequent amendments are not comprehensively audited by this slice.
