## PACL: regulatory finding and court-directed recovery

Case ID: `pacl-land-cis-refunds`.

The [22 August 2014 SEBI order](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/1408704987673.pdf) found PACL's plans to be unregistered collective investment schemes under section 11AA. It directed collections to stop, schemes to be wound up and amounts with contractual returns to be refunded. This is an attributed regulatory finding, not a claim of criminal conviction.

The new connections trace investors → PACL → the described commission and land-purchase routes, then SEBI/Supreme Court → Lodha Committee → verified refund aggregate and named auction administrators. The commission recipients and land sellers remain aggregate cohorts. PACL's statement describes direct purchase payments to sellers while land was held through associate companies; no parcel-level bank ledger is invented. SEBI's conclusion about associate arrangements to circumvent land ceilings is attributed to that order; no separate state conviction is claimed.

No recovery percentage, current unpaid principal or aggregate theft estimate is computed from these incompatible measures. The memorandum is in a July 2026 archive but has no independently established publication day; `publishedAt` is null. The embedded older memorandum's March 2025 figures are not substituted for current totals. Only refund §2.2 expressly dates its observation to 5 May; that date is not copied to auction or bank balances.

The principal contrary qualification is visible in its own response record: PACL asserted a land-sale/development business and relied on verified deeds. SEBI rejected the scheme-level defence while **paragraph 37 preserves sale deeds certified as genuine by Justice K. Swamidurai**. The finding that none of a selected 500-customer sample had a completed sale deed does not mean that no customer ever received land. PACL also sought five years to execute repayment; the order rejected that proposal.

Latest opened procedural sources are the [3 August 2026 bank-deficiency notice](https://www.sebi.gov.in/documents/extension%20of%20time.pdf), [9 September 2026 Supreme Court order](https://www.sebi.gov.in/documents/Order-34668_2015-09-09-2026.pdf), and [30 September 2026 Bengaluru Rural tender](https://www.sebi.gov.in/documents/RFP%20of%20Bangaluru_Rural%20dated-30sep2026.pdf). The notice extended rectification to 6 October. The court accepted specified property-objection recommendations, requested reports on other applications and listed 8 October; it did not close the whole proceeding. The tender names **StockHolding Document Management Services Limited** as assistance agency and **C1 India Private Limited** as platform provider and expressly says the Committee is not owner. These providers are administrative participants, not accused beneficiaries. November auction milestones are future plans, with no buyer or receipt asserted.

Audit test: reconcile scheme bank entries, paid commission schedules, genuinely registered deeds, auction proceeds and verified refund acknowledgements at matching dates. A regulator's aggregate, a book-value or auction figure and a cash refund are different evidentiary objects. Do not publish investors' account details to do this work.

## Chapwa: disputed collection and procedural judicial relief

Case ID: `chapwa-toll-debarment`.

The [Delhi High Court judgment of 11 December 2025](https://delhihighcourt.nic.in/app/showFileJudgment/58711122025CW92322025_135032.pdf), W.P.(C) 9232/2025, 2025:DHC:11216, records the exact relationship: **NHAI → Chapwa user-fee collection contract → Ganeshgarhia Construction Private Limited**, with **Ganeshgarhia → NHAI** for the annual remittance obligation. IHMCL's communication led to an inspection. No association with other toll contractors, national debarments or ED's later investigations is inferred.

| Amount | Financial stage |
| --- | --- |
| ₹6,82,10,000 | Annual contractor-to-NHAI remittance commitment for 29 July 2024–29 July 2025, not a public payment to the contractor |
| ₹50 | Single inspection cash collection NHAI alleged was not recorded; the factual dispute was not finally decided |
| ₹10,00,000 | Penalty imposed by 12 February 2025 letter; actual recovery unverified, and contractor said it had not been implemented |

NHAI alleged the ingress collection lacked a receipt and was entered as exempt, while the return trip was recorded. It invoked the contractual treatment of specified non-FASTag collections. The product does not multiply the alleged ₹50 by a percentage to invent loss, or scale it to a national network.

Ganeshgarhia disputed unauthorised software use, asserted local-resident treatment, relied on the recorded ₹70 return trip and argued that evidence/complaint particulars were not supplied. NHAI disputed exemption eligibility and said its deployed vehicle was not local. Both positions remain visible.

The High Court's precise outcome matters: §§30–40 set aside the **debarment** for an unreasoned decision and failure to consider the reply. Section 36 notes no POS device/system was seized. Section 34 expressly reserves underlying factual conclusions. Section 41 leaves termination and penalty to contractual/civil remedies. This is neither a continuing one-year ban nor factual exoneration or criminal acquittal; non-interference with the penalty is not proof of its receipt.

The judgment has an internal year discrepancy: §§1–2 and the reproduced work order identify a contract dated 25 July **2024**, whereas §41 says 25 July **2025**. The slice uses the identified original contract date and flags the discrepancy, rather than creating a second contract. The source also uses 3 and 4 February for the IHMCL communication in different passages, so the edge dates the undisputed 8 February inspection instead.

No later appeal, recovery or separate contractual award was established by searches through 7 October 2026. The dataset labels 11 December 2025 as its last verified judicial status. That is a knowledge boundary, not proof that no later event exists.

Audit test: compare official ETC records, video, receipts, exemption eligibility and remittance ledgers, then require a reasoned decision addressing the actual response. A process defect and a disputed fact must remain distinguishable.

## Access and reproducibility

`policy-evidence/source-archive-index.json` maps every admitted source to receipts and readable extracts. `sha256-manifest.json` inventories repository-relative tracked artifacts plus separately labelled originals in `/workspace/research-cache/allegations-policy`. Receipts retain retrieval time, URL, final URL, HTTP status/headers where available, byte count and hash. Extract PDFs identify original page numbers and have their own hashes. No OCR was run.

Official Delhi High Court direct downloads timed out twice. The official URL was opened through indexed full text and saved with a receipt explicitly identifying this access method. The 24-page court-document PDF was downloaded from [Rawlaw's mirror](https://rawlaw.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/GANESHGARHIA.pdf), and material passages were checked against the official-host indexed text. The mirror source tier is `reported`; the official-host indexed source independently supports the documented outcome. No byte-identical official download is claimed.

The first PACL-order fetch failed with an incomplete read; a retry fetched all 871,383 bytes and all 92 pages. A guessed NHAI release URL returned 404 and is failure evidence only. Neither failure is concealed or admitted as substantive evidence.

The Reuters contemporaneous corroboration was opened but only heading/lead returned. Only an authored access note is retained and hashed, explicitly not commercial article bytes. No wholesale commercial news article is captured. Government/court PDFs are archived as originals or accurately labelled mirror originals. Large PDFs live in the research cache with page extracts, full text and receipts available in the evidence directory.
