# Delhi Jal Board: follow the documents before extending the money graph

Review cutoff: 7 October 2026. This is a bounded investigation of the NKG electromagnetic flow-meter contract, not a survey of all DJB expenditure. No skills were used.

## What changed from the prior release

The earlier page had a 2025 bail transcription. This review retrieved two original 2026 decisions: the [1 April attachment appeal](https://atfp.gov.in/writereaddata/upload/Judgement/Judgement_P9OL1ZEXOA_410202632953PM.PDF) and the [4 July Delhi High Court approver-recall judgment, hosted by ED](https://enforcementdirectorate.gov.in/media/important-judgements/1071e816-dc0b-42b3-9195-127c8b93aa2b_TAJINDERPAL.pdf). It also recovered migrated original ED press releases and the company's FY2019–20 annual report.

The new evidence adds a payment-stage assertion, separates the prime-contractor and subcontractor branches, identifies documentary failures at the procurement gate, and tests a political destination against the party's denial. It also establishes that an attached property was an equivalent-value substitute, which contradicts a simple diagram of tender money directly buying that property.

## Connections with a documentary basis

**DJB ↔ NBCC ↔ NKG.** The tribunal describes a Faridabad-project performance certificate being used as eligibility evidence in another department's tender. It identifies NIT 22 dated 15 December 2017, complaints on 19 March and 1 June 2018, an inspection on 24 March, three certificate dates, an unsigned adverse draft and a later favourable note-sheet. The investigative question is whether authenticated versions, SCADA operational evidence and the procurement rules support the reversal. These are documentary links, not payments to NBCC officials. D.K. Mittal's retraction is retained; underlying originals were not obtained.

**NKG ↔ historical owners.** The [company's report](https://nkginfra.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/NKG-AR2020-28-10-2020-Final.pdf), printed pp24,31,54, supplies CIN U74899DL1989PLC038371, Pradeep Kumar Garg's DIN 00030683 and equity holdings as at 31 March 2020: Pradeep 38.48%, Archit Steels 29.98%, Pradeep Sons 14.88%, Aman Promoters 9.74%. Corporate shareholder names are bounded documentary mentions because their legal identifiers and ultimate owners were not independently verified. This source does not show them receiving DJB funds. Printed p33 uses a different total-capital denominator, including preference shares; its percentages cannot be substituted for equity percentages.

**Arora ↔ alleged political destination.** The [original ED release](https://enforcementdirectorate.gov.in/media/press-release-documents/c51f9d36-9ed5-49a2-8d76-b32990c29641_Press_Release-PC_DJB-342024_10.pdf) makes the allegation. [PTI's report of AAP's response](https://www.rediff.com/news/report/djb-contract-bribes-transferred-to-officials-aap-ed/20240403.htm) records its denial of involvement and challenge to the evidence. The graph's party edge is unquantified. It does not assign the entire combined ₹2 crore to AAP or infer a minister, treasurer or party worker as recipient. The court's ED recital and ED's press release share one originating agency; repetition does not create independent corroboration.

## Findings that challenge the apparent story

- The [7 February 2024 ED release](https://enforcementdirectorate.gov.in/media/press-release-documents/c70cc686-9013-4756-a7e6-74239f224a78_Press%20Release-%20DJB%20Search-722024.pdf) says about ₹17 crore was spent on work; the 3 April release says about ₹14 crore. No reconciled scope, tax, O&M or period schedule was supplied. Subtracting either from contract value would not establish loss.
- Tribunal §§8(ii),20 say “2.63 lakhs”; §27(b) explicitly gives ₹2,63,50,000, comprising ₹1.90 crore cash and ₹73.50 lakh bank channels. The precise latter amount is attributed to that paragraph. The source inconsistency remains visible, not silently corrected.
- The tribunal narrative sometimes says Jagdish Kumar Aggarwal rather than Arora; several sources inconsistently append “Ltd.” to Integral while calling it a proprietorship. No additional legal identity is invented from these variants.
- The 2025 applicants disputed the predicate-offence basis for the bribe allegations, GST inclusion, the deemed profit margin, and evidence. They received conditional bail for trial delay. That is not an acquittal.
- The tribunal excludes Poonam's share and treats the attachment as equivalent value because the alleged proceeds were unavailable. Neither marriage nor joint ownership establishes receipt.
- The July 2026 recall dismissal leaves approver status in place but preserves cross-examination at trial. It does not convict any defendant or authenticate every payment.

## Where the trace stops, and what would change it

1. Obtain court-permitted copies of relied-upon transaction exhibits, the seized workbook and forensic hash/report. Compare exact dates and counterparty legal identities with statements and cross-examination; reject unsupported association joins.
2. Reconcile DJB bills and disbursements to NKG receipts, the executed subcontract, GST and actual deliveries. Identify legitimate tax, O&M, retention and timing differences before calling them diverted funds.
3. Reconcile NKG's alleged retained branch with supplier liabilities, expenses, loans, dividends and related-party schedules. Shareholding alone cannot establish personal benefit.
4. Seek a recipient-by-recipient schedule for the combined political allegation and test against receipts, testimony and election-finance entries. A missing ledger entry alone neither proves clandestine receipt nor disproves a claimed cash transfer.
5. Check the current trial and appeal docket. This search located a 4 July 2026 procedural outcome; it does not claim to have the complete current docket or a merits judgment.
