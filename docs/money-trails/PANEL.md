# Transaction-level investigation expansion

Review date: 7 October 2026. Base release: `3d6ec883657a6c74799310f8919d4a57ba6447e7`. Working branch: `codex/education-funding-intelligence`. No skills are used.

## Round one: investigate the missing joins

The previous release catalogued sources, allegations, financial stages and replies. This review measures its value by new documentary joins and contradicted or narrowed interpretations, not by the number of accusations. Three independent investigators follow Delhi Jal Board procurement, Mumbai emergency-food contracts and the Mumbai CCTV contractor's corporate and political-finance disclosures. A fourth researcher independently challenges the originals and financial interpretations. Separate implementation owners build the evidence contract and map experience; the lead integrates and verifies the complete site.

The investigative questions are concrete:

1. Which document establishes a public-body payment, which legally identified counterparty received it, and which part of that account is an investigator's assertion reproduced in a judicial record?
2. What executed subcontract, invoice, tax reconciliation, acceptance certificate or bank exhibit supports the next transfer? Where do figures overlap or conflict?
3. Which director, shareholder, lender, office holder or political recipient is actually connected by a dated document? What would invalidate the identity or the claimed period?
4. Does a later court or tribunal decision narrow, reject or uphold an earlier account, and for what legal question? Bail, attachment, recovery and a criminal merits decision have different scopes.
5. Where does the money trail end? Which institution holds the missing record, and what result would defeat the adverse interpretation?

Thinking from an insider's perspective supplies hypotheses about approval, subcontracting, documentation and oversight failures. It does not supply facts about named actors. Each hypothesis must name expected documentary evidence, rival explanations, contrary evidence and a disconfirmation test. No graph relationship is admitted merely because a possible mechanism is plausible.

## Evidence and graph contract

Exact legal identities, source locators, event dates and financial stages accompany every explicit hop. A court-authored document can be a primary record of an agency's contested assertion without independently proving its cash narrative. Documentary ownership, public office and donation records remain context and never silently turn a connected graph into a cash path. Unknown recipients and missing transfer records become explicit stop points, not invented financial edges.

Amounts retain scope, stage, date basis and overlap groups. A tender ceiling, accepted contract price, payment, accounting recognition, alleged proceeds and attached property are never pooled. Source families distinguish multiple reports of one underlying claim from independent corroboration. Privacy-safe court extracts preserve locators and original-document hashes while omitting private account and home-address fields.

Original records take priority over aggregators. Secondary leads remain attributed until the original is retrieved. Reproducible extraction and exact identifiers are required for political-bond joins. The model may rank public authored source summaries for discovery; it cannot establish a financial relationship or legal identity.

## Round two and publication acceptance

The independent editor rereads material original passages, especially numbers, attribution, current procedural stage, defences, ownership dates and money direction. The UI review checks whether the map, hop reader and hypothesis panel communicate that evidence without implying a complete money trail. Unknown and rejected joins must remain visible.

Acceptance requires source and response closure, archive hashes, no fabricated gap edges, meaningful adversarial data tests, browser-history and deep-link behavior, evidence exports, mobile readers and retained-site regression checks. The final build is frozen before browser acceptance. Publication continues under the user's existing authorization; the normal public URL and deployed asset hashes are checked after GitHub's deployment gates pass.
