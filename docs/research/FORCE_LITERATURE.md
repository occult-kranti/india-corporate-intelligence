# Force — the literature on defence and police money, procurement, pay and the big cases

*Digest, compiled 2026-10-06 from the force fleet's raw files. Machine-readable twin: `research/raw/force/literature.json`
(asOf 2026-10-04, with a 2026-10-06 audit-corrections pass recorded in its gaps). No work appears here that the
force files do not cite. No figure appears here that is not copied from the file named beside it. Where two files
print different figures for the same thing, both are given with their files. Tiers are the files' own.*

**How to read the reliability notes.** Six genres answer six different questions. *Budget documents, Parliament
answers and audits* (Notes on Demands for Grants, MHA and MoD replies, CAG) establish what was allocated, spent or
found; they say nothing about adequacy or motive. *Analysts who transcribe them* (PRS, MP-IDSA, ORF) are reliable on
arithmetic and openly argue a side on policy; the fleet carries their figures at `reported` until the demand PDF is
reproduced. *Surveys* (the Common Cause–Lokniti-CSDS Status of Policing in India Reports, the India Justice Report)
measure attitudes and administrative capacity, not incidence. *Comparative econometric work* (Chalfin & McCrary, an NBER
working paper) supplies method and a foreign benchmark, never an Indian figure. *Court records* establish what was decided and on
what ground, not what happened. *Journalism of record* (Swedish Radio and The Hindu on Bofors; The Hindu on Rafale;
the Pegasus Project; Mediapart) produced the documents the cases turn on; where the fleet read it through Wikipedia
it is `reported`. Silence in one genre says nothing about the others.

*Tier ladder for claims:* documented → reported → alleged; `analytic` is the fleet's own computation.
*Ladder for narratives:* established → well-supported → contested → speculative → unsupported → debunked.

| MP-IDSA, Laxman Kumar Behera, *India's Defence Budget 2020-21* (2020-02-04) | For | MoD 2020-21 BE ₹4,71,378 crore; the gap between the services' projections and allocation 27% (2013-14), 14% (2015-16), 30% (2018-19), 25% (2019-20, BE). | An optimal level: projections are the services' demands, not an independent standard. | reported · `literature.json` |

**Strongest case for "too little".** The Standing Committee asked for about 3% of GDP. The Ministry received
₹9.01 lakh crore for capital outlay in 2021-26 against a plan projection of ₹17.46 lakh crore, and recorded "serious
capability gaps" (`literature.json`, via PRS). The four-demand ratio fell from 3.32% of GDP (BE 2000-01) to 2.00%
(BE 2026-27); actuals fell from 2.83% (2009-10) to 1.92% (2024-25) (`union-defence.json`).

**Strongest counter.** India sits at the SIPRI peer median. SIPRI's country averages are 1.9% for countries not in
conflict and 4.4% for countries in conflict. The MoD itself said 3% of GDP would produce allocations the services may
not be able to absorb (`literature.json`). The ratio fell under both governments: the four demands grew 11.0% a year
under UPA-II against GDP growth of 15.3%, and 8.8% a year from 2014-15 against GDP growth of 10.0%
(`union-defence.json`). The 3.32% start is a post-Kargil local peak (`union-defence.json`).

**The two files disagree on rank.** `literature.json` carries SIPRI's April 2025 fact sheet (fifth in 2024).
`union-defence.json` reads the v1.2 workbook, which it says superseded that release (sixth in 2024, fifth in 2025).
Both are carried; neither is corrected here.

**How the fleet used it.** As the denominator and peer set for the defence lines, never as the ₹ series. PRS figures
stay `reported` until the union-defence domain reproduces them from the demands.

**Rating.** Contested — `literature.json` ("India under-spends on defence; it should spend 3% of GDP") and
`union-defence.json` ("India under-spends on defence"). The deciding test, per `literature.json`, is an independent
CAG or Supreme Court capability audit.

**Strongest case.** The pension share of the MoD total (BE) went 16.9% (2000-01), 13.1% (2009-10), 17.9% (2014-15),
a peak of 28.4% (2020-21), 21.8% (2026-27) (`union-defence.json`). The average pension share was 15.6% under UPA-II
and 23.3% under NDA; the capital share 33.8% and 26.6% (`union-defence.json`). Defence Pensions actual rose 3.47×
from ₹45,499.54 crore (2013-14) to ₹157,653.65 crore (2024-25) against 2.33× for service pay and 2.02× for capital
(`pay-pensions.json`).

**Strongest counter.** Pensions are contracts, and the step changes are policy: OROP (2015) and the 7th CPC (2016)
(`literature.json`). "Nothing for modernisation" is contradicted by capital outlay of ₹1,92,388 crore, up 13% over
2024-25 RE (`literature.json`). The share has fallen back from its 2020-21 peak (`union-defence.json`). Service pay ÷
capital is a hump that rises with each pay commission under both governments: 0.70–0.79 (2010-11 to 2013-14),
1.01–1.13 (2015-16 to 2017-18), 0.90 (2024-25) (`pay-pensions.json`). Spend per pensioner was ₹4.79 lakh a year in
2024-25, nominal (`pay-pensions.json`).

**How the fleet used it.** ORF and PRS figures set the question; the union-defence and pay-pensions domains then
rebuilt the shares from the demands. The ORF +163% is carried as ORF's, on its mixed BE/actual basis.

**Rating.** Contested in three files. `literature.json`: the pay-and-pension half is supported, the "nothing for
modernisation" half is not. `union-defence.json`: the claim holds for 2000-01 to 2020-21 and is contested since.
`pay-pensions.json`: contested; whether it is "eating" depends on treating the MoD envelope as fixed. The OROP "betrayal" narrative is contested
(`pay-pensions.json`).

## 3. Is Agnipath a reform or a cost cut?

| Standing Committee on Defence, Eighth Report (Mar 2025) | Scale | 1,23,000 Agniveers enrolled (Army oral evidence); about 11% of 11,05,110 JCO/OR held on 1 Oct 2024, on the fleet's arithmetic. | The ratio itself; the report does not state it. | documented / analytic · `pay-pensions.json` |
| The Tribune (veterans, Jul 2024); Scroll / FactChecker.in (Jun 2022) | Against | Veterans' objections on training of about six months; the fact-check is cited for the Seva Nidhi arithmetic and the ex-servicemen reservation. | Outcomes: no service data on retention or cohesion has been released. | reported · `pay-pensions.json` |

**Strongest case for "cost cut".** The proponents state "a strong financial imperative". DPG: "it is apparent that the
rising pay and pension budget has been a key factor" (`literature.json`). The Agnipath line was ₹7,459.75 crore in
2024-25 actuals (4.9% of service pay plus Agnipath) and ₹17,396.18 crore in BE 2026-27 (10.5%) (`pay-pensions.json`).

**Strongest counter.** ORF finds no pension saving before 2044, so the near-term rationale is age profile. The
Defence Minister and the DMA denied a financial motive on the record (`literature.json`). Short service without
pension is normal in volunteer armies (`pay-pensions.json`).

**How the fleet used it.** ORF's arithmetic for the saving's timing; DPG for the objections; both carried as the two
sides' own cases. The Services have proposed raising retention to about 50% (Army, IAF) and about 75% (Navy); no
amendment was notified as of Oct 2026 (reported via relays of The Indian Express, `pay-pensions.json`). A change above
25% would void the ORF 34%/2044 arithmetic (`literature.json`).

**Rating.** "Agnipath is a cost-cutting measure disguised as military reform": contested — the cost motive is well
supported as a co-driver, "disguised" partly supported, "merely cost-cutting" unsupported (`literature.json`).
"Agnipath will hollow out the army": contested. "Agnipath saves ₹X crore": speculative; no official actuarial
projection exists (`pay-pensions.json`).

**Strongest case for "rigged".** The 2007 single-vendor finding; CAG 2019 on vendor-driven QRs; DAP 2020's surviving
single-vendor and IGA/FMS routes (`literature.json`). In the fleet's CPPP security slice the stores class (the services'
headquarters and stores buyers) single-bids at 12.87% and the research class (DRDO) at 18.49%
(`research/raw/cppp/security.json`). `literature.json` prints "40%+" for the services' headquarters; that figure is the
spec's first-cut probe (Air Force 41.6%, Coast Guard 42.5%, `docs/research/force/SPEC.md`, "leads, not findings"),
not the published slice.

equipment has one or two makers worldwide. The works class (MES and BRO) single-bids at 0.42%, and MES alone is 74.41%
of the slice's 411,943 award decisions (`research/raw/cppp/security.json`). `literature.json` prints 79% and 0.1% from the
same first-cut probe in `SPEC.md`. Single-source repeat orders occur for private incumbents too (`procurement-industry.json`).

**How the fleet used it.** The 2007 rate as a dated base rate, never as a current one. The CAG 2019 figures enter the
Rafale record from `money-people.json` at documented tier. The CPPP slice is the modern, different-universe
comparison.

**Rating.** "Defence procurement is rigged through single-vendor tenders": contested (`literature.json`). "DPSUs are
protected from competition": contested — DPSUs plus the ex-OFB companies produced 73.8% of output in 2023-24 and took
77% of the fleet's ₹4.07 lakh crore sample of PIB-named awards 2021-25 (HAL 58%), while private firms won categories
DPSUs had held (`procurement-industry.json`).

(`literature.json`). "Make in India has not reduced imports": contested — the past-tense claim is unsupported on SIPRI

joint award of ₹2,770 crore, 0.68% of the fleet's PIB-named sample, and one unpriced DRDO development partnership

**Strongest case.** 155 per lakh is 70% of 222 (`literature.json`). All-India actual strength was 155 against 197

(`state-police.json`).

shortfall is against India's own sanction, about 21% (`literature.json`). Vacancy does not track the one outcome

itself an administrative number (`state-police.json`). The comparative literature says more officers reduce crime, not
that a ratio is right (`literature.json`).

**How the fleet used it.** 222 is carried only with the words "unverified denominator". The base rate the fleet
prefers is actual over sanctioned in the same year (`literature.json`).

**Rating.** "India is dangerously under-policed: the UN recommends 222": contested; "dangerously" is a word no source
evidences (`literature.json`). "Police vacancies are the real problem": contested (`state-police.json`). "Kerala has
the best-funded police in India": unsupported — ₹1,212 per capita (2023-24 Accounts), 13th of 20 states; the
narrative's origin is SPIR 2019's adequacy index, which ranked Kerala second on staffing, infrastructure and budget
use, not on money (`state-police.json`).

## 13. Symmetry check

- **Bofors beside Rafale.** Same fields, same shape: a document trail from journalism of record, agent-side or
  dissent-side paper, a judiciary that declined to find corruption, and no conviction. The agent-payment field fills
  the same way for both: Swiss accounts in the 1980s, and Mediapart's 2007–12 commissions, mostly in the UPA era
  (`literature.json`).
- **AgustaWestland beside Tatra.** Both exposed under Defence Minister Antony, both CBI cases from 2012–13, neither with
  an Indian verdict. The depth difference is a finding about attention, not guilt (`literature.json`).
- **UPA-II beside NDA on the budget.** The pension share rose under both governments; the capital share fell under
  UPA-II and stayed in the high twenties under NDA (`union-defence.json`). Behera's adequacy gap was 27% in UPA's last
  year and 25–30% in 2018–20, so the method flatters neither side (`literature.json`).
- **BJP-run beside opposition-run states.** Among large states the two groups overlap on every measure: per capita
  ₹912–₹1,478 against ₹1,012–₹2,615; NHRC custodial-death cases per lakh police 3.51–21.93 against 1.64–11.77
  (`state-police.json`). States returning NCRB 0 against NHRC > 0 span parties (`state-police.json`). Installations
  per million residents: 0.12 against 0.14 (`footprint.json`).
- **Bonds.** The bond lens produces the same alarm-sized picture for non-defence donors of every party
  (`money-people.json`).
- **Pegasus.** The UPA-II control does not apply; the leaked list cut across parties (`literature.json`).
- **SPIR.** The attitude series begins in 2018; no UPA baseline can be run (`literature.json`).
