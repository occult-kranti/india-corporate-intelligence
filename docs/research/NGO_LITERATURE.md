# Foreign money in Indian civil society — the literature, the reports and the official statements

*Annotated bibliography, verified 2026-09-26 by the `donors-narratives` research agent. Machine-readable twin of the
claims it supports: `research/raw/ngo/donors-narratives.json` (with `fcra-actions.json` and `fcra-receipts.json`
alongside). Every row says what was actually opened: a publisher page, a PDF, a mirror, or nothing. Where the text was not
read, the row says so and the entry must not be cited for its findings from this file. Web search was rate-limited;
`sacw.net`, `sabrang.com`, `fcraonline.nic.in` and `mha.gov.in/en/commoncontent` were unreachable from the container.*

**How to read the reliability notes.** Five genres answer five different questions. *Political science* (Jenkins) tells
you why NGOs became political actors and how the state learned to regulate them; it does not count money. *Philanthropy
measurement* (CSIP-Ashoka, Bain–Dasra) tells you the scale of private giving — the denominator every foreign-money
narrative omits — but relies on consultant estimates, not audited flows. *Rights monitors* (Freedom House, CIVICUS, HRW,
Amnesty, ICNL) document the pattern of enforcement against critics; they are advocacy organisations and say so. *Activist
research* (Sabrang/SACW, AWAAZ, the pseudonymous 2022 report) reads US and UK charity filings to trace diaspora money to
Sangh affiliates; the flow it documents is lawful and declared, the characterisation is contested, and the affiliates'
rebuttals belong beside it. *The Ministry's own words* (PIB factsheets, Parliament answers) tell you what the regulator
says the law is for; they are the contra to every silencing claim and must be recorded with the same care.
