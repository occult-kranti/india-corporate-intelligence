# Investigating coordination hypotheses

Review date: 14 October 2026. The principal period is 26 May 2014–14 October 2026; earlier decisions are dated context and potential comparisons. This is a selected documentary investigation into institutional decisions during the Modi Union administrations. State institutions, private actors and the Union have different powers. A common period or beneficiary does not make all their actions Union decisions.

The page permits an adverse hypothesis to be investigated without presenting that hypothesis as a discovered conspiracy. “Alleged” is reserved for an attributed assertion; it cannot turn our conjecture into someone else's allegation. The test is what the records support, what they contradict, and which missing observation would distinguish rival explanations. Neither neutrality as an aesthetic nor an appetite for a dramatic story decides the result.

## Decision audit, rather than an invented payoff model

Each file identifies a dated decision, the actor legally entitled to make it, the rule in force, available information, feasible institutional options and their expected observable consequences. Actors can have legitimate competing incentives: speed versus competitive process, fiscal protection versus service continuity, transparency versus legally protected information. An adverse conflict mechanism may be considered where the evidence creates a concrete question, but private preferences, communications and intent stay unknown unless independently documented.

The same observed award can arise from different strategies and constraints. Working backward from its outcome does not recover a unique motive. We therefore use game-theory-inspired qualitative decision audits; we do not claim estimated utilities, Nash equilibria, an exhaustive action set, or a probability of criminal intent. Strategic scenarios describe institutional choices and oversight records, not ways to evade procurement rules, disguise payments, threaten witnesses or conceal wrongdoing.

For each decision, the worksheet asks:

1. Who could sign, approve, review or appeal the specific action on the operative date? Identify a formal power, not just the most senior officeholder.
2. What was known then? Separate a contemporaneous document, evidence of its receipt, later public disclosure and a later outcome. Unknown receipt is not knowledge.
3. Which legally available options are actually documented? A later change of law cannot be applied retroactively merely because it would make a better story.
4. What record would each explanation produce? Request dated criteria, evaluations, minutes, objections, declarations, payment references or subsequent dispositions that distinguish the mechanisms.
5. What would make the favored explanation fail? State this before collecting the next record. When the first formulation already used the outcome, label it retrospective.

## Three rival explanations are a floor

| Explanation | What it must specify | An informative comparison or discriminator |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unproved coordination or conflict mechanism | A particular institutional decision and the missing causal or agreement link; a possible beneficiary alone is insufficient. | Contemporaneous instructions, a documented undisclosed conflict, bid deviations or comparable decisions that differ after a specific intervention. None is assumed to exist. |
| Lawful institutional explanation | The rule, technical constraint, emergency, policy objective, competitive process or judicial direction that could account for the same outcome. | Contemporaneous reasons and evaluation applied consistently to unsuccessful and successful parties. Later justification alone is not the same as a dated decision record. |
| Data, identity or measurement error | The precise join, date, population, legal entity, financial stage or missing denominator that could create the apparent anomaly. | Original row identifiers, entity registrations, period reconciliation and amended/corrected records. A matching label is not an identifier. |

Alternatives must compete over a diagnostic observation. “More documents are needed” is a gap, not a test. Missing public material may reflect publication practice, incomplete acquisition, lawful secrecy or deliberate withholding; absence becomes evidence only with a documented expectation that the record should exist, be accessible and be captured by the search.

Every test records expected results under the proposition and at least one alternative, the current observation, a result of `supports`, `narrows`, `contradicts` or `unresolved`, the precise source and the next record's holder. `supports` applies to that narrow test; `supported-pattern` applies to an observable pattern. Neither means a conspiracy has been established. A formal audit finding, a litigant's assertion, a court's recital and a court's final holding remain different records.

## Baselines, common causes and source independence

Selection into an investigative corpus is strongly non-random. Prominent companies, controversial policies, large contracts, cities with more reporting and institutions with better disclosure attract more records. Their graph degree is not a measured corruption rate. These selected files cannot estimate prevalence across a government or compare administrations.

Before treating an apparent cluster as unusual, define the denominator: all eligible bidders in the same procedure and period; all awards of similar size and sector; comparable concessions; or the full relevant scheme population. Include failed bids, unremarkable cases, adverse decisions against the proposed beneficiary, and comparable state/earlier-administration decisions where authority and rules match. A before/after contrast without comparable eligibility, economic conditions and reporting practices is not a causal estimate.

A policy priority, fiscal year deadline, common supplier market, state procurement system, disaster, court order or reporting campaign can cause several actors to move at once. These are candidate common causes to investigate. Conditioning only on firms already sanctioned, scandals already reported or cases already litigated can itself induce misleading associations. This documentary network is not a fitted causal graph; arrows mean their cited institutional or financial relation, not an identified causal effect.

Repetition is not independence. A ministry release, its wire-service paraphrase and a dozen syndicated reports may be one source family. A judgment repeating a party's claim does not independently verify it. Public financial disclosures, a tender and an audit can provide distinct information while still depending on the same underlying ledger. Record what each source independently observed, its custody, publication date, extraction scope and current disposition.

## Recorded edges and proposed links

Recorded edges retain exact parties, direction, financial or institutional stage, date, citation, response and limitations. Proposed links are questions in a separate, optional layer. They have no amount, require missing and disconfirming evidence, and cannot be traversed as completed cash flows or counted as corroborated relations. Connecting two institutional nodes with a question does not imply that either coordinated wrongdoing.

An appropriation, sanction, award, loan balance, disbursement, receipt, write-off, recovery and estimated loss are different quantities. Do not sum stages or infer onward payment. Shared officeholding, addresses, advisers, donations, ownership groups or timing cannot substitute for a documented transfer. A corporate group is not automatically the legal party to every member's transaction. A lawful donation or meeting may be relevant context while proving no exchange.

When the source supports only an unresolved identity or aggregate group, keep it unresolved. A proposed-link endpoint must still refer to an identified source-supported entity. If the alleged counterparty itself is unknown, retain the issue as a textual next-record question instead of inventing a named participant.

## Retrospective tests and future review

The current questions are retrospective reconstructions made on 14 October 2026. Reading old documents in chronological order is useful, but does not restore an archived ex-ante information set or erase knowledge of the outcome. These cases are not a model evaluation set, and the existing failed model-promotion experiment remains unchanged.

A `nextReview` date schedules document acquisition; it is not a predicted event date. A future forecast would require a separately timestamped and frozen target, allowed information, resolution rule, observation window, comparison model, probabilities produced before outcomes, and proper evaluation on cases not used to select the method. The present page provides no calibrated probabilities, likelihood score for guilt, or claimed prediction accuracy. A future correction must preserve the earlier claim and explain which new record changed the assessment.
