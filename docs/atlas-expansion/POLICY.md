## Three chains

### Bhushan Steel: creditor resolution, acquisition funding and former-owner control

`bhushan-policy-resolution` extends a retained IBBI historical table with new original acquisition and court documents. SBI initiated the CIRP; NCLT approved Tata's plan on 15 May 2018. Tata's 18 May exchange disclosure says its wholly owned Bamnipal Steel acquired 72.65% through **₹158.89 crore equity** and a **₹34,973.69 crore inter-corporate loan**. Bamnipal's **₹16,500 crore external bridge** is a funding source, not another public payout. The lender for that bridge is not assigned here.

The issuer says **₹35,200 crore financial-creditor settlement was being undertaken**, with **₹1,200 crore operational-creditor payment scheduled over 12 months**. Only the **₹100 crore novation consideration** is explicitly attributed to Bamnipal on the directed creditor edge. The broader settlement remains a plan-level observation. The 15 May approval disclosure says the settlement includes debt-novation consideration; no separate addition or subtraction is used to reconcile the amounts.

IBBI's September 2022 row separately gives **₹56,022.06 crore FC claims**, **₹35,571 crore FC-realizable** and **₹14,541 crore liquidation value**. These are not a cash ledger, a SBI-only exposure or a waiver to a named former promoter. Its definition excludes the assumption that every plan-realizable amount was collected.

NCLAT's 7 March 2022 decision rejected the former promoters' challenge to selling their shares at **₹2 each** under the plan. The order records the prior Supreme Court appeal dismissed **as withdrawn** on 22 February 2021. Tata's original filing records Bamnipal/Tata Steel BSL amalgamation effective 11 November 2021. The entities are historical, not asserted as still separately owned in 2026. **Bhushan Steel/Tata Steel BSL is not Bhushan Power & Steel/JSW**; the latter's litigation cannot update the former's status.

Missing endpoint: the complete approved distribution schedule and creditor-wise dated bank receipts; latest retained order for this particular share dispute is March 2022. No broader criminal or tax disposition is inferred.

### Radhikapur West: auction right, development obligation and two different court tracks

`radhikapur-policy-enforcement` connects the final 2020 framework to **Vedanta Limited, bidder 68522**, not the inherited Vedanta/BALCO group aggregate. The auction date is 2 November 2020; the original Delhi High Court judgment records successful declaration on 28 December, CMDPA on 11 January 2021 and vesting on 3 March 2021. The bid was **21% revenue share**, not a measured payment. The same published tranche lists Hindalco/Chakla at 14.25% and JMS/Urtan at 10.5%; different mine economics prevent treating this rate alone as a favour signal.

The 21 July 2025 appropriation order identifies **₹29,23,55,117.68** from a performance guarantee. Vedanta raised boundary, forest-clearance, afforestation and force-majeure arguments; the Ministry contested them. The original 2 December 2025 judgment, paragraphs 19–21, sends disputed facts to the statutory tribunal and **expressly reserves merits**. Its PBG status quo ran for ten days or until approach to the tribunal, whichever earlier, and the guarantee had to remain alive.

There are two tracks:

* **PBG appropriation:** ST 28/2025; the later mirrored judgment records LPA 759/2025 extending protection pending the tribunal stay application. Final stay disposition and bank/state cash entries are not verified.
* **Proposed termination SCN-III:** ST 31/2025; the 10 April 2026 mirrored LPA 83/84 judgment dismisses both sides' appeals and leaves limited protection until that stay application is decided. A show-cause notice is not final termination.

The official 2026 pronouncement list confirms case numbers, parties and date, but **not the reasoning**. Full April 2026 reasoning remains `reported` because only a third-party PDF mirror was recovered. The original December 2025 judgment was recovered from the court in both text and PDF form. No final cancellation, current production, encashed guarantee or paid state royalty is asserted. The FY2025 issuer commissioning plan is retained as an expectation.

### Tuticorin berth 9: prospective tariff autonomy and recurring landlord payments

`tict-policy-concession` follows the 2021 Act and prospective guidelines to **V.O. Chidambaranar Port Authority → Tuticorin International Container Terminal Private Limited**, signed 3 September 2022. PIB estimates **₹434.17 crore concessionaire development**, not a government grant. Government records inauguration and the maiden container vessel on 16 September 2024.

CARE's original 5 September 2024 rationale reports a **₹1,900.44 per TEU royalty**, WPI escalation, minimum throughput and a **₹1 annual licence fee**. Calling it a “₹1 concession” would omit the material royalty and performance obligations. CARE reports the 30-year term beginning 6 April 2023, distinct from execution; its two passages differ by one day on the 2053 end date, so that detail remains unresolved. It also lists **₹482.41 crore long-term and ₹35 crore short-term rated facilities**, not drawn loans. These limits are not added to project cost.

CARE identifies J M Baxi Ports and Logistics Private Limited as the direct parent at the 2024 snapshot, with sponsor support and pending reorganisation. This is a `reported` ownership edge, not a current 2026 shareholder assertion. Full signed concession, audited royalty receipts and final reorganisation filings remain gaps. Nearby PSA Sical berth 7 and its litigation are a separate concession. Kamarajar/Ennore was dropped as an unsuitable scope shortcut; the statute's port list and prospective application matter.

The Indian Ports Association's original public profile explicitly supplies **8.75637793296619 N, 78.1790887243492 E**, with a Barathi Nagar, Tuticorin address. This supports a **public authority premises/campus marker only**, not a precise berth 9 or crane location.

The framework records these as gaps; it does not manufacture current judicial finality. No confidential access, private records, witness/whistleblower contact or company outreach occurred. No open model generated these claims; any parent-level discovery ranking remains separate from evidence admission.

2. Separated PBG/ST 28 from termination-notice/ST 31 and its 2026 appeals; retained the exact time limit of the December 2025 interim order.
3. Kept CARE's royalty/parent assertions `reported`, rating limits distinct from disbursements, and the ₹1 licence alongside royalty and throughput conditions.
