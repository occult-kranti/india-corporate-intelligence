# International defence contracts: bounded public financial paths

Scope: 6 October 2011–6 October 2026, accessed 7 October 2026. This is a curated extension of the existing force register, not a new national procurement census or an estimate of corruption. Namespace `atlas-defence-trade`; raw file `research/raw/atlas-expansion/defence-trade.json`. Three cases, 14 sources, 21 entities, 25 relationships and six records. No operational unit locations, private addresses or site coordinates are added.

## What the three cases establish

| Case | Financial instrument / stage | Verified parties and latest bounded status | Open question |
|---|---|---|---|
| C295, Spain–India | 24 September 2021 signed acquisition; INR 21,935 crore **total project cost** stated in October 2022 | MoD → Airbus Defence and Space S.A., Spain; 16 flyaway / 40 locally manufactured by TASL-led consortium explicitly including TCS; first delivery September 2023, **16 cumulative deliveries at 30 June 2026** | No Tata subcontract receipts, TASL/TCS split, offset settlement or first Indian-built delivery certificate retained |
| BrahMos, India/Russia–Philippines | 31 December 2021 notice accepts USD 374,962,800 price proposal, reported in January; 28 January 2022 signed contract reported as PHP 18.9 billion | DRDO and Russian NPOM are named JV partners; Philippine DND contracts with BrahMos; first delivery reported April 2024, another batch **en route** on 23 April 2025 | No receipt ledger, advance remittance, division of export proceeds or complete three-battery acceptance retained |
| MQ-9B, United States–India | DSCA 1 February 2024 possible-sale notification, estimated USD 3.99 billion; separate acquisition and logistics contracts signed 15 October 2024 | MoD → US Government for 31 aircraft; notification names General Atomics Aeronautical Systems as principal; MoD → General Atomics Global India Private Limited separately for logistics | Final signed price, initial payment and acceptance records absent from retained original ministry confirmation; future delivery schedule remains reported/conditional |

The amounts above cannot be added. C295 project cost does not equal a Tata award or cash receipt. BrahMos dollar award and peso contract descriptions refer to the same acquisition. MQ-9B's notification estimate is not its final price. The original notice calls funding “National Funds”; no US loan, grant or payment is inferred. Manufacturer country does not mean state ownership. A consortium is not automatically an incorporated JV, and similarly branded General Atomics entities are not automatically parent/subsidiary.

## Reuse and identity

The original force corpus was reviewed first. C295 `procurement-industry:c002`, `c055` and `c060` already retain the 2021 acquisition and 2022 project cost; this case adds a connected, carefully named consortium path and June 2026 delivery snapshot without counting a second award. Existing domestic BrahMos awards are separate from the Philippine export. Existing broad MQ-9B import references did not supply this reviewed notification/signature distinction.

Exact bridges use full existing IDs for Ministry of Defence, Indian Air Force, Airbus vendor, TASL, TCS, DRDO and BrahMos. PIB identifies the exact S.A. vendor and Spain; it is not merged with Airbus SE. The named TASL/TCS consortium is a distinct project grouping. The original Ministry of Defence 2022 review (PIB 1884353, 17 December 2022) spells out Defence Research & Development Organisation and DRDO, and names BrahMos Aerospace Private Limited (BAPL) as its JV company signing the Philippine export contract. This exact primary source also anchors the legacy BrahMos entity. It supplies the required documentary identity basis for both canonical bridges; self-reported company history alone does not satisfy that bridge guard. No common-name or address-only match is used. Country context uses ISO codes IN, ES, RU, PH and US; all country/state associations describe procurement or corporate context, not deployment or beneficial ownership.

## Provenance and failed retrievals

`defence-trade-evidence/sha256-manifest.json` maps every admitted source to hashed artifacts. Original HTML/PDF bytes and readable fulltext are retained with URL, retrieval time, final URL, content type, byte length and SHA256 receipts. Public institutional originals are retained directly. Commercial reporting originals/fulltext and the mixed search capture are stored only in `/workspace/research-cache/atlas-defence-trade`: nine files, 1,265,835 bytes, preserving original hashes. Public derivatives retain source URLs and authored summaries without article bodies; `publication-cache-receipt.json` and the manifest’s `externalOriginals` identify each cached capture. Local cache availability is not required for public-build or CI validation. `c295-count-2026-p1.png` was visually checked against the C295 column and India row: 56 orders, 16 deliveries. The “in operation” column is deliberately not translated into readiness, basing or combat capability.

The two PNA article URLs returned HTTP 200 after redirecting to unrelated Facebook content. Receipts explicitly mark these **content failures**; those HTML files do not support the claims. Full text of the indexed original public PNA articles is separately retained in `indexed-primary-pages.json`. The relevant relationships remain `reported`; the signed contract instrument itself has not been opened.

The old DSCA URL was HTTP 403 directly and resolved to a generic 2026 sales index through indexed retrieval. Neither supports the MQ-9B amount. The primary source was recovered instead from the US Government Publishing Office: Federal Register notice `2024-31699`, published 6 January 2025, reproducing Transmittal 24-07 and explicitly dating Congressional notification 1 February 2024. Its PDF SHA256 is `7ff060183862bfc41aafc79a1f3e856047fb6084546251fb1813fbd8a1a4e8b3`. Printed pages 624–625 provide the amount, funding source, quantity and principal-contractor role. January publication is not a second approval or contract.

C295's iframe PIB retrieval failed but the same original press-release ID opened successfully at its normal public URL. Reuters MQ-9B direct retrieval failed; the original Indian MoD confirmation and public broadcaster provide signature evidence. Failed and discovery-only sources are retained for reproducibility but do not become extra admitted evidence. Search-discovery snapshots include post-2024 status discovery; no source proves all three BrahMos batteries accepted, an MQ-9B payment, or a post-June C295 local handover in this corpus.

## Alternatives, dates and limits

These are routine publicly announced arms acquisitions and industrial partnerships, with no asserted corruption or political quid pro quo. The sources describe manufacturing capability, modernization and intergovernmental cooperation as their stated purposes. Corrected official contract schedules, payment ledgers, corporate records or delivery certificates would revise these conclusions. Missing records are gaps in this bounded corpus, not proof of non-payment or concealment.

BrahMos's 1998 formation is historical context outside the window. The company states inception authorised capital of USD 300 million at a 50.5% Indian / 49.5% Russian ratio and a 7 February 2014 indefinite IGA extension. No current shareholding ledger or paid-in capital is established; percentages and capital are not structured as current financial flows. The company source is undated and self-reported, so its retrieval date is not a one-day partnership date.

C295's 2024 factory opening, planned September 2026 local rollout, and June 2026 reported test flight are different from a delivery. The June ledger remains dated June. MQ-9B reporting expects deliveries 51–72 months after signing **and initial payment**, in 2029–2030; this forecast neither confirms payment nor adds any earlier leased aircraft to the 31-aircraft purchase.

Independent oversight review opened the original Federal Register, PIB releases, indexed PNA articles and visually checked the Airbus table. It accepted the financial-stage and identity boundaries. Its corrections changed the C295 title to follow the actual buyer → prime → consortium direction and removed an unsupported publication date from the June Airbus snapshot. The snapshot cutoff remains 30 June 2026; web publication date is unknown, and PDF creation metadata is not substituted for it. Shared adapter/schema, UI and model changes belong to the integration owner.

The final bridge correction retained the JV-history edges at their existing self-reported tier; it did not upgrade inception ownership ratios or any money stage. All seven canonical bridges now pass the documented-primary-source guard. Publication hygiene changed only archive storage and receipts; the raw slice and model corpus input remain byte-for-byte unchanged. A second original PIB vendor release (2010528, 1 March 2024) was also retrieved and hashed as corroborating discovery material, without importing its separate domestic contract amounts into the export case.
