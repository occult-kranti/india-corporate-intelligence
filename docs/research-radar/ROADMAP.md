## Agent roster and dispatch

The executed panel consists of three regional investigators, one data/forecast engineer, one product designer/implementer, one independent source/model challenger and the coordinating lead. These are AI agents, not hired human reporters. They share artifacts but have separate file ownership and source challenges.

Future dispatch is **bounded work packets**, not one unconstrained agent per suspected person. Each packet declares scope, source authority, output schema, stop condition, dependency and independent reviewer:

1. City/jurisdiction scout: source inventory, latest audit/response and coverage gaps.
2. Sector accountant: amounts, stage, fiscal perimeter, cohort and reconciliation.
3. Procurement investigator: stable contract IDs, amendments, competition and delivery evidence.
4. Corporate-identity researcher: legal registration, dated ownership and public beneficial-control evidence.
5. Public-role historian: one exact actor's office/decision/allegation/response/outcome timeline.
6. Policy historian: operative legal changes, effective dates, grandfathering and control tests.
7. International-finance researcher: public lender/foreign supplier/FDI jurisdiction and actual flows.
8. Independent legal/source challenger: disproof, latest proceedings and copied-source detection.
9. Forecast steward: prospective target, deadline, knowledge dates, eventual resolution and missingness.
10. Interface/quality reviewer: source-reader meaning, accessible map/graph, export closure and live verification.

Queue expansion forms city × sector acquisition tasks plus actor-history tasks from **sourced public identities**. The first machine-readable queue has 690 city/sector packets, 10 retained public-role history packets and 69 refresh packets from the existing Union-minister roster dated 11 August 2026: **769 planned work packets**, not 769 cases, people or running agents. Some roster/history tasks concern the same person and are deliberately different work types. The inherited roster's current positions have not been reverified in this release; refresh is the first task. State cabinets, opposition leadership, regulators and judicial/administrative public roles are acquired through each jurisdiction's administration/justice packets before names are admitted. Do not invent minister rosters, connections or allegations to fill a matrix. Prioritize by the public importance of the unresolved decision, available evidence and achievable next document; queue order is not a probability of corruption.

## Probability release gate

1. Define one observable outcome and eligible project/contract population, horizon and information cutoff. Freeze features available at prediction time.
2. Acquire both positive and negative outcomes with adequate follow-up; absence of a news story is not a negative label. Preserve censored/unknown cases and selection bias.
3. Split by time and legal-entity/project group; prevent subsidiaries, duplicate notices, later court outcomes or rewritten historical summaries leaking across splits.
4. Fit a simple empirical/base-rate model first. Compare any more complex model on the same locked holdout using proper scores, uncertainty, calibration/reliability and decision utility.
5. Check geographic/sector coverage, dataset shift and failure cases. Do not set an arbitrary minimum sample and call it proof of validity. Insufficient support means abstention or broad uncertainty.
6. Publish model card, cohort fingerprint, training/holdout periods, feature definitions, limitations and versioned predictions before outcome resolution. Never score a person's likelihood of committing crime from identity, office, allegations or character traits.
7. Monitor revisions and resolve predictions with sources. Retire or recalibrate models when performance or coverage changes; retain failed forecasts.

Suggested future review cadence: weekly source discovery, monthly outcome resolution and quarterly calibration review. These are plans; this release does not install a scheduler or send external requests.

## Retrieval fine-tuning milestone · executed 7 October 2026

A local MiniLM adapter has now been trained and independently tested, superseding any assumption that adaptation is only planned. The retained earlier ONNX discovery run is unchanged. The new experiment uses 64 source summaries, excluding the 23 case summaries to prevent question-copy leakage. It optimized 73,728 LoRA parameters against 70 training queries, selected epoch 1 on 16 development queries, and evaluated 20 frozen test questions from five held-out cases.

**Promotion failed:** the candidate's +0.00866 nDCG@5 gain over untuned FP32 did not meet +0.02, and only one of five cases improved. The saved adapter remains available for explicit local comparison; the original ONNX model stays default. Actual training, hashes, losses and the complete evaluation are preserved in `research/research-radar/fine-tuning/`, with a model card and evaluation report under `docs/research-radar/fine-tuning/`. This is a completed experiment with an unsuccessful promotion result, not an unfinished training job.

The next adaptation wave should acquire more independently reviewed information needs and source passages, measure annotation agreement and token truncation, and include multilingual/OCR and unseen-case tests. Freeze new case/source-family groups before trying alternate losses, rerankers or larger models. The current public benchmark may be used for transparent reproduction and exploratory error analysis, but cannot silently become a fresh held-out test. Compare against lexical retrieval because BM25 was competitive here. Outcome prediction remains separately gated by representative resolved labels, prospective feature timing, censoring and empirical calibration.
