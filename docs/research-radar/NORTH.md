## Round one: investigative priorities

1. **Procurement reviewer:** preserve tender IDs, exact legal-entity identifiers, amendment history and bid timestamps before connecting bidders. Common IP addresses may reflect gateways or filing agents. A large affected tender population is not a loss estimate.
2. **Finance reviewer:** separate approval, commitment, disbursement, consumer credit, outstanding advance and recovery. Patna's ₹62.52 crore vehicle approval remains a prospective watch; it supplies neither an award nor a vendor payment.
3. **Public-service reviewer:** test usable service and operating cost after capital delivery. Dehradun's school equipment/electricity mismatch and positive water SCADA findings are both retained.
4. **Legal reviewer:** keep proceeding-specific outcomes. Joshi's December 2025 PMLA bail, August 2026 ACB bail refusal, and September 2026 bail for Badaya/Dixit are different events. Neither bail nor refusal establishes the allegations.
5. **Forecast reviewer:** define observable targets and the next record holder. No personal propensity score or corruption probability is supplied. Every scenario has alternatives and disproof conditions.

## Findings that change the narrative

**Delhi:** August reporting of CAG findings gives 7,181 tenders sharing bid IPs, a separate common-PAN cohort, and award metadata for only 5,363 of approximately 155,000 tenders. The original report could not be retrieved in this run; two reports share the same audit family. The next useful work is selected-tender reconciliation, not manufacturing connections between unnamed bidders and ministers.

**Lucknow:** the original 27 August 2026 UPERC order, Petition 73SM/2026, takes compliance on record and disposes the proceeding. Page 7 records 93,138 examined accounts, 907 exclusions and 92,231 credits. The original page 6 paragraph 17 table gives **₹32.74 crore**, while page 8 paragraph 22 gives **₹32.75 crore**; SolarQuarter matches the table. Both original amounts remain explicit, with no reconciled exact total asserted. The full OCR also explains that the larger 373,509 Jhatpat figure covers all connections, rather than only excess-charged smart meters. The order also has a one-day discrepancy between the tariff-order date quoted in paragraph 20 (21 November 2025) and paragraph 21 (22 November 2025). A replacement roadmap is still a future deliverable; it is not a pending-refund finding. The author visually checked pages 6–8 and the independent reviewer checked page 8.

**Jaipur:** the 29 September Supreme Court order recites the prosecution's certificate/intermediary theory, including an alleged indirect route to Sumangalam Landmark LLP, but expressly reserves merits. The 2025 PMLA order names several additional corporate mentions and a differently styled Sumangalam LLP. Those labels are **not merged** without an LLPIN/filing check; the text does not prove the pairwise transaction order. Joshi's response about departmental action and non-membership of tender committees remains visible. Searching later status did not establish a subsequent merits decision by the cutoff.

**Punjab:** CAG's original chapter shows recurrence after earlier PAC assurances. It distinguishes the tender database from the documentary sample, and maximum registrations sharing an identifier from proven collusive entities. The inspected chapter extract is partial, so graph relations remain `reported` even though the source is an official audit. The missing current remediation record is an acquisition gap, not proof that an audit-era system weakness is still present.

**Dehradun:** the official executive summary reports non-functional assets, consultant-payment issues and unreturned implementing-agency funds, while recognising working water SCADA and other benefits. Management accepted all 12 recommendations. The audit was tabled in March 2026 but concerns older spending; no newly inspected July–October remediation record was found.

**Patna:** approval estimates reconcile as 30 × ₹88 lakh plus 86 × ₹42 lakh = ₹62.52 crore. Declining recorded riot counts challenge one possible need narrative, but fleet age, coverage and other duties are unmeasured alternatives. No corruption claim or supplier identity is inferred.

## Round two: independent challenge incorporated

The independent reviewer checked the UPERC original scan and Jaipur mirrored judgment. The review first flagged paragraph 22’s ₹32.75 crore. Full OCR and visual inspection then found the original paragraph 17 table’s ₹32.74 crore; the edge now preserves the internal discrepancy and separates disposal from the roadmap obligation. It also challenged whether a system-provider edge implied a signed contract where the audit reported an absent MoU; the Punjab edge now describes system-provision oversight. The reviewer and engine audit required allegation edges to remain alleged, limited-access official extracts to remain reported, and source limitations on every actor event. These changes were made before model-corpus freeze.
