# Welfare and institutions: primary evidence panel

Snapshot: **6 October 2026**. Owned slice: `research/raw/investigation/welfare-research.json`.

The new slice contains **9 primary government documents, 24 entities, 15 relationships, 12 evidence records and 11 district localities**. It adds delivery and accounting evidence to the earlier corpus. It does not claim complete scheme, beneficiary, district or village coverage.

## Round 1: panel decisions

The existing welfare corpus contains extensive scheme chronology, fiscal discussion and political attribution. The NGO corpus already documents FCRA enforcement, national grant channels and reported nutrition disputes. Its own gaps identify the missing primary documents: final nutrition audits, recipient-level grant tables and delivery controls. Education and water/public-works slices already contain PMJAY claims, Karnataka school reimbursements, JJM implementation and procurement findings. Those existing records should remain available through their original namespaces rather than be duplicated here.

The panel prioritised three questions:

1. **Accounting:** Was money sanctioned, released, paid to a bank account, returned, spent, estimated as a potential loss, or recovered? These stages are separate fields and cannot be summed indiscriminately.
2. **Delivery:** Did eligible people receive a service, and did the database’s status correspond to the bank credit or physical service? Inclusion errors and exclusion errors both matter.
3. **Geography and identity:** What is the finest place actually established by the document? A constituency does not automatically equal a district; a ministry address does not locate its programme; a beneficiary record does not establish criminal intent.

The graph links public institutions to their named programmes and records transfers or a dated professional role. It does not connect private beneficiaries to alleged wrongdoing, infer political control from a grant, or join people by surname. B. L. Verma and Savitri Thakur appear only in the capacities and on the dates printed in their Parliament answers.

## Findings and controls

- **NSAP, national:** CAG found ₹2.83 crore of IEC funds used for other Ministry schemes, with payment before work confirmation. The December 2022 reply says the matter was taken up with the IEC division. The source does not establish partisan publicity, private gain or a completed recovery.
- **NSAP, Chhattisgarh:** Table 6.2 identifies 61,913 cases and ₹3,917 lakh of short-payment in selected districts because pension did not begin at its sanctioned effective date. The districts are not named in that table; only state geography is attached. The Ministry referred observations to states/UTs.
- **MP THR:** ₹122.99 crore is supply value comprising ₹100.90 crore paid and ₹22.09 crore pending. CAG’s ₹110.78 crore likely avoidable excess is a separate estimate over that activity, not an additional payment. Government defended Census projections/MIS and claimed a survey; CAG found no reliable supporting survey evidence. The final audit now supersedes reliance on the 2022 draft press accounts in the earlier NGO corpus.
- **Odisha:** Six sampled districts show 1,98,103 missing-coverage entries over 2018–23; distinct people across all years are not established. The 13 of 121 interview responses about Aadhaar, enrolment and migration cannot explain every gap. Government says it is pursuing full enrolment and coverage.
- **Odisha feeding days:** 3,300 is a count of missing centre-days in 45 of 60 inspected AWCs. Government says COVID and heat interruptions were covered by dry rations; CAG could not verify replacement delivery because records were unavailable. Missing documentation alone does not prove no food was delivered.
- **Tamil Nadu PMAY-G:** 1,547 identified suspect sanctions equal 981 sampled-block cases plus 566 found in a check of 595 of 5,949 similar non-sampled-block records. The remaining similar records are not presumed proven ineligible. ₹27.52 crore is CAG’s estimated loss associated with these sanctions; recovery is not established. Government promised verification and action for falsification.
- **PM-SVANidhi:** 1.15 crore loan disbursements to 76.95 lakh vendors are compatible with successive loan tranches. ₹18,475.06 crore is loan principal, not a welfare-grant bill or net public fiscal cost. The answer supplies repayment schedules but not the requested numerical repayment rate.
- **DDRS grants:** Voluntary Organisation for Rural Improvement is named as supporting a hearing/visual-disability school in Keonjhar Lok Sabha constituency. The source does not resolve a campus or district location. Dahod is separately identified as a district. A FY2025–26 dash does not prove school closure, zero services, fraud or any particular cause of a release change.
- **UP PMMVY:** The source explicitly explains NIL/zero fresh central releases through adequate SNA balances. A zero release is neither proof of no funds nor proof that eligible mothers were paid on time.
- **Kerala scholarships:** ₹30.24 lakh/2,360 pre-matric transactions and ₹43.14 lakh/7,438 post-matric transactions were rejected and returned to government in different periods. Transactions are not unique students; returned money is not an established theft amount. June 2023 government replies cite alerts; the June 2022 data snapshot cannot establish present arrears.
- **Jharkhand scholarships:** CAG calls ₹40.31 lakh granted through 410 applications associated with 205 student records doubtful because UID validation was missing. Its ₹38.31 lakh within-state and ₹2 lakh outside-state components are already included. The report records that the state reply was awaited after October 2022 referral; current recovery or adjudication is unknown.

## Geographic contract

The 11 mapped localities are districts: Balangir, Ganjam, Jharsuguda, Mayurbhanj, Puri and Rayagada (Odisha); Kollam, Ernakulam, Kozhikode and Wayanad (Kerala); Dahod (Gujarat). These are document coverage, not interpolated village service estimates. Puri’s zero gap in Table 2.5 is retained as a control and does not imply universal service quality.

Keonjhar remains Odisha-level **constituency association**, with the exact constituency wording in its note. The Jharkhand source covers a state-administered scheme that also supports some students studying outside the state; their institution locations remain unknown. No coordinates are invented.

## Archives and reproducibility

Artifacts are under `research/raw/investigation/evidence/welfare/`. `original-fetch.json` preserves each original retrieval result; `sha256-manifest.json` records content hashes, sizes and paths. Original Parliament PDFs and their `pdftotext -layout` extractions are retained. CAG extracts include the canonical official URL and exact audit sections, including the government's response. Search receipts are discovery records, not substitutes for the opened text.

An Assam-targeted search surfaced a **Tamil Nadu** report. Its opened title page and official catalogue establish Tamil Nadu; archive files were renamed `tn-pmay-audit-*`. The initial search and original-fetch receipt deliberately retain the discovery history. No Assam geography was inferred.

The long MP extract reaches its requested 500,000-character cap. The decisive THR section and reply are present; unretained appendices are not represented as fully reviewed. Smaller first-pass extracts are retained alongside the fuller NSAP, MP and Kerala reads.

Initial self-check verified source, entity, relationship, record and locality references, explicit geography, staged amounts and date interpretation. Independent review by the finance/roads researcher passed the four requested checks: MP amount overlap, Tamil Nadu sample arithmetic, Odisha cumulative entries and constituency-versus-district scope. The reviewer re-read the retained official passages and original Parliament PDF; no substantive corrections were requested. Platform validation and rendered interaction review remain separate tasks; this research ledger does not claim browser verification.

The platform validator caught legacy state codes `CT` and `OR`; the raw slice and matching localities now use the platform’s current `CG` and `OD` codes. This is a code normalisation, with no change to claimed geography.

All 38 retained artifact sizes and SHA-256 hashes were recomputed and matched the manifest. In reciprocal panel review, this researcher independently read the original PM CARES FY2023–24 and FY2024–25 scanned statements and re-added the fresh accounts to the rupee. Receipts excluding opening balances, payments and closing balances matched. The FY2022–23 comparative reduces receipts and payments by the same ₹1,51,23,111 contribution-refund reclassification, leaving closing cash unchanged. Stale prose about only four published accounts and absent FY2023–24 statements was flagged to the finance owner for correction; account numbers themselves required no correction.

Follow-up research should obtain final audit action-taken statements, beneficiary-bank reconciliation, current NGO grant utilisation and exact institution locations. The present graph supplies documented leads and testable questions, not a comprehensive corruption map.
