# Protest evidence supplement

As of 14 October 2026. Selected source checks, not a global census.

## What changed

The inherited Protest Atlas snapshot contains 84 episodes in 81 countries/territories, checked on 2 October. Its underlying articles were not all reread. This supplement adds 22 opened sources and 13 records: nine candidate new episode records, two October updates to existing episodes, and two historical accountability contexts. Belgium, Ireland and the Philippines add represented countries. Do not count linked updates and historical contexts as new episodes.

A country ledger retains all 249 repository country/territory codes. Its 123 city rows combine 101 reference cities with explicit source-linked baseline and supplementary cities. A point on the reference map does not establish a protest. The global city and population denominators remain unknown. No row means unreviewed, never no protest.

## Institution and case paths

Sixteen typed edges connect episodes to public targets, institutions and proceedings. The strongest acquired primary record is the Supreme Court of India's 9 October order in M A Baby v Union of India, Diary 63560/2026, with 63544/2026. It concerns restrictions on public transportation and requires compliance reporting. It does not adjudicate electoral wrongdoing. Counsel explicitly said they did not represent the proposed protesters or organizers. The order does not itself name the CEC movement: a separate context node and an attributed legal-reporting bridge prevent treating that association as a direct judicial finding.

The separate reported challenge to Election Commission decision-making remains an unresolved case-identifier acquisition job. Reporting says interim suspension of the commissioner was declined and responses sought. A refusal of interim relief is neither exoneration nor a finding of misconduct. The Election Commission's reported account of unanimous final decisions sits alongside the protest allegations.

Other edges identify government budget, environmental-policy and collective-bargaining targets. No payment or funding connection was established. Common public organizational participation is narrower: reporting names Greenpeace Africa in both Nairobi and Cape Town episodes. It does not establish common financing or central direction.

## Three rounds and rejected joins

1. Source collection retained provenance, event/report dates, article versus caption access and source families.
2. Candidate connections were tested against independent local grievances and selection bias. Public institutional paths survived; hidden-financier speculation did not.
3. Falsification removed publication-as-event-date errors, retained opposing accounts, distinguished allegations from findings and prevented duplication.

Pune hostel-protest timing remains unknown; the overview's publication date is not an event date. Albania's new caption-supported student episode is not automatically joined to earlier coastal-development rallies. A Kenya alternative source had inconsistent date metadata and failed to open, so it was not counted as corroboration. A union index mixed 2025 item dates with current page metadata, so it was not used to invent a 2026 event.

The Bangladesh entry reflects an OHCHR reasonable-grounds assessment, not a criminal conviction. The Nepal entry is a UN allegations communication, not equivalent to that fact-finding assessment. Published responses and later legal outcomes still require records. Ordinary participant identities, future tactical locations, private groups and speculative death attribution are excluded.
