The inquiry's responsibility finding concerns the State's failure to protect and institutional impunity. It expressly does not establish that the State itself participated in the assassination. The original Maltese report and a separately identified courtesy translation were opened in the web reader; the latter is not an official English judgment. [M4–M5, translation pp399–401]

These identifiers resolve the named companies for this historical source; they are not fresh registry extracts or proof of current ownership. The EUR450m loan facility is financing, not a public loss; the guarantee was not called. [M6]

PACE recorded FIAU-reported receipts into 17 Black and separately described planned Panama-company payments. Underlying bank statements were not acquired. Actual receipt, intended onward payment, beneficial-ownership attribution, and criminal motive must remain separate. No money path from the audited energy project to the killing is established by this packet. [M7; C07–C08]

## Slovakia financial-context limit

Its estimate exceeds EUR1m of possible overpayments, not proven theft or recovered money. The companies are unnamed. This is a useful control-system comparator, but no identity bridge to the murder defendants is established. Do not display it as a Kuciak murder-financing path. [S4; C14]

## Competing explanations and discriminating records
1. Criminal commissioning by an accused individual: requires admissible evidence and operative merits findings, not business links. Acquittal or a retrial without final conviction weakens any asserted guilt conclusion. Next record: Malta appeal docket/disposal and Slovak remand judgment plus current trial docket.
2. Systemic institutional failure without State participation in murder: directly distinguished in the public inquiry. Next record: implementation measures and institutional decisions, assessed independently of defendants' criminal verdicts.
3. Ordinary project finance plus administrative deficiencies: compatible with a loan facility, uncalled guarantee and historical shareholdings. A money-laundering or kickback claim requires transaction-level evidence, account beneficiaries, dates and adjudication; none can be inferred from consortium topology.
4. Financial-source selection or mistaken identity: especially likely when unnamed beneficiaries are matched by topic or geography. Registry identifiers and exact OLAF case records would discriminate; absent them the proposed join stays rejected.
