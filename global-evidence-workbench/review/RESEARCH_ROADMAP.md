# Global evidence workbench: review and acquisition roadmap

Review opened: 2026-10-14 UTC. This is a review plan, not a claim that every gate has passed.

## Research boundary

The workbench connects four research domains only through explicitly supported claims: protest events; India public finance and power networks; organizational ownership, procurement and donations; and public Epstein-related records and lawfully published leaks. Coverage is a sampled research inventory unless a defined denominator establishes otherwise. A geographic map is not a measure of global incidence. A person appearing in a record is not evidence of wrongdoing.

## Round 1: source baseline and coverage

Dependency: actual source files and acquisition manifests from each branch.

1. Freeze a source inventory with publisher, canonical URL, title, publication date, retrieval timestamp, access depth, jurisdiction, covered period, access restrictions and source family. Unknown values stay unknown. Distinguish search result, index page, full text, downloaded original, OCR and supplied summary.
2. Establish each domain's unit and eligible population before comparing examples. For protests: country × date × event definition, duplicate handling, languages searched and missing regions. For procurement: tender/bidder/award universe, unsuccessful bids and reporting exemptions. For court records: docket/document/version and procedural status.
3. Require an exact locator and bounded support statement for every consequential claim. Separate event date, filing date, publication date, retrieval date and relationship validity interval. Preserve partial precision.
4. Record disagreement, corrections, publication responses and dependence. Multiple outlets copying one filing count as one source family.

Pass: all displayable consequential claims have usable source and locator, appropriate status and dates; coverage limitations are shown alongside views. Missing originals are explicitly attributed rather than represented as read. Stop a batch after the declared official sources and query variants are exhausted or a documented access barrier is reached; that is not proof the record does not exist.

## Round 2: multihop candidate claims

Dependency: Round 1 source classification plus stable, reversible entity IDs.

1. Write the precise candidate claim: actors, action, jurisdiction, period and mechanism. Draw each required edge separately with direction, predicate, evidence status and valid interval.
2. Resolve organizations using jurisdiction-scoped registration IDs where possible. Retain rejected and unresolved pairs. A same-name person or company is not a match; shared service addresses are weak clues. Review entire clusters for contradictions.
3. Produce a path certificate. Each hop must identify its source, locator, support limit and time overlap. Ownership, director roles, contractual awards and actual payments are different predicates. Missing payment records remain missing bridges.
4. Compare lawful, misconduct and measurement explanations. Identify the next record that discriminates them rather than adding more repetitions of the same allegation.

Pass: no inferred transaction is drawn as documented, no source mention becomes a culpability edge, and no time-invalid path is used as an explanation. Failed candidates remain in the rejected-join ledger with reasons.

## Round 3: falsification, evidence and code QA

Dependency: frozen Round 2 claims, source packet and runnable application.

1. Reconstruct selected claims from raw records, without treating the initial interpretation as authoritative. Reviews by agents using the same source are independent checks, not independent evidence. This coordinating review sees branch descriptions and is not blind.
2. Challenge each mechanism with a contrary observation and an explicit stopping criterion. Absence can support a claim only if the record should exist, be retained and be discoverable in the inspected corpus.
3. Run schema, referential integrity, duplicate, date-precision, amount-stage, provenance and graph-path tests. Check imports for accidental identity merging and privacy leakage.
4. Build the application and test source links, timeline boundary inclusion, unknown dates, empty filters, keyboard use, legend clarity and whether geographic positions represent observed events or illustrative centroids. Test adversarial evidence cases, including namesakes and later ownership.
5. Freeze a delivery manifest and hashes after branches stop writing. Exclude caches, node_modules, credentials, private paths and unnecessary personal data. Publish no external site or GitHub changes without authorization.

Pass: critical failures fixed or blocked from rendering; limitations visible; exact commands/results recorded. A passing build alone does not validate evidence or visual usability.

## Descriptive game-theory hypothesis register

These are analytic assumptions to test, not findings of coordination. No numerical payoff or equilibrium is estimated without data.

| Question | Assumed incentives / information | Ordinary explanation | Hypothetical misconduct explanation | Measurement explanation | Discriminating observations |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Why might donor firms win awards? | Firms seek contracts; agencies seek eligible capacity; observers see partial records | Larger eligible suppliers both donate and win | Improper influence changes evaluation | Winner-only and publicity-biased sample | Bid eligibility, losing bids, evaluation scores, timing and comparable non-donors; size/sector differences can weaken misconduct inference |
| Why do protest tactics or dates resemble one another? | Participants observe public examples and respond to shared pressures | Public diffusion or common shocks | Specific undisclosed coordination | Repeated syndicated reports / duplicated events | Direct dated organizational evidence, communication attribution and independent event records; shared dates alone are insufficient |
| Why do people appear together in public records? | Social/professional proximity creates documents; record availability is uneven | Ordinary association or incidental mention | Specific alleged conduct | Entity confusion or selective document release | A source recording the specific act, reliable identity and dates; presence in contact/flight records alone cannot decide conduct |
| Why are disclosures missing or delayed? | Institutions face workload, privacy and legal constraints | Retention, access exemptions or processing delay | Intentional suppression of a required record | Wrong custodian/date/query | Retention schedule, legal obligation, release history and custodian response; non-access alone cannot identify intent |

## Rejected inference rules (not accusations about named entities)

- Same name → same identity: reject without independent disambiguation.
- Shared address/registered agent → common control: reject; seek dated ownership/control record.
- Director → owner → decision-maker: reject predicate substitution.
- Ownership chain → payment chain: reject without transactions for each hop.
- Later role/ownership → earlier award explanation: reject temporal reversal.
- Court allegation → judicial finding: reject procedural conflation.
- Flight/contact/leak mention → criminal conduct: reject guilt by association.
- Protest coincidence → covert coordination: reject absent mechanism-specific evidence.
- Missing document → concealment: reject absent expectation/retention/discoverability tests.
- Several articles from one source → independent confirmations: reject source-family inflation.
- Connected graph or centrality → intent: reject; topology does not identify causal mechanisms.

## Completion and continuation rules

Deliver a bounded snapshot when the three review rounds have results, unresolved gates are explicit, and the bundle is reproducible. Continue useful read-only acquisition within scope when a specific missing record can resolve a material uncertainty. Pause for authority before account access beyond existing authorization, external contact, formal requests, paid data, publishing or sharing. Never describe a sampled workbench as exhaustive global coverage, an unavailable record as disproven, or a research hypothesis as an established hidden network.

## Added scope: document changes and protest targets

A document-change row must record source URL/document ID, custodian, observation time, observed HTTP/result state, earlier observation/snapshot if actually available, before/after hashes if bytes were acquired, and access-depth. Classify separately: unavailable during check, HTTP 404/403, moved with verified redirect or replacement, withdrawn with official notice, redacted/version changed, or unresolved. A single failed request cannot establish removal; a before/after difference cannot by itself establish motive. Preserve plausible maintenance, legal/privacy, versioning, retention and measurement explanations. Alleged concealment requires attribution and remains distinct from observed availability. No bypass of access restrictions, private leaked victim data or hidden caches.

For each protest-target edge, record the source's precise predicate (demand directed to, opposition to policy, alleged conduct by, employer bargaining counterpart, or institutional response). Verify target identity and date. A target of criticism is not automatically a protest funder, controlling actor, or adjudicated wrongdoer. Any join from a target to an ownership, donation or procurement graph requires its own independently supported identity, predicate and valid dates. Keep opposition claims and published institutional responses adjacent. Stop the current join when the bridge is unsupported, record the rejected candidate, and name the specific next record needed.
